Safran is a free desktop app for macOS, Windows and Linux that keeps your texts on your own computer. Pick the books and languages you want from the [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org) library once, and read, search and annotate them afterwards without an internet connection. The **textual apparatus** compares the Masoretic text with seven witnesses: the Aleppo text, the Samaritan Pentateuch, the Qumran scrolls, the Septuagint, Field's Hexapla, the Peshitta and Kennicott's manuscripts. The **whole Tanakh word by word in German** (interlinear) and a **word card** show how Hebrew words are translated. On top of that, Safran ships **more than 50 German works** (translations, commentaries and reference works) that are hard to find anywhere else, among them Louis Ginzberg's *Legenden der Juden* from his German manuscript, plus **eleven dictionaries and reference works** with more than 300,000 entries. Safran is an independent project and not affiliated with Sefaria.

**New in 2.8.0:** the Peshitta for all 39 books (reading text, interlinear and the Syr witness), Field's Hexapla, the Aleppo comparison (MA), classified LXX and Peshitta readings, the reading line and *Similar chapters* – see the [release notes](docs/releases/2.8.0.md) and the [overview video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_htLzC7Q3Ug).

## Install

Download the file for your system from the [latest release](https://github.com/mitkin89/Safran/releases/latest). Safran has no paid developer certificate, so the first start needs one extra step.

- **macOS** – open the `.dmg` and drag **Safran** into **Applications**.
  > ⚠️ **"Safran is damaged and can't be opened"?** The app is not broken; macOS says this about apps without a paid Apple certificate. Open the `.dmg` again and right-click **Safran freischalten.command** → *Open* → *Open* (the file name is German for "unlock Safran"). Or run in the Terminal: `xattr -cr /Applications/Safran.app`. For *"the developer cannot be verified"*: *System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway*.
- **Windows** – run the installer (or the portable `.exe`). If SmartScreen shows *"Windows protected your PC"*: **More info** → **Run anyway**.
- **Linux** – make the `.AppImage` executable once (`chmod +x Safran-*.AppImage`) and start it, or install the `.deb` (`sudo apt install ./safran_*.deb`).

More on this in the [FAQ](#faq).

### First steps

The interface comes in German, English and Hebrew. It starts in German or English, depending on your system language, and can be changed at any time under ⋯ → *Language* (*Sprache* in the German interface). This page uses the English names; the screenshots show the German interface.

1. **Choose your texts.** Open ⋯ → *Download…* (*Herunterladen…*), load the catalog once (about 22 MB), tick categories or single books, and pick the languages (Hebrew, English, German). The bundled German texts and the dictionaries are there before you download anything.
2. **Read.** Pick a book, type a passage into the jump field (`2:5`, `Daf 3a`, `gen. 1:1`), and press Cmd+K (Ctrl+K on Windows and Linux) for the commentaries. A new tab shows the weekly Torah portion and the Daf Yomi; a click opens them.
3. **Go deeper.** In a book of the Tanakh, press *Interlinear* for the German under every Hebrew word. Click a Hebrew word for its grammar and its *Word card* (*Wortkarte*), double-click any word to look it up in the dictionaries, and press Cmd+I (Ctrl+I) for *Everything on this passage* (*Alles zu dieser Stelle*).

## Textual apparatus

The **≠ Apparatus** pane compares the Masoretic text (Westminster Leningrad Codex, OSHB) verse by verse with the manuscripts and ancient versions. It works for every passage of the Tanakh, needs no reading column of the witness, and can show a single verse or the whole chapter.

| Siglum | Witness | Scope | Source and license |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **MA** | Aleppo text against the Leningrad Codex: consonants, plene/defective, vowels, word division; accents on request | 39 books; * where the codex itself is lost | *Miqra according to the Masorah* (he.wikisource, CACCHT), CC BY-SA 4.0 |
| **SMR** | Samaritan Pentateuch after Tal–Florentin (2024) with the original notes | Torah | DT-UCPH / CACCHT, CC BY-NC 4.0 |
| **DSS** | biblical Qumran scrolls, every word with its siglum and reconstruction marks | 36 books, 262 sigla | ETCBC / Abegg, CC BY-NC 4.0 |
| **LXX** | Septuagint: Tov/Kraft's CATSS notes and retroversions, plus classified readings (different reading, different interpretation, plus, minus, harmonisation, doublet, name, number, translation technique …) | 38 books (Esther: reading text after Rahlfs) | CATSS, CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 |
| **Hex** | Hexapla after Field (1875): the LXX with ※ and ÷, Aquila, Symmachus, Theodotion, Quinta …, as a synopsis with one row per translator; sigla explained on hover, Field's footnotes in place (English, Latin folded), morphology and word cards | 39 books | Field: public domain; morphology Perseus Morpheus (CC BY-SA 3.0 US), meanings LSJ (CC BY-SA) |
| **Syr** | Peshitta: pluses and minuses from Safran's own word alignment, and Safran's own classified readings (not the apparatus of the printed Leiden edition) | 39 books | ETCBC Peshitta, CC BY-NC 4.0 |
| **Ken** | Kennicott (1776–1780): readings of the medieval manuscripts with catalogue cards | 39 books | Digital Kennicott, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 |

Field's Hexapla is also available as an open dataset: [field-hexapla](https://github.com/mitkin89/field-hexapla), DOI [10.5281/zenodo.24464811](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24464811). The classified LXX and Peshitta readings and the MT–Peshitta word alignment likewise: [lxx-peshitta-apparatus](https://github.com/mitkin89/lxx-peshitta-apparatus), DOI [10.5281/zenodo.24465785](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24465785) (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).

For Mishnah Berakhot the pane compares the manuscripts Kaufmann, Parma and Cambridge.

- **Reading line:** above every MT word group a short line sums up the witnesses – which read the same, which differ only in spelling, and which have a plus or a minus, e.g. in Genesis 2:2, where SMR, LXX and Syr read “sixth day” for “seventh”.
- **Context and grammar:** hover a witness label (SMR, 4Q7, LXX, Ken, Syr) for the verse in that witness with the word marked; click a word for its morphology and word card (for the Peshitta where ETCBC morphology exists).
- **Labels:** every Hexapla row says how it relates to the LXX row – *as LXX*, *different rendering*, *follows the MT*, *transliteration*, and, after review, *different Vorlage* or *different interpretation* with the presupposed Hebrew.
- **Assessments:** the classification of LXX, Peshitta and Hexapla readings and the inferred Hebrew Vorlagen were made with Claude (2026) and reviewed. They are an aid, not a critical edition; uncertain cases are marked, and every entry keeps its source.

New in 2.8.0: the Peshitta (reading text, interlinear and the Syr witness), Field's Hexapla, the Aleppo comparison (MA), the classified LXX readings and the reading line – see the [release notes](docs/releases/2.8.0.md).

- **Smart lookup:** vowel points and final-letter forms are ignored, complete headwords are matched by default. Use `*` for word beginnings (for example `שיח*`); the “Remove first letter” button lets you shorten a query explicitly. Suggestions appear while typing.
- **Full text:** tick *also in text* (*auch im Text*) to search inside the articles, not only the headwords. Hits are highlighted. Phrases in quotation marks and the wildcards `*` and `?` work too.
- **From the text and back:** a word looked up from the text shows where it came from (*from Genesis 25:8*); a click returns to the verse.
- **Choose dictionaries:** the 📚 button selects which dictionaries are searched. The choice is remembered.
- **Headword list:** *☰ Stichwörter* (headwords) opens the complete, filterable headword list of a dictionary.
- **Linked sources:** a citation such as *Rosh Hashanah 18b* opens the passage in the reader, if the text is in your library.
- **Bring your own:** add `.dsl` / `.dsl.dz` dictionaries (ABBYY Lingvo format) via the 📚 menu → *Add DSL file …* (*DSL-Datei hinzufügen …*). Citations marked `{{ref:…}}` become clickable in Safran.

## German texts that are not on Sefaria

Safran bundles more than 50 German works in `bundled/` (over 400 book files). They are imported on first start and appear under *Own texts* (*Eigene Texte*) and as extra versions and commentators next to the Sefaria texts. Among them: Zunz, Philippson and Herxheimer for the whole Tanakh, Mendelssohn, Goldschmidt and Hirsch, the Kautzsch Apocrypha, Wünsche's Midrash translations, 19th-century Talmud translations, the Schulchan Aruch, Josephus, Graetz and Ginzberg.

When an app update includes a different version of an existing text, Safran asks before replacing it. Books are grouped by translation, including older own-text imports. You can keep the existing version, accept the new one, or choose “Use all new versions” to update every offered text without further prompts. Before replacement, the old file is backed up under `backups/text-updates` in the library folder. Keeping a version is remembered for that offered version; a later change prompts again.

## FAQ

<details>
<summary><b>First start on macOS, Windows and Linux: "damaged", "unidentified developer", SmartScreen, AppImage</b></summary>

The app has no paid developer certificate (Apple's and Microsoft's certificates cost money), so the first start needs one extra step. The app is not broken. On macOS it is signed locally (ad hoc).

**macOS** – if you see *"Safran is damaged and can't be opened"* or *"the developer cannot be verified"*:

1. Drag **Safran** into the **Applications** folder.
2. Easiest: open the `.dmg` again and right-click **Safran freischalten.command** → *Open* → *Open*. Or run in the Terminal: `xattr -cr /Applications/Safran.app`
3. For *"developer cannot be verified"*: *System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway* (scroll down), or right-click the app → *Open* → *Open*.

**Windows** – SmartScreen may show *"Windows protected your PC"*: click **More info** → **Run anyway**.

**Linux** – the `.AppImage` needs to be made executable once (`chmod +x Safran-*.AppImage`, or in the file manager: *Properties → Permissions → Allow executing*). The `.deb` installs normally (`sudo apt install ./safran_*.deb`).
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>How much disk space do I need?</b></summary>

Only what you choose. Rough figures: all merged Hebrew texts ≈ 2.8 GB, English ≈ 0.5 GB, German translations from Sefaria ≈ 0.13 GB. All JSON texts including every single version ≈ 7 GB. Sefaria's link data ≈ 680 MB (optional). Hebrew audio ≈ 3 GB in total, downloaded per book (optional). The storage location can be moved, for example to an external drive.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Does it really work offline?</b></summary>

Yes. You need a connection to download texts, links and audio, and for the optional update check. Everything else, including search, dictionaries, maps and the bundled German texts, works without internet.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>What about privacy?</b></summary>

Safran has no account, no tracking and no server of its own. It downloads from the public Sefaria export and from GitHub releases, only what you ask for. Your notes, bookmarks, imported texts and dictionaries stay on your computer. Safran never uploads anything to Sefaria.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>How is Safran related to Sefaria?</b></summary>

Safran is an independent, open-source project and not affiliated with Sefaria. The Sefaria texts are not part of the app; they are downloaded from the public [Sefaria export](https://github.com/Sefaria/Sefaria-Export) and keep their own licenses. Safran adds its own German texts, dictionaries and tools on top. The Tools menu can also prepare and check CSV files for a Sefaria upload.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Which versions of macOS, Windows and Linux?</b></summary>

Safran is an Electron app; installers for macOS (`.dmg`), Windows (installer and portable `.exe`) and Linux (`.AppImage` and `.deb`) are attached to each [release](https://github.com/mitkin89/Safran/releases). For Hebrew text, install the free **SBL Hebrew** font from the Society of Biblical Literature; Safran uses it automatically.
</details>

## Editorial notes

Philippson's imported text has been corrected for misplaced verses, duplicated blocks, clear OCR errors and word breaks. Restored verse endings are marked in brackets; uncertain readings and differing translation choices remain documented in the [review and correction lists](tools/interlinear/philippson/PRUEFLISTE-TANACH.md). Editorial section headings have been removed from the verse text.

Mendelssohn's German Pentateuch (Dessau 1815, Latin script) is a separate interlinear source from the Taitsch and Biur. All five books / 5,846 verse fields are aligned against Westminster, including joint Ketiv/Qere segments. Confirmed misplaced sentence continuations have been returned to their verses and realigned; the nonbiblical printing appendix is preserved separately. Mendelssohn's translation (Dessau 1815 and the Taitsch of 1795), the Biur and this word alignment are also published as an open dataset: [mendelssohn-pentateuch](https://github.com/mitkin89/mendelssohn-pentateuch), DOI [10.5281/zenodo.24466528](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24466528). Existing OCR candidates and possible omissions remain unchanged in the [review and correction lists](tools/interlinear/mendelssohn/PRUEFLISTE-PENTATEUCH.md).

Berakhot 2a–10b also has a separate **William Davidson Edition – Safran-Morphologie**: 10,786 Hebrew/Aramaic word annotations with lemma, morphology, explicit alternatives and source notes. Click a word for its analysis or the forms actually attested in the covered passage. Grammar and lemma searches include this edition; unresolved features remain marked. The original wording and vocalization are preserved. See the [method and review report](tools/bavli/PRUEFBERICHT.md).

Berakhot 2a–10b also offers an experimental **Wortzuordnung · Test** viewer for Goldschmidt and Pinner. Both versions of 2a–3b are manually aligned, with further checked passages and explicitly separate automatic/open proposals. [Review status](tools/bavli/interlinear/README.md).

**Samaritan Pentateuch:** in Genesis to Deuteronomy, choose “Samaritanischer Pentateuch (Tal–Florentin)” and the English “The Samaritan Pentateuch, Florentin and Tal (2024)” under **Versions**. The 1,074 Tal readings from the DT-UCPH data are applied to the Hebrew main text. **Red** marks the MT differences of the printed edition in both languages and in the notes. The 3,459 footnotes and 790 endnotes keep their original reference and printed page. Unchanged words still offer the CACCHT word card with lexeme, morphology and transliteration; new or changed forms get no unchecked analysis. Both versions follow Tal's verse numbering. **CC BY-NC 4.0**; [sources and build](tools/samaritan/README.md).
