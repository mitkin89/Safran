## Sefaria source and licenses

All texts, the catalog and the link data come from the public **Sefaria export** ([Sefaria/Sefaria-Export](https://github.com/Sefaria/Sefaria-Export), files hosted in Google Cloud Storage). Sefaria itself is an open-source project: <https://github.com/Sefaria/Sefaria-Project> · <https://www.sefaria.org>.

The texts are **not** covered by this project's license. Each text version has its own license (mostly Creative Commons, some public domain, some restricted); it is listed in the version information on Sefaria and in the exported files. If you reuse or redistribute texts, please follow those licenses and credit Sefaria and the original sources. Texts you import yourself stay on your computer.

The Bible cross-references in `xref.json` (Tanakh only, shown among the Sefaria links) come from [OpenBible.info](https://www.openbible.info/labs/cross-references/) (mostly the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge), licensed CC BY 4.0. Converter: `tools/xref/`.

The Josephus links (Antiquities, Jewish War ↔ Bible, Maccabees and each other) come from the parallel-passage index of B. Niese's edition (Berlin 1885–1895, public domain); Niese's § numbers are mapped to Whiston's chapters and sections (Sefaria's structure) with the Perseus texts ([PerseusDL/canonical-greekLit](https://github.com/PerseusDL/canonical-greekLit), CC BY-SA 4.0). Converter: `tools/josephus/`. Winter/Wünsche, *Mechiltha* (Leipzig 1909): text transcribed from the page images on [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/MechilthaEinTannaitischerMidraschZuExodusVonAugustWnsche) (public domain) with Claude Opus and aligned sentence by sentence to the Sefaria segments of *Mekhilta DeRabbi Yishmael*. Graetz, *Geschichte der Juden*: text from [zeno.org](http://www.zeno.org/Geschichte/M/Graetz,+Heinrich/Geschichte+der+Juden) (public domain); the reversed Hebrew was turned around by `tools/graetz/build.py`. The links from Safran's own texts (`tools/ownlinks/`) are taken from the passage references in those texts. The Mishnah ↔ Talmud table (`bundled/mishnah_talmud.json`) is derived from Sefaria's links of the type *mishnah in talmud* (converter: `tools/mishnah_talmud/`).

## Hebrew Tanakh with morphology (OSHB)

All 39 books are included as an additional Hebrew version “Westminster Leningrad Codex (OSHB, with morphology)” under `bundled/own/…/he/`. Every word carries a lemma (Strong number) and a morphology code; a click in the reader shows stem, tense/form, person, gender and number and looks the lemma up in the dictionary.

Source and license: Original work of the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible, <https://github.com/openscriptures/morphhb> – text (WLC) public domain, lemma and morphology CC BY 4.0. Lemma table from <https://github.com/openscriptures/HebrewLexicon> (`oshb-lemmas.js`). Converter: `tools/oshb/`.

## Aleppo Codex, Targumim and parallels (ETCBC / CACCHT)

- **Aleppo Codex** – all 39 books as the Hebrew version “Aleppo Codex (מקרא על פי המסורה, ETCBC-CACCHT)”. Text: “מקרא על פי המסורה”, he.wikisource.org, dataset by Martijn Naaijer ([MartijnNaaijer/aleppo](https://github.com/MartijnNaaijer/aleppo)), license **CC BY-SA 4.0** (<https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/>). Changes: converted to chapters/verses; the reading text shows the Qere instead of the Ketiv. This text stays under CC BY-SA. Converter: `tools/aleppo/`.
- **Targum Onkelos (Sperber edition), Targum Pseudo-Jonathan and Targum Neofiti 1** (Genesis–Deuteronomy) with word morphology (`targum-lex.js`) – [ETCBC Targum Corpus](https://github.com/ETCBC/targum), release of 2026-10-28, license **CC BY-NC 4.0** (<https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/>) – non-commercial use only. For Onkelos, Sperber's variants and editorial deletions are left out of the reading text. Converter: `tools/targum/`. The word alignment for the interlinear (`targum-il.js`) is computed by machine (`tools/targum/align.py`).
- **Qumran scrolls** – biblical manuscripts from the Dead Sea as the Hebrew version “Qumran scrolls (ETCBC DSS)” for 36 books; per verse all scrolls with their sigla. Source: [ETCBC/dss](https://github.com/ETCBC/dss) (CACCHT Dead Sea Scrolls dataset), data by Martin G. Abegg Jr., James E. Bowley and Edward M. Cook, conversion by Jarod Jacobs, Martijn Naaijer and Dirk Roorda; license **CC BY-NC 4.0** (<https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/>) – non-commercial use only. Changes: grouped by book, chapter and verse, simplified characters. Converter: `tools/dss/`.
- **Qumran scrolls, non-biblical** – 735 non-biblical scrolls from the same dataset: the 40 largest (Community Rule, Damascus Document, War Scroll, Hodayot, Temple Scroll, Pesher Habakkuk, Genesis Apocryphon, Instruction, Jubilees and others) as separate Hebrew books, all others collected by find site (caves 1–11, other sites); fragment → line, reconstructions in [ ]. Every word carries lemma and morphological tags (click a word: part of speech, stem, form, person, gender, number, state, lemma; jump to the dictionary). Parallels: line similarity ≥ 70 % (ETCBC `sim`) between the scrolls and to Bible verses, as links. Same source and license as above (**CC BY-NC 4.0**, non-commercial use only). Converter: `tools/dss/nonbib.py`.
- **Pape, Griechisch-deutsches Handwörterbuch** – user-supplied StarDict files corresponding to the [Zeno-based offline edition by tovotu](https://tovotu.de/blog/520-Kostenlose-Griechisch-und-Lateinwrterbcher-fr-die-Offlinenutzung/4). Printed source identified by [Zeno](http://www.zeno.org/Pape-1880): third edition, revised by Max Sengebusch, sixth impression, Braunschweig 1914; marked public domain there. At the user’s request, 60 foreign Latin entries and Latin search aliases were removed, and joining hyphens removed from Greek headwords and article headings; 98,833 articles remain. Explanations and citations are unchanged, with 17 inline images embedded for offline use. No missing entries were added from Zeno. Converter, complete removal list and SHA256: `tools/pape/`.
- **Liddell & Scott, Greek-English Lexicon** – 115,617 articles under 115,628 Greek headwords. User-supplied `Grc-Eng_L&S_Greek-English Lexicon_or_1.dsl`; its companion annotation identifies Perseus (`Perseus:text:1999.04.0057`) as the text source and O.Rest, version 1.0 (02.2010), as the DSL compiler. The general abbreviation list in the `.ann` file supplies hover explanations. Identical articles share their headwords; wording is preserved. The files contain no explicit license statement. Provenance and SHA256: `tools/lsj/source.json`; converter: `tools/lsj/build.js`.
- **BDB (Brown-Driver-Briggs, unabridged)** – dictionary with 10,022 entries, also searchable by Strong number (H3) and BDB number. Public domain (1906); text base by Tim Morton (Bible Analyzer), Bible references by Stephen Ku et al., processing by Eliran Wong: [eliranwong/unabridged-BDB-Hebrew-lexicon](https://github.com/eliranwong/unabridged-BDB-Hebrew-lexicon). Converter: `tools/bdb/`.
- **Phonetic transcription** – version “Umschrift (ETCBC phono)” in all 39 books, the interlinear line “Umschrift (Aussprache)” and the pronunciation in the word popup. Source: [ETCBC/phono](https://github.com/ETCBC/phono) by Dirk Roorda (program and data under the **MIT** license), computed from the BHSA 2021 (Constantijn Sikkel, ETCBC; the BHSA itself is **CC BY-NC 4.0**, so the derived transcription is treated as non-commercial too). Changes: word parts joined to written words and matched to the words of the Westminster text by machine; the vocalised Qere carries the pronunciation (“*” marks it), the Ketiv stays empty. Converter: `tools/phono/`.
- **Ancient Hebrew inscriptions** – Mesha Stele, Siloam Inscription, Lachish Letters (3–6), Arad Ostraca (1, 2, 40), Ketef Hinnom Amulets, Deir Alla (Balaam) Inscription, Kuntillet Ajrud and Mesad Hashavyahu as separate Hebrew books under *Ancient Inscriptions*, every word with lemma and morphology, linked to the related Bible passages (e.g. Ketef Hinnom ↔ Numbers 6:24–26, Mesha ↔ 2 Kings 3:4–5, Siloam ↔ 2 Kings 20:20). Source: [ETCBC/extrabiblical](https://github.com/ETCBC/extrabiblical), version 0.2 (Constantijn Sikkel, Martijn Naaijer, Dirk Roorda), license **CC BY-NC 4.0** – non-commercial use only. Changes: arranged by piece and line (Mesha) or sentence; the Bible links were added by Safran. Converter: `tools/inscriptions/`.
- **Parallel passages** (`parallels.js`) – [ETCBC/parallels](https://github.com/ETCBC/parallels), MIT license, based on the BHSA (CC BY-NC 4.0, only as the starting data of the similarity computation). Converter: `tools/parallels/`.
- **Septuagint (CATSS)** – Greek version with the Hebrew counterpart per word group (interlinear switchable), 40 versions (Tanakh; Judges as Codex A and B, Daniel additionally after Theodotion); plus, minus and alignment tags are marked. Source: CATSS (Tov/Kraft), [Oxford Text Archive 1111](https://ota.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/repository/xmlui/handle/20.500.12024/1111), **CC BY-NC-SA 3.0**, non-commercial only – see `LICENSE-CATSS.md`. Converter: `tools/catss/`. The word card's list of Septuagint renderings (`oshb-lxx.js`, built with `tools/catss/lxx-lemma.js`) is derived from this alignment, the OSHB lemmas and the lxx-morph lemmas (below) and falls under the same licence. The CATSS file for Esther does not contain the book of Esther (it repeats Ezra) and carries no morphology; use the Rahlfs version of Esther listed below.
- **Septuagint morphology (lxx-morph)** – lemma (LSJ headword), part of speech and parsing for every Greek word of the bundled Septuagint versions (`lxx-morph.js`, about 620,000 words, 14,080 lemmas; word popup, Greek word card and forms table, lemma search). Source: [OpenScriptorium/lxx-morph](https://github.com/OpenScriptorium/lxx-morph) (mirror [git.sr.ht/~sethkush/lxx-morph](https://git.sr.ht/~sethkush/lxx-morph), state 2026-08-27), data **CC BY 4.0**: *LXX morphology data by Seth Kushniryk, CC BY 4.0, derived from Rahlfs Septuagint (Eliran Wong digital edition).* Changes: converted to a compact index; in the books of the Tanakh the words are aligned to the word order and verse numbering of the CATSS text (99.9 % of its words matched in context; the remaining 0.08 % take the most frequent parsing of their word form and are marked, 40 words have none). Converter: `tools/lxxmorph/build.js`; `tools/lxxmorph/compare-crosswire.js` only counts agreement with the CrossWire LXX module (nothing of that module is included).
- **Septuagint after Rahlfs** – Greek text of the books that have no CATSS version, taken from the same lxx-morph data (Rahlfs, *Septuaginta*, 1935, public domain; digital edition Eliran Wong), 21 files under `bundled/own/…/other/Septuaginta (Rahlfs….json`: as an additional version of Hosea, Esther (with the additions, marked by verse letters), *Das dritte Buch der Makkabäer*, *Das Buch Baruch*, *Der Brief Jeremias*, *Die Psalmen Salomos* and *Prayer of Manasseh* (= Ode 12), where chapters and verses match the existing versions; as separate titles "… (Septuaginta)" where the numbering of Rahlfs differs from the Sefaria/Kautzsch numbering: 1, 2 and 4 Maccabees, Sirach (the prologue as chapter 52), Wisdom of Solomon, Tobit (BA and Sinaiticus), Judith, Susanna and Bel and the Dragon (Old Greek and Theodotion), 1 Esdras and the Odes.
- **Greek glosses (STEPBible)** – short English meanings of the Greek lemmas in `lxx-morph.js`, from *TBESG – Translators Brief lexicon of Extended Strongs for Greek* and the gloss column of *TFLSJ*: [STEPBible-Data](https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data), created by Tyndale House, Cambridge / STEPBible.org, **CC BY 4.0**. Matched by lemma (60 % of the lemmas, 97 % of the words; mostly proper names have none).
- **Verse numbering traditions (TVTMS)** – table for converting verse references between the Hebrew (Masoretic, = Sefaria), English (KJV/NRSV), Latin (Vulgate) and Greek (Septuagint) numbering of the 39 books of the Tanakh (`versemap-data.js`, 655 rows, 32 KB; library `versemap.js`). Source: *TVTMS – Translators Versification Traditions with Methodology for Standardisation* in [STEPBible-Data](https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data) (`Versification/`), data created by STEPBible.org based on work at Tyndale House, Cambridge, **CC BY 4.0**. Built with `tools/tvtms/build.js` from the “condensed version” of the file. Changes against the source, as its licence asks to be noted: only the four main traditions and only the Tanakh are taken over; Hebrew verses that Sefaria does not count are dropped (Num 25:19); for the Greek tradition the tool picks, section by section, the column that the Rahlfs edition (lxx-morph) actually follows – the tests given in TVTMS plus a comparison of the wording with the bundled, Hebrew-numbered CATSS text – which is another Greek column or the Hebrew numbering in 40 of 297 sections, and 40 single verses (mostly Jer 49 = Rahlfs 30) are corrected by wording. TVTMS has no German (Luther) tradition.

## Bible map

Map of the places of a chapter (🗺 in the reading bar, also from the outline), with distance measurement, elevation profile, notes on places and routes. Sources:
- Places, references, location confidence: [OpenBible.info Bible Geocoding Data](https://github.com/openbibleinfo/Bible-Geocoding-Data) (CC BY 4.0).
- Terrain: Copernicus DEM GLO-30 (© DLR/ESA/Copernicus, free to use with attribution); coasts, rivers, lakes and current borders: Natural Earth (public domain). Converter: `tools/map/`.
- Finer terrain for the Land of Israel and its surroundings (33.8–36.95° E, 29.25–33.7° N; tiles in `map/hi/`, loaded when zooming in): Copernicus DEM GLO-30 with its water body mask (© DLR e.V. 2010–2014 and © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014–2018, provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA; all rights reserved), same colours and shading as the base relief.
- Streams and wadis (`map/water.js`) and modern towns (`map/towns.js`): © [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) contributors (ODbL), via the country exports of the [Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team](https://data.humdata.org/organization/hot) and the extracts of download.openstreetmap.fr; modern towns also from Natural Earth populated places (public domain).
- Roman roads (`map/roads.js`): [Itiner-e](https://itiner-e.org) – P. de Soto, A. Pažout, T. Brughmans et al., *A High-Resolution Dataset of Roads of the Roman Empire: Itiner-e static version 2024*, Zenodo, [doi:10.5281/zenodo.17122148](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17122148) (CC BY 4.0); clipped to the map area and simplified.
- Routes: [UBS Bible Routes](https://github.com/ubsicap/ubs-open-license) (MARBLE, after Leen Ritmeyer, © United Bible Societies 2023, **CC BY-SA 4.0**) – `map/routes.js` is therefore under the same license; Bible references and German titles are our own assignment.
- Places in Josephus (Jewish War, Antiquities 11–20) and in the books of the Maccabees: [ToposText](https://topostext.org) (Brady Kiesling, Aikaterini Laskaridis Foundation; Open Data Commons Attribution). The assignment to Sefaria chapters is derived from the Whiston text (`tools/map/extract_topostext.py`, `tools/map/build_josephus.py`) and stored in `map/josephus.js`.
- Places in rabbinic literature (Talmud Bavli and Yerushalmi, Mishnah, Tosefta, Midrash Rabbah, Sifre and others): ALMA – Ancient Locations in rabbinic literature, compiled by Ido Wachtel (Hebrew University of Jerusalem, GIS), [online map](https://experience.arcgis.com/experience/5ebdd921862447daa05118ad17b118a1). The references were mapped to Sefaria's sections with Sefaria's API (`tools/map/build_alma.py`).
- Jerusalem in detail (🏛 button in the map, at high zoom): [Ordnance Survey of Jerusalem](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:1865_Ordnance_Survey_of_Jerusalem_Old_City_full_map.jpg) (Charles W. Wilson, 1864/65, public domain, Wikimedia Commons), hillshade from Copernicus DEM GLO-30, today's streets and wall from [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). Build: `tools/map/build_jerusalem.py`, `tools/map/build_jer_meta.py`.

## Audio and outlines

- **Audio:** Hebrew reading of the Tanakh by Abraham Schmueloff, source: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/HebrewOldTestamentReadByAbrahamSchmueloff) (Talking Bibles International). Split into chapters; verse markers in `audio-markers/`. The audio files are not part of the repository but of the GitHub release `audio` (one `Audio_<Book>.zip` per book). The licence information is being clarified with Talking Bibles International.
- **Outlines (`outlines/`):** outlines of 39 books transcribed from a printed edition; German and English translations are machine-generated and not proofread. Tools: `tools/assemble_outline_photos.py`, `tools/merge_outline_tr.py`.
- **Talmud outlines (`outlines/Berakhot.json` … `Niddah.json`, 38 tractates):** sugya sections and argument steps from [talmud.dev](https://talmud.dev) (Shaun Regenbaum), AI-generated there; English, Hebrew where talmud.dev has the same division. Pages that talmud.dev has not generated yet (e.g. most of Tamid) are missing. Tools: `tools/talmud_dev/`.

## Interface language

The interface is available in German and English (⋯ menu → Sprache). The English strings live in `i18n.js`: German original on the left, translation on the right; `{}` and `{#}` (number) are placeholders. New interface strings in the code stay German and get an entry there. `test10.js` checks the table.
