[20] Syro-hex. according to Norberg's transcript: ܗܠܝܢ ܕܫܪܟܐ ܗܘ ܕܚܪܒ[?] ܠܗܘ ܕܢܣܪ[?]. ܘܗܘ ܕܕܪܟ ܥܢܒ̈ܐ ܠܗܘ ܕܢܣܪ[?] ◆. Cf. our note in _Otium Norvic._ p. 71, where we suspected an error in the repeated ܠܗܘ ܕܢܣܪ[?], which nevertheless we left untouched; but for ܕܚܪܒ[?] we confidently wrote ܕܟܪܒ[?]. In the codex, however, ܕܟܪܒ[?] is written, and twice ܠܗܘ ܕܢܣܪ[?]. At length a probable remedy for the other error was supplied by the Rev. Dr. Skat Rördam, who in a letter to us dated the 16th of September 1868 wrote: "For the second ܕܢܣܪ[?] one should without doubt read ܕܢܓܕ[?], ἑλκύοντα. Cf. Sym. on Psal. cxxv. 6. In the somewhat obscure Estrangela script (ܢܓܕ[?]) these words could quite easily be confused." (In the text of the LXX, for περκάσει ἡ σταφυλὴ our Syrian put ܢܚܟܡܢ[?] ܥܢܒ̈ܐ, which Middeld. absurdly rendered _the grapes shall be joined in marriage_, concluding that the translator had not grasped the sense of the verb περκάζειν. Bernstein in _Zeitschrift_ etc. T. IV, p. 210 recognizes the error, but attributes it to Norberg's careless transcription, contending that the transcript had ܢܚܩܡܢ[?], _they shall ripen_. In vain: for the codex too supports ܢܚܟܡܢ[?], and ܚܟܡ[?], _it ripened_, represents the Greek πεπαίνεσθαι, not περκάζειν. That is, περκάζειν, _to grow dark_, or _to begin to be painted with dark spots_, in Hesiod αἰόλλεσθαι (as Hieron. here learnedly rendered περκάσει by _shall be variegated_), belongs to the _third_, not yet ripe, stage of the grape, according to the description of Philostratus in Imagg. II, 1: Βότρυς δὲ οἱ μὲν ὀργῶσιν (i.e. οἱ πεπανθέντες, or πέπειροι, in Syriac ܒܫ̈ܝܠܐ), οἱ δὲ περκάζουσιν (ܚܟܡ̈ܐ[?], or ܚܟܝ̈ܡܐ[?]), οἱ δ᾽ ὄμφακες (ܒܣܪ̈ܐ), οἱ δ᾽ οἰνάνθαι (cf. Hex. on Cant. Cant. ii. 5) δοκοῦσιν; to which passage the gloss of Phavorinus refers: Περκάζουσιν, παρὰ Φιλοστράτῳ, ἤγουν ἄρχονται πεπαίνεσθαι. The word ܚܟܡ[?] in this sense is almost unknown to the Lexicons except from the derivatives ܚܶܟܡܳܐ[?] and ܚܶܟܡܽܘܡܳܐ[?], used of _unripe fruits_, especially of _sour grapes_; but besides our passage another undoubted example exists in S. Ephraem, Carm. Nisib. LXX, 26, in Bickell's rendering: _Marvellous also is the deed of Jephthah, the vinedresser who plucked a grape still unripe_ (ܚܘܡܣܬܐ[?] ܕܚܶܟܡܳܐ[?]), _and offered it to the lord of the vineyard._ I add one thing: in the Hexapla on Zach. xi. 8, for בָּחַל, _he loathed_ (which notion learned men also attribute to the Hebrew בָּעַל in a pair of passages, Jerem. iii. 14. xxxi. 32), Aquila rendered ἐπέρκασεν, evidently, in a word ἅπαξ λεγομένῳ, taking refuge in the Chaldee dialect, in which בְּחַל yields exactly the same sense that we have endeavoured to claim for the Syriac ܚܟܡ[?], that is, _to begin to ripen._ For thus Buxtorf in _Lex. Chald. et Talmud._ p. 285: "For _the beginning of ripeness_ is properly signified by this verb; whence the Aruch explains, _when they ripen_, ולא נתבשלו בטוב, _but have not yet ripened well._")
