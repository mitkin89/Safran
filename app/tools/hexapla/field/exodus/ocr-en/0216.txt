[21] Cod. X. Cat. Niceph. p. 701: ὁ δὲ Ἀκ. ἐνωπλισμένοι, φησί· Σύμ. ὁπλῖται. Somewhat differently Nobil. and Cod. 85, on the testimony of Montef.: Ἀ. Σ. καθωπλισμένοι. Θ. πεμπταΐζοντες. Finally Jerome in Epist. XXXVI to Damasus (Opp. T. I, p. 167): “For Aquila, who translates word for word, not more contentiously, as some think, but more studiously, in the place where the LXX put: _But in the fifth generation the children of Israel went up out of the land of Egypt_, rendered it thus: καὶ ἐνοπλισάμενοι [Cod. Vat., on the testimony of Vallarsi, ἐνωπλισμένοι] ἀνέβησαν οἱ υἱοὶ Ἰσραὴλ ἀπὸ τῆς γῆς Αἰγύπτου; that is, _And the children of Israel went up armed out of the land of Egypt._ Although, for what we have called _armed_, owing to the ambiguity of the Greek language, _equipped_ or _furnished_ may be understood, on account of the goods with which they despoiled the Egyptians.” As regards Theodotion's version, πεμπταΐζοντες does not mean _fifthed, divided into five parts_, as Montef. and Schleusner dreamed; nor _by the number five_, from πεμπτὰς, which is Drusius's error; but _doing something on the fifth day_ (or, καταχρηστικῶς, _in the fifth generation;_ cf. Hex. on Gen. iv. 24), from πεμπταῖος, as in the medical writers τριταΐζω and τεταρταΐζω, from τριταῖος and τεταρταῖος.
