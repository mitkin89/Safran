[6] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܡܢ ܚܒܠܐ ܕܡܘܬܐ܀, i.e., as Bugati renders it, _from the destruction of death._ I would not, however, believe that the Syrian read ἀπὸ ἐπιβουλῆς θανάτου in the Greek, but rather that he did not wish to imitate what seemed to him an inversion of the Greek words. Agellius, on what authority I do not know: "Symmachus translated λόγον ἐπηρείας, _a word of insult;_ Aquila λόγον ἐπιβουλῆς, _an insidious speech._" Cf. on Psal. li. 4. lvi. 2. xciii. 20.
