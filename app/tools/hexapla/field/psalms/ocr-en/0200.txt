[5] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܠܡܫܡܠܝܐ[?] ܥܠܝ. Cf. on Psal. vii. 10. Almost the same Hebrew, יִגְמֹר בַּעֲדִי, the same translator renders more correctly at Psal. cxxxvii. 8, ἐπιτελέσει ὑπὲρ ἐμοῦ, as Bugatus also gave in our passage, _perfecting upon me_, though the usage of the passive participle is against it. But when Heidenheim (_Deutsche Vierteljahrschrift_ etc. T. I, p. 275), in describing a specimen of the readings of the Cod. Mus. Brit., our B., states that in that cod. ܠܡܫܡܠܡ[?] is found for ܠܡܫܡܠܝܐ[?], he is greatly mistaken; for it reads exactly as Cod. A. We can also affirm with certainty that the other readings extracted by the same learned man from the said cod. were either carelessly copied or, what seems truer, are to be imputed to the negligence of the printers.

[9] Euseb. cites: Σ. ἐν μέσῳ λεόντων εὐθαρσῶν ἐκοιμήθην. Thus one Colb., but εὐθαρσὴς ὢν for εὐθαρσῶν. Nobil., Vat.: Σ. εὐθαρσῶν ἐκοιμήθην μεταξὺ φλεγόντων. Theodoret.: Σ. μετὰ φλεγόντων. Finally Syro-hex.: Σ. ἐκοιμήθην μεταξὺ φλεγόντων (ܕܫܟܒܬ ܒܝܢܬ ܡܫܠܗ̈ܒܢܐ). These can be reconciled with one another if we assume that Symmachus, a rather wordy translator, put εὐθαρσῶν ἐκοιμήθην for the single word אֶשְׁכְּבָה, and before לֹהֲטִים, as modern translators too have done, assumed an ellipsis τοῦ μετὰ or μεταξὺ. Montfaucon restored the reading of Symmachus by conjecture thus: Σ. μεταξὺ λεόντων ἐκοιμήθην φλεγόντων.
