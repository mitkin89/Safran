[26] Hieron. in the Epist. to Sun. and Fret. 57: "‘_To thy saints._’ For which you say you found in the Greek ‘_to thy sons_’. But it should be known that in the Hebrew it has LAASIDACH, which all translated τοῖς ὁσίοις σου, that is, ‘_to thy saints_’; and the Sixth edition alone rendered ‘_to thy prophets_’, expressing the sense rather than the word. And only in the Κοινῇ have I found ‘_sons_’ for ‘_saints_’." Where Vallarsi says: "The Hexapla and the old [Jerome] edd. have ἁγίοις for ὁσίοις, which seems truer." On the contrary, not a single Greek translator has said ἅγιος for the Hebr. חָסִיד, but all constantly ὅσιος. The error of the old edd. misled Montfaucon, who "from MSS. and Hieron." constructed the hexaplar note thus: Ἀ. Ο΄. τότε ἐλάλησας ἐν ὁράσει τοῖς ἁγίοις σου (Ο΄. υἱοῖς σου). Σ. τότε ἐλάλησας δι᾽ ὁράσεως τοῖς ἁγίοις σου. Ϛ΄. τοῖς προφήταις σου. In Parsons' apparatus no codex has τοῖς ἁγίοις σου, one (Cod. 175) τοῖς ὁσίοις σου, another (Cod. 264) gives in the margin: γρ. τοῖς ὁσίοις σου. Ἀ. Σ. Ε΄. οὕτως καὶ (καὶ written in abbreviated form hardly differs from the siglum Ϛ΄) τοῖς προφήταις σου.
