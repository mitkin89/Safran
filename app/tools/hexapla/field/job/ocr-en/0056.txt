[13] In the present-day copies τῶν Ο΄ these words, διὰ τοῦτο—ἐφρόντισα αὐτοῦ, are taken for v. 14, although entirely unlike the Hebrew: then those words, ἐπὶ τούτῳ—ἐξ αὐτοῦ, are passed off as the Septuagint version of v. 15. Others, however, with Grabe, have more correctly determined that v. 14, which in Latin runs: “_For he will perform my appointed portion_ (that which he has appointed for me), _and many such things_ are _with him_,” is entirely wanting both in the Septuagint version and in the remains of the other interpreters; and that what is read today in the printed editions should be taken as a double rendering of v. 15.
[14] “The Colbertine, with an asterisk prefixed, Ἐκ τῶν Ἀκύλα καὶ Θ. οἱ β΄. τετάραγμαι. That τετάραγμαι seems likewise to be Aquila's and Theodotion's, in place τοῦ κατασπουδασθῶ, which is read today in the version τῶν Ο΄. The Latin Ed. of Jerome has _I shall be troubled_, and marks these two versicles with an asterisk.”—_Montef._ Syro-hex. in the text gives: ※ Θ. (not Ἀ. Θ.) ἐν τούτῳ (ܒܗܕܐ) ἀπὸ πρ. αὐτοῦ κατασπουδασθῶ (ܐܬܬܪܗܒ[?]) κ. τ. ἑ. But since κατασπουδασθῶ entirely recalls Aquila (see Hex. on 2 Reg. iv. 1. Psal. ii. 5. vi. 3, 10), it is more credible that τετάραγμαι belongs to another interpreter. See the next note.
[15] “Syro-hex. ܣ. ܡܫܓܫ ܐܢܐ ܀ Σ. ταράσσω. The Syriac verb seems to be wrongly written; for the active form of the participle is offensive.”—_Middeld._ ܡܫܓܫ ܐܢܐ can indeed be defended, in an intransitive sense, _I shall be in tumult_, θορυβήσω (cf. Hex. on Psal. ii. 1. lviii. 15). But since I have been assured that in the codex ܡܫܓܫ[?] ܐܢܐ is expressly written, I have not hesitated to claim the anonymous reading τετάραγμαι for Symmachus. Moreover it should be noted that Nobilius gives on ἐσπούδακα: Schol. ἐθροήθην καὶ ἐταράχθην (Cod. 248 in the margin adds: ἀγνοῶν τῆς πληγῆς τὴν αἰτίαν); and also on the words κατασπουδασθῶ, κατανοήσω: Schol. ἀμηχανῶ καὶ κατανοῶν πτοοῦμαι, which Cod. 248 also has in the margin.
