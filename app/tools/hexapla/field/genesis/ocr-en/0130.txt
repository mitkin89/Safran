The latter part Matthaei transcribed from Cod. 127 thus: καὶ πρὸς σὲ ἡ ὁρμὴ αὕτη. Συ. ἀλλ’ ἐξουσιάσῃς αὐτῆς, which seems to be slightly corrupt. Hieron.: “The sense in the Hebrew is very different from that in the LXX translators … _Shall it not, if thou doest well, be forgiven thee; and if thou doest not well, thy sin shall sit before the doors, and to thee shall be its fellowship_ [cf. on Cap. iii. 17]; _but do thou rather rule over it_ … What, however, caused the error in the LXX translators is this, that _sin_, that is, ATTATH, is of the masculine gender in Hebrew, of the feminine in Greek; and those who translated rendered it in the masculine gender, as it was in the Hebrew.”

[16] Combefis's MSS. See Origen. Opp. T. II, p. 30. From this scholium, misunderstood, Montef. set down in the Hexapla: Ἀ. Ο΄. διέλθωμεν εἰς τὸ πεδίον. That is, he wrongly translated the words καὶ οἱ περὶ Ἀκύλαν ἔδειξαν as: _Aquila, however, exhibits [it];_ whereas they rather mean: _which the version of Aquila also shows._ The truth was seen by Scharfenb. in _Animadv._ pp. 4, 5, who moreover probably conjectures ἔκδοσιν for ἐκδοχὴν. But as for the most learned critic's assertion that the remaining words of this scholium, ὅτι ἐν τῷ ἀποκρύφῳ κ.τ.ἑ., can hardly be intact, _because_ ὅτι _does not seem to fit the sequence of the sentence_, his error was pointed out by Villoison in the Preface to _Nov. Vers. Gr._ etc. p. xix, who appeals to the scholiasts' habit of beginning with the customary word ὅτι, namely so that σημείωσαι or σημειωτέον is to be understood. For the rest, that Aquila did not read the superfluous clause is clearly demonstrated by another scholium in the margin of Cod. 127: Ταῦτα ἐκ τοῦ ἀποκρύφου δοκεῖ ὑπὸ τῶν Ο΄ εἰλῆφθαι· ἔχειν (sic) δὲ αὐτὰ καὶ
