[11] Cod. X. Hieron.: “Again Aq. interpreted it as συχνεῶνα, which we can call a _briar_ or a _thorn-brake_, and, to render the force of the word, _dense and interwoven brushwood_. Hence Sym. too, led to the same opinion, says: _And there appeared after this a ram caught in a net_ [שְׂבָכָה] _by its horns._ But to some, in this passage at least, the LXX and Theod. seem to have interpreted better, who put the very name _sabech_, saying: _in the thicket sabech by its horns._ For [they say that] συχνεὼν, or _net_, which Aq. and Sym. put, is written with the letter SIN, whereas here the letter SAMECH is used: from which it is plain that the word SABECH does not mean dense growth and brushwood interwoven like a net, but is the name of a shrub which is so called in Hebrew. I, however, on careful inquiry, have frequently found συχνεῶνα written with the letter SAMECH.” Before Martianay ἐν συχνῷ or συχνεῷ was printed, against the manuscripts. Cod. 135 on σαβὲκ adds in the margin: συχνέω (sic) εἶπεν τὸ σαβέκ.
[12] Diodorus in Cat. Niceph. p. 282: τὸ, ἐν φυτῷ, οὐκ ἔχει ὁ Σύρος, μόνον δὲ τὸ σαβέκ· τοῦτο δὲ τὸ ὄνομα τοῦ φυτοῦ εἶναι νομίζω. The Hebrew word, with regard to its etymology, Graeco-Ven. not badly renders ἐν ξυνηρεφεῖ; but best of all the English translator, _in a thicket._
