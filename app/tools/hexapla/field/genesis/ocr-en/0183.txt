[28] (Forts.) v. 2: “Finally, SOREC is interpreted by some as καλλίκαρπος, which we can render as _most beautiful fruits_.”
[29] So Codd. 18, 29, 57, 64 (from a correction), and others, Justin. M. in Dial. p. 242, and the Fathers. The word is illustrated by a passage of Aristot. de Gener. Anim. V. 1, 17: Τὰ δὲ τῶν ἀνθρώπων ὄμματα πολύχροα συμβέβηκεν εἶναι· καὶ γὰρ γλαυκοὶ, καὶ χαροποὶ (_blue-grey_), καὶ μελανόφθαλμοί τινές εἰσιν, οἱ δ᾽ αἰγωποί.
[30] Codd. X, 85 (without name), 127. Cat. Niceph. p. 526: ἈΔΗΛΟΥ. κατάκοροι, θερμοὶ, διάπυροι, φοβεροί. Cod. 57 in the margin: Ἄλλος φησί· κατάκαροι (sic), θερμοὶ, κ.τ.ἑ. “Κατάκαρος can be referred to κάρος, _stupor_ . . . and κατάκορος to κόρος, _satiety_, by the same law of analogy as κατάκοπος to κόπος.”—_Holmes._ On the contrary, κατάκαρος is no word at all; and κατάκορος means _black_, παρὰ τὸ κορὸν, τὸ μέλαν (Schol. ad Oppian. Hal. I, 133), in which sense the Graeco-Ven. translator uses the word at Cant. Cant. i. 6: μηδαμῶς ὁρᾶτέ με, ὅτι εἰμὶ κατάκορος. Cf. Hex. ad Prov. xxiii. 29.
[31] So in the text Codd. 15, 29, 38 (with θαλασσῶν in the margin), and others.
[32] Cod. X.
[33] The same. Cod. VII in the margin by a second hand: ὄνος ὀγκαρίζων.
[34] The same; i.e. _among the drippings_, or _waters_. The anonymous reading may perhaps favour those interpreters who affirm that the much-vexed Hebrew word bears the meaning of _troughs_ at which sheep are watered (Psal. lxvii. 14), or in which drippings are caught (Ezech. xl. 43).
[35] “So Drusius [who copied it from the fragments which he himself had once gathered from every quarter]. _Of neighbourhoods_, i.e. γειτονιῶν, as we have put it between two brackets.”—_Montef._ The reading belongs to Jud. v. 16, and comes from Eusebius in the Onomasticon s. v. Μοσφεθαΐμ, where for the Greek μεταιχμίων Hieron. rendered _of neighbourhoods_. Cf. Hex. ad Jud. l. c., where the LXX rendered ἀναμέσον τῆς διγομίας, as in our passage the Graeco-Ven. translator renders ἀνὰ τὰ ἡμιφόρτια.

[38] Cod. VII in the margin by a second hand. The anonymous translator seems to have had in view the Syriac ܫܦ[?], _he crept_; the Seventy, however, the Hebrew שׁוּף in the sense of _lying in wait_, which they gave it at Gen. iii. 15.
