[5] Hieron.: “Many and various things are disputed among the Hebrews about this passage; among the Greeks and our own writers, indeed, there is silence about it. Some think that IAMIM means _seas_ [הַיַּמִּים]; for _seas_ is written with the same letters with which this word here is written. And they hold that he, while feeding his father's asses in the desert, found gatherings of waters, which according to the idiom of the Hebrew language are called _seas;_ that is, that he found a pool, a thing difficult to find in the wilderness. Some think that _hot springs_ [so Hieron. himself in the Vulgate] are signified by this word, after the neighbouring Punic language, which borders on the Hebrew. There are those who suppose that wild asses were let in by him to the she-asses, and that he himself discovered this kind of mating, so that from them very swift asses might be born, which are called IAMIM. Most [Kimchi, Vers. Arab., Graeco-Ven.] think that he was the first to have herds of mares covered by asses in the desert, so that from this mules, new animals contrary to nature, might be born. Aquila translated this passage thus: _This is Ana, who found_ σὺν τοὺς ἡμίν [Martianay edited from the MSS. ἐμὶμ, affirming that some read η̄ for ε̄, and ν̄ for μ̄. Vallarsi, however, attests that some MSS. offer ἰαμεὶμ or ἰαμὶμ; one Reginensis ἠαμὶν, another ἠμὴν, and for Sym. ἡμὶν or ἡμύν. Finally, Lagarde's three agree in ἡμὶν]; and Sym. likewise τοὺς ἡμὶν, a rendering which signifies the plural number. But the LXX and Theod. translated alike, saying τὸν ἰαμὶν, which indicates the singular number.”
