CAP. XXXVIII.
[1] So Ald., Codd. III, Bodl., 14, 15, 16, and others, Syro-hex.
[2] Hieron.: “A Hebrew word is here taken as the name of a place, which Aquila translated as a thing, saying: _And he called his name Selom. And it came to pass that she lied in childbearing, after she had borne him._ For after she had borne Selom, her childbearing ceased. CHAZBI, therefore, is not the name of a place, but means _lie_ [as if from כִּזֵּב, _he lied_]. Hence also in another place [Habac. iii. 17] it is written: _The work of the olive shall lie_ [כִּחֵשׁ], that is, the olive shall not yield fruit.” Jerome's Latin in Greek would be: καὶ ἐγένετο ὅτι ἐψεύσατο ἐν τοκετῷ, μετὰ τὸ τεκεῖν αὐτόν; which, however, seems to be redundant compared with the Hebrew.
[3] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܣ. ܡܚܒܠ ܗܘܐ ❖. Cod. 38 in the margin without name: διέφθειρε, and so a Regius cited by Montef.
[4] So Syro-hex., Arm. 1, and without asterisk Cod. 15.
[5] Nobil. cites: Schol. σπαθαρίσκον. So in the margin Cod. 38, and two Regii cited by Montef. (one of which has σπαθαρίσκῳ). Perhaps it is Symmachus's; compare Hex. ad Jesai. iii. 23. Cod. 129 offers in the margin the gloss ἀρκαδικὴν; by means of which Theodoret. Opp. T. I, p. 562 can be emended: οὐ τὸ βασιλικὸν περιεβέβλητο σχῆμα (David dancing before the ark), ἀλλ’ ἐξωμίδα μόνον, ἣν νῦν καλοῦσιν Ἀρκαδίκιν (sic).

[7] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܕܨܘܪܐ[?] ❖, with the Greek added: ΠΕΡΙΤΡΑΧΗΛΟΝ (sic). Cf. on v. 25. Montef., from what source I know not, edited: Ἄλλος· τὸν ἐπιτράχηλον.
[8] Nobil. (with καὶ στρεπτὸν), Cod. 127 (with καὶ τὸν τρεπτὸν), Cat. Niceph. p. 440 (with καὶ τὸ στρεπτόν). Suidas: στρεπτός· περιδέρραιος κόσμος. Glossae: _Torques_ [necklaces], στρεπτοί. Cf. Hex. ad Cant. i. 11.
[9] Diodorus in Cat. Niceph. p. 440: Τὸν ὁρμίσκον, ὃν δέδωκεν ὁ Ἰούδας τῇ Θάμαρ, ὁ Σύρος ὡράριον [write ὠράριον, _orarium;_ cf. Hex. ad Jesai. iii. 20 in the _Addenda_] λέγεσθαί φησι, καὶ οὐχ ὁρμίσκον. The simple Syriac renders ܣܘܕܪܐ, Onkelos שׁוֹשִׁיפָא, both of which mean _linen cloth_ or _napkin;_ Graeco-Ven. καλύπτρα, i.e. _a woman's head-covering._
[10] Nobil. cites: Σ. ἐγχειρίδιον. Moreover Diodorus, on the word ὁρμίσκον, continues: Σύμμαχος δὲ στρεπτὸν ἐγχειρίδιον, where στρεπτὸν seems to belong to Aquila's reading, while ἐγχειρίδιον belongs not to ὁρμίσκον but to ῥάβδον. Unless we grant Scharfenberg (_Animadv._ p. 38) that the expression στρεπτὸν ἐγχειρίδιον is to be explained as one phrase, as if one were to say _a hand-torque_, i.e. _a bracelet_, which is Jerome's rendering. Finally, on ὁρμίσκον (ܐܘܪܡܝܣܩܘܣ[?], a word missing from the Lexicons) our Syriac notes: “They say that Ὁρμίσκον is called in Greek _monile_ [necklace] (ܡܘܢܝܠܐ[?]), that is, a torque which is put round the neck (ܗܢܐ ܕܨܘܪܐ[?]), and its band (ܦܣܩܝܬܐ[?]), and also _bracelet_ (ܫܒܝܪܐ[?]).”
