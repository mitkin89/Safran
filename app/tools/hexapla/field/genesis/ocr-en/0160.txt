[9] Hieron.: “_For you have prevailed with God, and with men you shall prevail._ Josephus, in the first book of the Antiquities [20, 2], thinks that he was called Israel because he stood against the angel [σημαίνει δὲ τοῦτο κατὰ τὴν Ἑβραίων γλῶτταν, τὸν ἀντιστάντα ἀγγέλῳ θείῳ]; which I, examining it diligently, could not find in the Hebrew at all. And what need is there for me to seek out the opinions of individuals, when he who gave the name himself explains its etymology? _Your name_, he says, _shall not be called Jacob, but Israel shall be your name._ Why? Aq. renders ὅτι ἦρξας μετὰ θεοῦ; Sym., ὅτι ἤρξω πρὸς θεόν; LXX and Theod., ὅτι ἐνίσχυσας μετὰ θεοῦ. For SARITH, from which the word Israel is derived, means _prince_. The sense, therefore, is: your name shall not be called _supplanter_, that is, _Jacob;_ but your name shall be called _prince with God_, that is, _Israel._ For as I am a prince, so you too, who were able to wrestle with me, shall be called a prince. But if you were able to fight with me, who am God, or an angel . . . , how much more with men, that is, with Esau, whom you need not fear!” What sense the reading of Symmachus, ὅτι ἤρξω πρὸς θεὸν, _because you began_ (not, as some wrongly render, _you were prince_) _towards God_, can yield, I do not know. Moreover, before Vallarsi the editions read ὅτι ἤρξω ἰσχύϊ πρὸς θεὸν, and in two MSS. cited by Lagarde (the third agrees with Vallarsi) the letters Ι̅C̅ stand between ω̄ and πρὸς. In the parallel passage Hos. xii. 4 (5), for וַיָּשַׂר אֶל־מַלְאָךְ our Syriac assigns to Symmachus ܐܬܥܫܢ[?] ܥܡ ܡܠܐܟܐ, which we have rendered κατεδυνάστευσε τὸν ἄγγελον.
