CAP. XLIX.
[1] So in the margin Codd. X, 64; in the text, however, Comp., Codd. III, VI, VII, 14, 15, 16, and others. Cf. Hex. ad Jesai. xiii. 14. xxxiv. 15. liv. 7. Ezech. xx. 34.
[2] Cat. Niceph. p. 508 (with Ῥουβὶμ three times for Ῥουβήν). Nobil. cites: Ἀ. σὺ ἰσχύς μου, κ.τ.ἑ. Σ. Ῥουβὴν (sic) πρ. μου, κ.τ.ἑ. For Aquila Cod. X, according to Griesb., has only: Ἀ. κεφάλαιον λύπης μου. Cf. Hex. ad Psal. lxxvii. 51. For Symmachus Niceph. and Nobil. write defectively: περισσὰ λαβεῖν, καὶ ἐκ περισσοῦ ὑπερζέσας ὡς ὕδωρ κ.τ.ἑ., which must be supplemented thus from Procopius (who wrongly ascribes them to Aquila) in Cat. Niceph. pp. 507, 508 (in the lower margin): π. λαβεῖν, καὶ ἐκ π. κρατῆσαι· ὑπερέζεσας (sic) κ.τ.ἑ. Hieron. in the Vulgate imitated Aquila, rendering: “_Reuben, my firstborn, you are my strength, and the beginning of my sorrow; first in gifts, greater in command._” The latter words he translated thus in Quaest. in Gen. p. 374: “_and the chief among my children; greater in bearing, and greater in strength._”
[3] Diodorus in Cat. Niceph. ibid. (with Ῥουβὶμ), and Nobil. (with ἀρχὴ without the article).
[4] Cod. VII in the margin by a second hand. Perhaps it is Theodotion's, whose version just mentioned does not depart a finger's breadth from the LXX (if you except σκλ. καὶ αὐθ. for καὶ σκλ. αὐθ., a reading which Origen and Ambrose also seem to have found in the LXX).
[5] Epiphan. in Ancorat. 99: ἐν δὲ τῷ Ἑβραϊκῷ ἐλθωβὰρ (sic), ὅπερ ἐστὶν ἑρμηνευόμενον· μὴ ἀνακάμψῃς, ἢ μὴ προστεθείης, ἢ πάλιν, μὴ περισσεύσῃς.
[6] Nobil., Cat. Niceph. (with μὴ περισσεύσας), both in continuation. For ὑπερζέσας, however, we have taken ὑπερέζεσας (פָּחַזְתָּ, a reading commended by nearly all the ancient translators) from Procopius. For ἐθάμβευσας cf. Hex. ad Jud. ix. 4. Zeph. iv. 3. On ἐξύβρισας Cod. VII has added by a second hand ἐθαμβήθης, and also on ἐκζέσῃς, περισσεύθῃς (sic). (In the LXX Origen, Hieron., and Ambrose direct the punctuation: ἐξύβρισας (“_you have treated me with insult_”), ὡς ὕδωρ μὴ ἐκζέσῃς. On the contrary S. Cyril. in Genes. p. 211: ἐξυβρίζων ὡς ὕδωρ, τουτέστιν, ἀφόρητον ἔχων τὴν καθ᾽ ὧν ἂν ἕλοιτο καταδρομήν.)
[7] Diodorus in continuation, on the testimony of the same. The common Syriac agrees: ܛܥܝܬ ܐܝܟ ܡܝܐ. ܠܐ ܬܦܘܫ[?].
[8] Cod. VII in the margin by a second hand: ὁμόγνῶ[?] (sic). (For the form cf. Lobeck. ad Phryn. p. 385.)
[9] So Ald., Codd. I (“with some space left between.”—_Grabe_), VII (“The first hand itself seems to read ἐξαίρεσεως, although, given the practice of the codex, it would not follow with sufficient certainty that it read ἐξ αἱρέσεως; the second hand certainly read this.”—_Ceriani_), and others, Arab. 1, 2, the Syriac cited by Bar Hebraeus, and the Fathers.
[10] Cod. X cites: Ἀ. σκεύη ἀδικίας ἀνάσκαφε (sic). “So the Coislinianus. The last word seems to be completely corrupt. For although ἀνασκάπτειν fits the Hebrew word כָּרָה, that corrupt ἀνάσκαφε can in no way agree with מְכֵרֹת.”—_Montef._ For ἀνάσκαφε we have restored, by a very slight change, ἀνασκαφαὶ, i.e. מְכֵרֹת, _diggings_.
