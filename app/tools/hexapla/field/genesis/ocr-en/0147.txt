[5] Nobil. cites: Schol. ἐστρατεύσατο. Cod. X: Σ. ὁ δὲ ἐστρατεύετο. Cod. 127: Σ. ὁ δὲ ἐστρατεύσατο. On the confusion of the verbs στρατεύεσθαι and στραγγεύεσθαι, than which none is more inveterate, one should read Kuster on Suid. s. v. Ἠ δεῖ χελώνης κ. τ. ἑ.; Valckenaer on Ammon. p. 132; Wesseling, _Probabil._ p. 290. For the rest, the spelling στραγεύεσθαι with a single γ̄, which is distinguished from στρατεύεσθαι by the minutest stroke, has something to defend itself with. Cf. Hex. on Prov. xxiv. 10.

[10] Nobil., Cod. 135: ὁράσει ἐδ. τὸ (τὸ omitted by Cod. 135) πρ. σ. Hieron.: “In the Hebrew it has: _Behold! I have accepted thy face_, that is, _I yield to thy prayers;_ which Sym., interpreting according to the sense, renders: ὁράσει ἐδυσωπήθην τὸ πρόσωπόν σου.” For ὁράσει, for which Vallarsi, from the older codex of the Queen [of Sweden], unhappily attempts ὅρα ἤΰτε (or εὖτε), _see how_ (!), Scharfenb. in _Animadv._ p. 18 gave his vote to Drusius's conjecture ὅρα σοι. But in three codices collated by Lagarde the strokes of the letters plainly indicate the reading ὅρα εἰ. For the rest of the reading cf. Hex. on Mal. i. 8. ii. 9.
