[34] Hieron.: "_And a herald cried before him._ For which Aq. rendered: _and he cried in his sight a kneeling;_ Sym., interpreting the Hebrew word itself, says: _and he cried before him, Abrech._ Hence it seems to me that what is to be understood is not so much _herald_, or _kneeling_, which can be taken of saluting or adoring Joseph, as rather what the Hebrews [Targ. Jonath. and Hieros.: _This is the father of the king, great in wisdom and tender_ (רַכִּיךְ) _in years_] hand down, saying that _tender father_ is rendered from this word: for AB means _father_, RECH _delicate_, or _most tender_." Origen had led the way in Cat. Niceph. p. 461: Τὸ Ἑβραϊκὸν ἔχει ἀβρὴχ, ὃ κυρίως σημαίνει πατὴρ ἁπαλός· καὶ εἰκότως· ἐνάπαλος γὰρ ὢν κατὰ τὴν ἡλικίαν, ὡς πατὴρ σωτήριον ἀρχὴν Αἰγυπτίοις ἐνεδείξατο. Δηλοῖ δὲ οὐδὲν ἡ λέξις, ἢ τὸ γονατίζειν. Cod. X cites: Ἀ. γονατίζει; Cod. 127, however: Ἀ. γονατίζειν, in agreement with the Graeco-Ven. translator, who renders γονυπετεῖν.

[36] Montef. from the Royal MSS. The spelling Ἀσαμφανὴ is in Cat. Niceph. p. 462, and Σαφαμφανὴ in Origen. Opp. T. II, p. 46. Procop. in Cat. Niceph. ibid.: Ψομθονφανὴχ (sic) ἑρμηνεύεται, κεκρυμμένα ἀνεκάλυψε· παρὰ δὲ τῷ Σύρῳ, ὁ εἰδὼς τὰ κρυπτά. Somewhat differently Origen. ibid.: Ψομθομφανὴχ, ὃ ἑρμηνεύεται, ᾧ ἀπεκαλύφθη τὸ μέλλον. Hieron.: "Although in Hebrew this name means _finder of hidden things_, yet, because it is given by an Egyptian, it must bear the sense of that language. In the Egyptian tongue, then, SAPHANETH PHANEE, or, as the LXX chose to render it, _Psomthom-Phanech_, is interpreted _saviour of the world_ [so in the text Cod. 75: ὅ ἐστιν σωτὴρ κόσμου], because he freed the world from the impending ruin of famine."
