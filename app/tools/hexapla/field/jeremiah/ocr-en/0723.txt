[32] “For τὴν σμίλακά σου the Syriac Hexapla has ܠܟܪܟܐ ܕܝܠܟܝ, _around thee_; but it seems to be an interpretation from another version.”—_J. D. Michaelis._ In another passage indeed (Nah. i. 10) for σμῖλαξ Syro-hex. gave ܣܡܝܠܐ (thus the MS., as Middeld. edited it), or, according to the emendation of Bernstein, ܣܡܝܠܐ[?]. But nothing prevents ܟܪܟܐ (thus in the MS.) also, from the root ܟܪܟ, _he wound round_, from denoting, among other meanings, the plant _smilax_, which in Latin is called _convolvulus_. Certainly the phrase ܠܟܪܟܐ ܕܝܠܟܝ for _around thee_ is altogether unusual with our translator.

[35] These words, ἀπὸ σοῦ ὁ Ἆπις, are wanting in Codd. 22, 48, 88, 96, and Syro-hex., as also in the Cod. Bavar. of Theodoret, and Chrysost. in Cat. Ghisler. T. II, p. 824. In Ald., Codd. III, XII, 33, 36, others, and the printed Theodoret, ἀπὸ σοῦ is wanting. Finally in Comp., Codd. 23, 26, 86, 233, is read: ἔφυγεν ὀπίσω ὁ μόσχος κ. τ. ἑ. It is commonly held that the LXX read in two words נָס חַף, ἔφυγεν ὁ Ἆπις, which Schleusner disapproves in the _Opusc. Crit._ p. 382, who thinks that ὁ Ἆπις is a marginal note of some copyist who wished to explain ὁ μόσχος ὁ ἐκλεκτός σου. Thus Theodoret.: μόσχον ἐκλεκτὸν τὸν Ἆπιν λέγει· βοῦς δὲ ἦν οὗτος, παρ᾽ αὐτῶν τρεφόμενος κ. τ. ἑ.
