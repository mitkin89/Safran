=== Blatt 0640 · S. 624 · JEREMIAS. [Cap. XX. 3–
--- Fußnoten
[7] Jerome: "For _terror_, which in the Hebrew is written MAGUR, the LXX and Theod. rendered μέτοικον, that is, _one who migrates_; Aquila's second edition _a stranger_ [perhaps πάροικον, or προσήλυτον], the first, _one looking around_; Sym. _taken away_, or _gathered together_ and _driven together_." The same on v. 4: "MAGUR [LXX μετοικίαν] all have likewise translated so as to signify either _terror_, or _sojourning_ [παροικίαν, or προσηλύτευσιν; cf. Hex. on Gen. xlvii. 9. Psal. cxviii. 54], or _removal_ and _transfer_, and _gathering together_ [MS. Vat. _migration together_]." Jerome's Latin is too uncertain for us to attach much weight to the Greek readings proposed by someone: Ἀ. 1. περιορῶντα. Ἀ. 2. ξένον. Σ. ἀφῃρημένον, ἢ συνηθροισμένον. We note only that for מָגוֹר in the same expression at Cap. xlvi. 5 Symmachus gave ܟܢܘܫܝܐ, "_a gathering_", for which we would rather put συστροφὴ than συναγωγὴ because of the passages Psal. xxx. 14. liv. 16 in the Hexapla. With Symmachus, as it seems, Jarchi agrees, who notes: "The noun מָגוֹר here signifies _gathering_ (אסיפה), as in Ezech. xxi. 17, _gathered_ (מְגוּרֵי) _to the sword_."
