[8] Jerome: "_And he shall be like the tamarisk_, which in Hebrew is called AROER, or, as Sym. rendered, _a fruitless tree._" Sym. put ἄκαρπον ξύλον for the Hebrew שִׁטָּה at Jesai. xli. 19. Cf. on Cap. xlviii. 6.

[10] The same: ܣ. ܒܥܪܒܐ ◆. "It seems to be the version not of Symmachus but of Theodotion, who is accustomed to render this little word thus, e.g. Job. xxxix. 6."—_Spohn._ Cf. our _Otium Norvic._ p. 35. For Symmachus עֲרָבָה is ἡ ἄβατος (Jesai. xxxv. 1. xl. 3), or ἡ ἀοίκητος (Job. xxxix. 6. Jerem. lii. 7).
[11] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܒܝܬ ܡܦܣܩܐ ◆. The Greek of the word ܡܦܣܩܐ (more correctly ܡܦܣ̈ܩܐ) is uncertain. As regards Aquila's usage, ἐν ψαθυροῖς, or ἐν τοῖς ἐψαθυρωμένοις (cf. Hex. on Psal. ci. 4), would not be bad; a notion, however, which the Syriac word seems scarcely to admit.
[12] The same: ܣ. ܒܝܒܝܫܘܬܐ ◆. Schleusner in the _Nov. Thes._ s. v. ἅλιμα gives from a MS. Glossary on Jerem.: Ἐν ἀλύμοις (write ἁλίμοις), ἐν ξηρότητι. Jerome too renders the Hebrew: "_in dryness._"
[13] The same: ܬ. ܒܦܩܥܬܐ[?] ◆. The word ܦܩܥܬܐ[?] is interchanged with χάος at Mich. i. 6. Zach. xiv. 4 in Syro-hex., and with χάσμα at Luc. xvi. 26 in Philox.

[18] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܓܒܪܐ ܕܝܢ ܡܢܘ ܗܘ ܕܢܕܥܝܗ̇ ◆. Jerome gives something different, and not even self-consistent: "_The heart of all is perverse and unsearchable; who shall know it?_ LXX: _The heart is deep above all things, and it is man; who shall know him?_ . . . The Hebrew word . . . if it be read ENOS, means _man_; but if ANUS, _unsearchable_, or _desperate_, because no one can find out the heart of men. But Sym. rendered this passage thus: _Unsearchable is the heart of all; but who is the man that shall find it out?_" From which someone has elicited: Σ. ἀνεξερεύνητος ἡ κ. πάντων, καὶ τίς ἀνὴρ ὃς εὑρήσει αὐτήν;
