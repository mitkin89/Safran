[13] Jerome: "_And we thought him to be unclean_, or _in pain_, as the LXX translated, for which Aq. and Sym. put _leprous_, Theod. _scourged_." The same a little after: "For that which Sym. translated, ἐν ἀφῇ ὄντα, that is, _in leprosy_, Aq. put ἀφημένον, that is, _leprous;_ which many, not understanding, think means _forsaken_ [ἀφειμένον], and others read καθήμενον, that is, _sitting_." Namely, from ἀφὴ, _touch, stroke_, especially of leprosy (Lev. xiii. 2), Aquila according to his custom invented the non-Greek verb ἀφάομαι, whence ἀφημένος (in the Ionic manner for ἡφημένος), _stricken with a plague;_ so that there is no need to take refuge with the latest Editor of Jerome in the Homeric ἀφήμενος (= ἄποθεν καθήμενος Hesych.), much less with Erasmus to change ἀφῇ and ἀφημένον into the non-existent words ἀλφῇ and ἀλφημένον.
