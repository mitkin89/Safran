CAP. XXII.
[1] Parsons. cites from Cod. XII: Ἀ. ὅραμα τισπιρίου (sic); in which reading, not mentioned by Curterius, there seems to lurk the word ὁραματισμοῦ, which is Aquila's regular rendering of חִזָּיוֹן. Cf. Hex. on Job. xxxiii. 15.
[2] Curter. Syro-hex. cites: ܬ. ܡܫܩܠܐ ܕܢܚܠܐ ܕܚܙܘܐ[?] ◆.
[3] Jerome p. 310: “In the Hebrew it has APHPHO, which we have rendered _now also_, and Aquila, wishing to preserve the Hebrew idiom, put καίπερ τοι.”
[4] Curter. cites from the margin of Cod. Jes.: ※ ἡ π. γαυριῶσα (Parsons. ἀγαυριῶσα). Codd. 22, 23, 36, 48, and others insert into the text: ἡ π. ἡ (ἡ om. Cod. 22) γαυριῶσα. Jerome: _the exulting city._ Finally Syro-hex. ܬ. ܡܕܝܢܬܐ ܫܒܗܪܬܐ[?] ◆.
[5] Thus Cod. XII (which has πολέμου in the text), according to Parsons. Curter. cites: Ἀ. Θ. πόλεμος; the rest as Parsons. Syro-hex. in the text, πολέμῳ (ܠܩܪܒܐ[?]. Thus Cod. C.; Cod. A., as Middeld., ܠܩܪ̈ܒܐ[?]); but in the margin: Ἀ. Θ. πολέμου (ܕܩܪܒܐ). Σ. ὁμοίως τοῖς Ο΄.
[6] Procop.
[7] Thus Codd. 48, 62, and others. Jerome p. 311: “_All thy princes fled together, and were harshly bound._ In this passage we have followed the LXX translators, because in sense it does not differ much from the Hebrew. But to translate word for word from the Hebrew, it reads thus among them: _All thy chief men have departed together, they have been bound by the bow;_ which edition the other translators also followed.”
[8] Curter. Codd. 22, 36, and others add the same after πεφεύγασι. Syro-hex. in the margin: ܬ. (Cod. C. ※ܬ) ܐܬܐܣܪܘ ܐܟܚܕܐ ◆.
[9] Curter.
[10] Thus Parsons. from Cod. XII, more correctly than Curter., who copied: Θ. συνεπιβάται ἀνθρώπων ἱππέων.
[11] Curter.
[12] “Thus one codex without the translator's name.”—_Montef._ Procopius, who erroneously reads ῥήματα, names Symmachus as the author, as Schleusner points out in _Opusc. Crit._ p. 342.
[13] Procop.: Ἐπειδὴ τὸ κατὰ τὴν πόλιν ὕδωρ μὴ νομίσαντες ἀρκέσειν αὐτοῖς ὡς δὴ πλείοσιν ὑπάρχουσιν Ἰουδαῖοι, ἐστοχάσαντο δὲ τὰς οἰκίας κατὰ Σύμμαχον ἀριθμήσαντες, τὸ τῆς ἀρχαίας κολυμβήθρας ὕδωρ εἰς τὴν πόλιν μετοχετεύσαντες, κ.τ.ἑ. From which Montef., following Drusius, unskilfully produced: Σ. καὶ οἰκίας Ἱερ. ἀριθμήσαντες. What we have printed is inserted in Codd. 22 (under ※, according to Grabe), 36, and others, before the LXX reading καὶ καθεῖλον (sic) τοὺς οἴκους Ἱερ.
[14] Curter. Then Montef., following Drusius, assigns to Symmachus the words of Procopius himself, καὶ τὸ τῆς ἀρχαίας — μετοχετεύσαντες.
[15] Syro-hex. ※ ܬ. ܡܢ ܪܘܚܩܐ ◆. Curter. cites: ※ πόρρωθε (sic). Cod. 48 in the text: ※ πόρρωθεν; and thus without asterisk Codd. 22, 23, 36, and others.
[16] Curter. Montef. printed: Σ. Θ., because Aquila always prefers στρατιῶν.
