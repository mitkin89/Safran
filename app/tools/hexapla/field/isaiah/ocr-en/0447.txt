CAP. I.
[1] Hieron.: “For what we have translated from the Hebrew, _over Judah and Jerusalem_, the LXX and Theod. rendered _against Judaea and Jerusalem._ Sym., in his usual manner more plainly, _concerning Judah and Jerusalem_, so that he wishes neither prosperity nor adversity to be indicated by the title.” The reading of Eusebius, Σ. ὑπὲρ τῆς Ἰουδαίας κ.τ.ἑ., rashly referred here by Montef., belongs to Cap. ii. 1.
[2] Thus silently Montef. Syro-hex. ܐ. ܣ. ܬ. ܒܝ̈ܘܡܬܐ[?] ܀
[3] “Thus the MS. of the RR. PP. Jesuits is accustomed to annotate in the margin at the word κύριος, namely when יהוה is read in the Hebrew. In this way the Hebrew letters of the name τετραγράμμου are represented.”—_Montef._ Our Syrian renders the Greek ΠΙΠΙ throughout this book by ܝܗܝܗ, but in other passages outside Isaiah (on which see Ceriani in _Monum. S. et P._, T. II, p. 110) by ܦܝܦܝ, once (Exod. xxix. 11) with the Greek written beside it, sometimes with ܡ[?] or ܡܪ[?] prefixed. The error of a double Ι for different Hebrew letters seems to have arisen from the fact that in MSS. the letter Yod is sometimes so drawn out that it is almost equal to τῷ Vav. The Syrian translator, therefore, who put ܦܝܦܝ, faithfully imitated the Greek writing; but he who changed it into ܝܗܝܗ was indeed skilled in Hebrew letters, but either did not know the true spelling of the name יהוה (which is Ceriani's opinion), or was unwilling to correct the error of his archetype.—“Codex C. is mutilated here, but it too always has ܝܗܝܗ, except Cap. lv. 7, where Cod. A. has in the margin first ܀ ܡܪܝܐ[?] ܀, then ܀ ܝܗܝܗ ܀, Cod. C. in the margin ܀ ܦܝܦܝ .ܡܪܝܐ[?]; so that it appears that he corrected elsewhere, when ܦܝܦܝ occurred by itself, but here, where it had merged into one reading with the preceding word, left it untouched. Moreover, this codex very often places beneath the reading ܝܗܝܗ the Greek letters ΗΕΗΕ (sic), Η being taken for Ι, as also happened in the system of Syriac vowels derived from the Greek, because of the prevailing pronunciation of the Greek Η.”—_Ceriani._ To save paper, let it suffice to have noted here once and for all the constant usage both of Cod. Jes. and of the translator of Syro-hex.
