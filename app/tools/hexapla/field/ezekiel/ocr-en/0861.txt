[93] Cod. Jes., according to Parsons. Montef. cites from the same: ※ Θ. καὶ λήψονται (sic) — θρήνημά σοι: then from Drusius [Nobil.]: Ἄλλ. τίς — θαλάσσης; In Cod. 87 the passage reads thus: λήψονται ἐπὶ σὲ ÷ οἱ υἱοὶ αὐτῶν ↙ θρῆνον, θρήνημά σοι· ※ τίς — θαλάσσης ↙. Syro-hex. has in one continuous stretch: ※ καὶ φαλακρώσουσιν — καὶ κ. πικρὸν λήψονται ἐπὶ σὲ οἱ υἱοὶ αὐτῶν θρῆνον, θρήνημα Σόρ (ܕܨܘܪ)· τίς ὥσπερ Τύρος (ܨܘܪ) κατασιγηθεῖσα (ܕܐܫܬܠܝܬ[?]) ἐν μέσῳ θαλάσσης ↙; Finally Hieron.: “_What is like Tyre, which has become dumb_ (or _has fallen silent_) _in the midst of the sea?_ Which is not found in the LXX.” It is indeed found, besides the witnesses already mentioned, in Comp. (with τῆς θαλ.), Ald., Codd. III, 22, 23 (as Comp.), 26 (with ὡς Τ.), and others, and Theodoret. (as Comp.).
[94] “Barb. in the margin. To which Hebrew word it refers I cannot guess, unless perhaps to קִינָה, but Aquila would be far off.”—_De Reg._ Parsons. cites at θρῆνον: “Ἀ. ενιαυτου. Σ. εν μελει 88. Thus, unless ενιαυτον 86.” I have no doubt that the reading of Symmachus belongs not to קִינָה, but to בְּנִיהֶם, comparing Hex. on Jerem. ix. 9, 19. Amos v. 16. As for Aquila's manifestly faulty spelling ενιαυτων, I do not know whether it is permissible to resolve it into ἐν ἰὰ (whence ἰασμὸς, _a mournful song_, Hex. on Jerem. xxv. 30) αὐτῶν, so that it may be referred to the same Hebrew.
