=== Blatt 0784 · S. [768] · EZECHIEL.
--- Fußnoten
CAP. I.
[1] Syro-hex. marks: ܘܐܢܐ ÷ ܐܝܬܝ ↙ ܗܘܝܬ.
[2] The same: ܣ. ܟܕ ܐܝܬܝ ܒܡܫܢܝܘܬܐ[?] ◆. Cf. on Cap. iii. 11.
[3] Codd. 86, 88. De Reg. from Cod. 86 gives: Οἱ λοιποί· ὀπτασίαν ἢ ὅρασιν.
[4] Hieron.: “And more significantly, according to the Hebrews and the other translators, it is called the _transmigration_ of Joachin, and not the _captivity_, as the LXX translated. For he was not captured when the city was overcome, but, surrendering himself voluntarily, was led to Babylon.”
[5] Thus Codd. XII (in the margin), 87, 88, Syro-hex., and without the asterisk Cod. 62.
[6] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܚܙܐ ܗܘܝܬ ܕܝܢ ܐܝܟ ܢܫܡܬܐ ܕܡܬܥܠܥܠܐ[?] ◆. Besides, in the text the Syrian reads and marks: πνεῦμα ※ κυρίου ↙ ἐξαῖρον, with the note: Ὁ Ἑβραῖος· κυρίου.
[7] Cod. 86, according to De Reg., who uselessly corrects Aquila to πν. καταιγίδος. Cf. Hex. ad Psal. cxlviii. 8. The reading of Symmachus is confirmed by Syro-hex., and Theodoret. Montef. indeed, after Drusius, edited from the latter: Σ. πνεῦμα καταιγίζον; but Theodoret only states: τοῦτο γὰρ σημαίνει τὸ, πν. ἐξαῖρον ἤρχετο ἀπὸ βορρᾶ, ἢ ὡς Σύμ. ἔφη, καταιγίζον. The same readings of the three translators Parsons copied from Cod. 88, who nevertheless assigns entirely different ones to Cod. 86, of which the other is a mere copy.
[8] Cod. 86, as copied by Parsons, with whom Jerome seems to agree in every respect: “And first it must be known that the _spirit carrying away_, or _lifting up_, which we, following Aquila, have translated _wind of the whirlwind_ [Montef. translates ἄνεμος λαίλαπος], and according to Sym. and Theod. _blast_ and _spirit of the tempest_, is taken by some in a good sense, by others in the contrary sense.” But, to say what I think, Parsons' Greek words are not genuine, much less copied from Cod. Barber., but mere Latin of Jerome, endowed with Greek by a certain person so often praised. Certainly L. Bos mentions these very readings without an authority, on the passage.
[9] Cod. 86, according to De Reg. Parsons from Cod. 86 gives: Ἀ. ὀφθαλμός. Θ. εἶδος. Ο΄. ὁμοίωμα [thus Codd. III, 26, 233]; but from the other codex: Ἀ. ὀφθαλμός. Σ. εἶδος.
[10] Hieron.: “And what follows, _and brightness in it_, is to be marked beforehand with an obelus.”
[11] “The Royal cotton-paper MS., and likewise the Coislin. from Polychronius.”—_Montef._
