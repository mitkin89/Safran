[21] Cod. Jes. in the margin: Ὁ Ἑβραῖος ἀντὶ τῆς διεκβολῆς, τῆς ἁλμυρᾶς τῆς θ. ἔχει. For הַמּוּצָאִים, which has some obscurity, the reading חֲמוּצִים seems to be indicated, from the root חָמֵץ, _was sour, salty._
[22] Thus Syro-hex. Codd. 87, 88 read and mark: ἂν ἐπέλθῃ ※ ἐκεῖ ↙ ὁ ποταμός. The word ἐκεῖ is wanting in Ald., Codd. 49, 68, and others.
[23] Thus Codd. 22, 23, 51, and others, and Syro-hex., which in the margin, char. med., cites: ἐκεῖ.
[24] Thus Syro-hex. (unless perhaps it should be marked: ÷ πολλοί ↙), and without the asterisk Codd. 22, 23, 36, and others, and Theodoret. Next, at ψυγμὸς σαγηνῶν ἔσται Syro-hex. in the margin, char. med.: ψυγμοὶ σαγηνῶν ἔσονται.
[25] The obelus is in Cod. 87, Syro-hex. Next, at ὑγιάσωσιν Syro-hex. in the margin, char. med.: ἁγιάσωσιν (ܢܩܕܫܘܢ), which spelling exists in Codd. III, 49, 68, and others.
[26] Thus for οὐ μὴ ὑγιάσωσιν Codd. 22 (with κατέναντι instead of τὰ τενάγη), 23 (with ἰαθῇ), 36, and others, and Theodoret., from a double version of the word גְּבָאָיו.
[27] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܟܕ ܕܝܢ ܢܦܩܘܢ ܠܗܠܝܢ ܕܩܪܝܒܢ. ܠܐ ܢܬܐܣܘܢ ◆.
[28] The same: ܢܨܝܒ[?] ܗܘܐ ◆. See Bernstein in _Lex. ad Chrestom. Kirsch._ p. 232. It would be excessively rash to attribute to Symmachus the Pythagorean word ἐμπεφυσιώμενον ἦν.
[29] Hieron.: "_Every fruit-bearing tree,_ or, as all translated with one voice, βρώσιμον."
[30] Codd. 22, 23 (with οὗ instead of ὃ), 36, and others, in the text: ὃ οὐκ ἀπορυήσεται (sic) τὸ φ. αὐτοῦ, καὶ οὐ μὴ παλαιωθῇ ἐπ᾽ αὐτοῦ, from a double version. Cf. Psal. i. 3 in the LXX.
[31] Cod. Jes. The pronoun is wanting in Ald., Codd. 26, 49, and others.
[32] The double version, τῆς καινότητος αὐτοῦ εἰς τοὺς μ. αὐτοῦ, is in Codd. 22, 23, 36, and others, and Theodoret. Cod. Jes. in the margin: Ἐν ἄλλοις· εἰς τοὺς μ. αὐτοῦ πρωτογεννήσει. (For πρωτοβολήσει, πρωτογενήσει (sic) is in Codd. 23, 51; πρωτοβολη γενήσει (sic) in Codd. 22, 48.)
[33] Thus Codd. 22, 23, 36, and others, and Syro-hex. (ܡܣܩܬܐ[?]; cf. Hex. on Job. xiv. 14). The common reading is supported by Hieron.: "For _leaves,_ which in Hebrew are called ALE . . . the LXX translated _ascent._"
[34] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܝܘܣܦ ܚܒܠܐ ܦܫܝܛܐ ◆. Cod. 23 alone inserts before πρόσθεσις: Ἰωσὴφ διπλοῦν σχοίνισμα. Hieron.: "For what we have said, _for Joseph has a double cord,_ the LXX translated, _an increase of the cord._"
[35] Thus Ald., Codd. 49, 68, 87, 88, and others. Syro-hex. ܨܕܕ[?].
[36] Thus Ald., Codd. XII (in the margin), 49, 68, 87, 88, and others. Syro-hex. ܚܡܬ. ܒܪܘܬ. ܣܒܪܘܝܡ[?]. The same cites in the margin, char. med.: ܐܠܕܡܐ[?]. ܠܬܝܪܐ[?]. ܐܦܪܝܡ. ܐܠܝܡ ◆, to which the reading of Codd. 22, 23, 36, and others comes very close: Μαὰβ (or Μαὰμ), Θηιρὰς (or ὁ Ἡιρὰς), Ἐφραῒμ, Ἠλιάμ. (In the word ܐܠܕܡܐ[?] I should believe that the first syllable ܐܠ[?], variously attempted by Bernstein in the _Zeitschrift_ etc. T. III, p. 410, is a trace of the spelling Ἐλδαμαὰμ, which Codd. 22, 36, 48 exhibit.)
