=== Blatt 0820 · S. 804 · EZECHIEL. [Cap. XVI. 10–
--- Fußnoten
[15] (Forts.) _to clothe someone with_.)
[16] Hieron.: “LXX: _And I girded thee about with trichapton;_ for which Aq. _flowered_, or _palpable;_ Sym. _with a garment;_ Theod. set down the Hebrew word itself, MESSE.” And soon after: “According to the translation of the second edition of Aquila, who rendered _flowered garment_.” Nobil. and Coislin. in Montef., both from Origen: τρίχαπτον ἡρμήνευσαν οἱ ἄλλοι ἑρμηνευταὶ, ἄνθιμον, καὶ ἐπένδυμα. The same Coislin. cites a scholion: Ἀκύλας· τρίχαπτόν ἐστι τὸ ψηλαφητόν.
[17] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܘܟܣܝܬܟܝ ܒܟܣܝܬܐ ◆. Had not Origen stood for ἐπενδύματι, I would have rendered it in Greek: Σ. καὶ περιέβαλόν σε περιβολαίῳ.
[18] Hieron.: “For _necklace_, which we translated according to the second edition of Aquila and Symmachus, the LXX and Theod. rendered κάθεμα.” Montef. edited from Hieronymus: Ἀ. 2. Σ. κλοιόν; as regards Aquila, perhaps rightly; but that Symmachus rendered otherwise is attested by our Syrian, who cites: ܣ. ܡܚܙܘܕܐ[?] ◆. To this seems to belong Montefalcon's hexaplaric reading, wrongly placed at מֶשִׁי (v. 10): Ἄλλ. περιδέρραιον. More correctly Cod. 238 notes in the margin at κάθεμα: περιδέρραιον.
[19] Hieron.: “The Hebrew word NEZEM, except Symmachus, who translated ἐπιρρίνιον, all rendered _earring_.”
[20] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܥܠ ܢܚܝܪܐ ܕܝܠܟܝ ◆. Thus in the text Codd. III, 22, 23, 36, and others.
[21] Hieron.: “We translated _beauty_ according to Symmachus because of the elegance of the sense. Otherwise both in the Hebrew THOPHERTH and in the other translators καύχησις, _glorying_, is said.” On Sym. cf. Hex. on Psal. lxx. 8. Ezech. xxiii. 26.
[22] Hieron.: “Here too Theod. set down the Hebrew word MESI itself, as above, Aq. ἄνθιμον, Sym. _polymitum_ [woven of many threads]. And what we called _fine_ above, here we called _polymitum_ according to Symmachus.” Our Syrian, unless I am mistaken, more correctly refers the reading of Symmachus to the following וְרִקְמָה.
[23] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܘܢܚܬ̈ܐ[?] ܡܨܝ̈ܪܐ ◆. Cf. on Cap. xxvii. 16, 24.
[24] Cod. Jes. Thus without the asterisk Codd. 23, 26, 36, and others, and Syro-hex.
[25] Thus in the text Codd. 87, 88, and without the asterisk Codd. 22, 23, 36, and others. Syro-hex. marks: καὶ κατ. ※ εἰς βασιλείαν ↙. Hieron.: “_And thou didst advance to a kingdom._ This is not found in the LXX, who perhaps, translating the Scriptures at Alexandria from the Hebrew truth into the Greek tongue, feared to publish this, lest they should seem to offend the king of Egypt.”
[26] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܘܐܬܛܝܒܬܝ[?] ◆. See Prov. vi. 8 in the LXX and Syro-hex. The Syriac word is also commonly interchanged with προκόπτειν, but the other seems more suitable here. Cf. Hex. on Dan. iii. 30.
[27] Hieron. (In the text the double reading ἐν τῇ ὡραιότητι is wanting in Codd. XII, 87, 88, and Syro-hex. Hieron.: _in beauty._ Comp., Codd. 22, 23, 36, and others: ἐν εὐπρεπείᾳ ὡραιότητος.)
[28] Thus Cod. XII, Syro-hex. (ܠܗ ܗܘܝܬܝ ܗܘܝܬ[?]).
[29] Thus Comp., Codd. 87, 88 (with ἐγένετο).
[30] Thus Codd. III, 26, 49, 90.
[31] Thus Ald., Codd. 42, 68, and others. Finally in Codd. 22, 23, 36, and others there is a double reading: αὐτῷ ἐγίνετο (or ἐγένετο), καὶ (or ὃ) οὐκ ἔσται; which Theodoret. explains thus: τουτέστι, συνήπτου ἑτέροις, οὐδαμόθεν αὐτοῖς ἁρμόττουσα.
