[30] Cod. 86, according to De Reg., gives: Ἀ. ἆ ἆ ἆ σχετλίασον (sic). Hieron.: "For which Aq. translated ἆ ἆ; Sym. σχετλίασον, which means _lament_, or _wail;_ the LXX and Theod. εὖγε, which belongs rather to one who exults than to one who wails." Whence Montef. attributes σχετλίασον to Symmachus for the Hebrew אָח, wrongly; for σχετλιάζειν is _to cry out in complaint_, or _to say, alas!_ Syro-hex. on καὶ εἰπόν· εὖγε (thus once) gives: Σ. καὶ σχετλίασον (ܘܐܬܓܥܣ[?]); with which agrees Origen, cited by Nobilius: τὸ, καὶ εἰπὸν, εὖγε, παραφράζων ὁ Σύμ. πεποίηκε, καὶ σχετλίασον. (The passage is Opp. T. III, p. 417, where in the editions it is read less well: τὸ δὲ, καὶ εἶπον (sic), εὖγε, παρ. ὁ Σ. πεποίηκεν, ἐπένθησα καὶ ἐσχετλίασα.)

[42] "Barb. [Cod. 86] gives in the margin: Ἀ. τερεβίντῳ ἰθαρατόπῳ (sic)."—_De Reg._ Somewhat differently Parsons copied from Codd. 86, 88: Ἀ. τερεβινθω. Σ. θαρατοπω. Where, whatever be decided about the latter reading, τερεβίνθου certainly seems to be claimed for Aquila, as the same translator rendered at least once at Hos. iv. 13, where the reading of the Hexapla, called into doubt by Gesenius in the _Thes. Ling. Hebr._ p. 50, is confirmed by Syro-hex. Then for ἰθαρατόπῳ Drach attempts ἰθαρῷ (= καθαρῷ) τόπῳ, wrongly; for there can hardly be a doubt that ἰθαρᾷ, whatever sense it may bear, pertains to τερεβίνθῳ, and τόπῳ to the following מְקוֹם. Not far from Parsons' writing is σκιαρᾷ, which, however, I should not readily believe Aquila put in place of his constant, and indeed more accurate, rendering of the Hebrew word, δασείᾳ (cf. on Cap. xix. 11. xx. 28. xxxi. 3).
