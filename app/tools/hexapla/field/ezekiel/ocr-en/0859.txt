De Reg. cites from Cod. 86: Σ. ἀπηνεγμένα (sic), which reading seems capable of being reconciled with the passive participle ܕܐܡ̣ܛܝܢ[?] (not with the active ܕܐܡܛܝܢ[?], as Norberg falsely copied). (To the examples of this form adduced by Bernstein and Payne Smith I add, besides this passage, Jerem. xxxi. 21: ܐܙܠܬܝ[?] (not, as Norberg, ܐܙܠܬܝ̇[?]), πεπόρευσαι. One may compare ܐܬܝܐ[?] of the same form for ἧκται, Jesai. xxiii. 21, where I fear I have accused our Syrian of error in vain.) But if we attribute a middle force to the Greek ἀπηνεγμένοι (as in the passage Esth. iii. 13: καὶ δεύτερον τῶν βασιλειῶν γέρας ἀπενηνεγμένος), we shall have Hieronymus in agreement, who says: “For _Mozel_ too Sym. rendered _bringing_.”
