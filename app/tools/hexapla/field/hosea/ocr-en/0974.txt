=== Blatt 0974 · S. 958 · HOSEAS. [Cap. XI. 3–
--- Fußnoten
[1] (Forts.) ἐξαπλῶν; and at the end, with a scholion added which seems to concern Aquila alone: Τούτῳ ἐχρήσατο ὁ Ματθαῖος, ὡς οὕτως ἔχοντος δηλονότι τοῦ Ἑβραϊκοῦ ὡς καὶ ὁ Ἀ. ἡρμήνευσε. Euseb. in Eclog. Prophet. p. 112 continues for Aquila: ὅτι παῖς Ἰσραὴλ, καὶ ἠγάπησα αὐτὸν, καὶ ἀπὸ Αἰγύπτου ἐκάλεσα τὸν υἱόν μου. Syro-hex. cites: ܐ. ܘܡܢ ܡܨܪܝܢ ܩܪܝܬ ܠܒܪܐ ܕܝܠܝ ܀. But whether its attributing to Symmachus: εἰς τὸν υἱόν μου (ܣ. ܠܒܪܐ ܕܝܠܝ ܀) is to be imputed to the scribe, writing ܠܒܪܐ for ܒܪܐ, I do not know. (In the reading τῶν Ο΄, διότι for ὅτι is supported by Comp., Ald., Codd. III, 22, 23, 26, and others; but the copula before ἐγὼ seems to be missing by accident. Syro-hex. writes: ÷ καὶ ἐγὼ ↙ ἠγάπησα.)
[2] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܡܕܒܪܢܐ ܗܘܝܬ ܀ ܬ. ܒܡܪܕܝܬܐ[?] ܀. On Theod. cf. Jesai. xli. 2 in LXX and Syro-hex. Here, moreover, a very remarkable passage of S. Cyril. in XII Proph. p. 158 E must be brought to light: Εἰδέναι δὲ ἀναγκαῖον ὅτι τῶν Ἑβραίων ἡ ἔκδοσις, καὶ μὴν καὶ ἡ τῶν ἑτέρων, οὐκ ἔχει τὸ, συνεπόδισα· τεθείκασι δὲ μᾶλλον τὸ, γέγονα ὡς τιθηνὸς τῷ Ἐφραΐμ. Here the Commentator either expressed the sense of the three translators in general terms, or, as I think more likely, gave the version of one translator in so many words. That this one is Symmachus is shown, besides the more elegant style, by Jerome's own version (which we have often observed to be conformed to that of Symmachus above the others): _And I was as a foster-father to Ephraim._ The undoubted reading of our Syrian, Σ. ἐπαιδαγώγουν, does indeed stand in the way; but nothing prevents our referring it to another recension of Symmachus.

[11] Hieron.: “_The sword_ therefore _began in his cities_, or _will rush in_, as Aq. rendered, or _will wound_, as Sym. translated.” Syro-hex. cites: ܐ. ܢܦܠ ܥܠ ܬܫܘܝܬܐ[?] ܀; on which Middeld.: “Ἀ. ἔπεσεν ἐπὶ τὴν στρωμνήν. The Syr. phrase can be explained by a similar one, ܢܦܠ ܥܠ ܥܪܣܐ, _he fell upon the bed_ [ἔπεσεν ἐπὶ τὴν κοίτην], for _he became sick_, 1 Macc. i. 6 . . . The Syr. circumlocution is quite foreign to the character of Aquila.” Not foreign to the character of Aquila would be ἐστρωματίσθη, _he was covered with coverlets_, which perhaps the Syrian translated by periphrasis. But against Jerome I determine nothing.
[12] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܘܢܫܡܠܐ ܠܕܪ̈ܥܐ ܕܝܠܗ. Hieron.: “_And it shall consume his chosen ones_, or _his arms_, as Sym. rendered, which in Hebrew is BADDAU.”
