[45] Cod. 243 in the text: β̣δ̣ε̣λυγμός (sic); but in the margin: βδελυγμός. Ἀ. Θ. τὸ ἔσω (cf. on v. 1), i.e. λυγμός. Cod. 108 cites: Ἀ. λυγμός. Procop. p. 81: καὶ οὐκ ἔσται σοι βδελυγμός. Ἀκύλας· λυγμός· αἰνίττεται δὲ τὰς τοῦ συνειδότος ἀκίδας. Theodoret. ibid. has the same, with the reading νυγμὸς, which alone seems to suit ἀκίδας. But that it is false is shown both by the agreement of Codd. 56, 71, 119, 246 (with θρῆνος above the line), all of which have λυγμὸς in the text, and by the cognate forms قَاقَ, “_he sobbed_,” and ܦܘܥܡܐ[?], “_yawning._” Hieron. too translated “_into sobbing._”

Cf. on v. 26. It seems altogether to be an error, not of the scribes who handed down to us the text of the Seventy, which here at any rate is above all suspicion, but of those who out of the readings μὴ σώζειν and μὴ σῶσαι hammered out the words of doubtful authority μυσάζειν and μυσῶσαι, and marked them with Aquila's name.
