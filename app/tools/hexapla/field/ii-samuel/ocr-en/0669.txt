CAP. VIII.
[1] Cod. 243. The word κολάζειν, suspected by Schleusner without reason, means not only “_to punish_,” but “_to restrain, to check_,” e.g. the arrogant, a sense which agrees excellently with the meaning of the Hebrew word. As regards Symmachus's version, however, Bar Hebraeus rises up against it, noting: ܥܒܪܝܐ ܐܪܟܢ ܐܢܘܢ. ܣ̄ܡ ܐܪܡܝ.[?]; i.e. Ὁ Ἑβραῖος· ἀνέκλινεν (or ἐκοίμισεν) αὐτούς. Σ. κατέβαλεν. It must therefore be considered whether the other reading should not be claimed for Theodotion, who put καταισχυνεῖς for תַּכְנִיעַ in Jesai. xxv. 4.
[2] Cod. Reg. Procop. p. 125: Ἀκ. δὲ τὴν ἀφωρισμένην χαλινὸν ὑδραγωγίου ἐξέδωκεν. After the readings of Aquila and Symmachus to be mentioned presently, Cod. 243 adds: τόπος ἐστίν. ὁ δὲ Ἀκ. φησὶ, τὸν χαλινὸν τοῦ ὑδραγωγοῦ. On אַמָּה, ὑδραγωγὸς (a usage which belongs to Syriac and to later Hebrew), cf. on Cap. ii. 24.
