Montef. copied from the Regius: Ἀ. καὶ ἔλαβε Δαυὶδ τοὺς κλοιοὺς χρυσοῦς [from Theodoret, as it seems, who in Quaest. XXII on 2 Reg., p. 420 says: τοὺς μέντοι χλιδῶνας τῶν Σύρων τοὺς χρυσοῦς, κλοιοὺς ὁ Ἀκ. ἡρμήνευσεν· Ἐμὰθ δὲ τὴν Ἐπιφάνειαν τῆς Συρίας]. Ἄλλος· βραχιάρια χρυσᾶ; noting: “Here I would have you observe that in this place three different versions of Aquila are cited for the word שִׁלְטֵי, namely κλοιοὺς, βραχιάρια, and πανοπλίας. The two last the same translator uses elsewhere for the same word, as you may see in the _Greek Lexicon_ [pp. 566, 610], but the first nowhere else.” As regards βραχιάριον, it is ascribed to Aquila only once, and that _not_ for the same Hebrew, in the passage 2 Reg. i. 10, where we have already observed that the reading is suspect. The other word, πανοπλία, is put for the same Hebrew in 4 Reg. xi. 10, but the translator is Symmachus, not Aquila; and in the passage Psal. xc. 14, where the Hexapla have: Ἀ. ὡς πανοπλία καὶ περιφέρεια ἡ ἀλήθεια αὐτοῦ, not only is the Hebrew word צִנָּה, not שֶׁלֶט, but the particle ὡς, not found in the Hebrew, betrays the hand of Symmachus. Montfaucon's remark is therefore without foundation. Let us now appeal to Scharfenberg, in whose judgement the reading βραχιάρια is to be referred, as a gloss, to the Septuagint rendering τοὺς χλιδῶνας, according to the gloss of Phavorinus: Βραχιάλια, ἃ παρ᾽ ἡμῖν χλιδῶνες καλοῦνται; while what is ascribed to Aquila and Symmachus in Cod. 243, πανοπλίας and φαρέτρας χρυσᾶς, are the words of Josephus, Antiq. VII, 5, 3: καὶ τάς τε χρυσᾶς φαρέτρας καὶ τὰς πανοπλίας, ἃς οἱ τοῦ Ἀδάδου σωματοφύλακες ἐφόρουν, ἀνέθηκε τῷ θεῷ εἰς Ἱεροσόλυμα. So far the learned Animadversor, with whom we agree to the extent of rejecting βραχιάρια as a gloss, as we already rejected it on Cap. i. 10; but as regards the other reading, we are compelled to hold it for the genuine version of Aquila and Symmachus, besides the authority of the Coislin MS., by another most weighty witness, Bar Hebraeus, from whose unpublished commentary Parsons, doing I know not what, cited: Aq. “_bracelets;_” whereas in reality a threefold reading is preserved in it: ܫ̄ܒ ܡܘܢܟ̈ܐ. ܐ̄ܩ ܙܝܢܐ. ܣ̄ܡ ܡܛܪ̈ܬܐ[?].; i.e. Ο΄. χλιδῶνας. Ἀ. “_arms._” Σ. φαρέτρας. Here, as regards Aquila, there is nothing about _bracelets_, nothing about _necklaces;_ although, we admit, the word ܙܝܢܐ according to our Syrian's usage means not so much πανοπλία (ܡܐܢ̈ܝ ܙܝܢܐ[?] Luc. xi. 22 in Philox.) as ὅπλα. There remains Theodoret's reading κλοιοὺς, which itself too, according to the scholium of Cod. 243, may be a gloss on the word χλιδῶνας; but if anyone should prefer to refer it to Aquila's second edition, he will not find us strongly opposed. Lastly Montef., from the Regius as it seems, published: Ἄλλος· τὰ ὅπλα τὰ χρυσᾶ καὶ τὰ δόρατα, words which after Σουβὰ are inserted into the text by Codd. 19 (with καὶ πάντα τὰ ὅ. τὰ χρ. καὶ δόρατα), 82 (with καὶ πάντα τὰ ὅ. τὰ χρ. καὶ τὰ δ.), 93 (the same), 108 (as 19), 158 (with καὶ τὰ ὅ. τὰ χρ. καὶ τὰ δ.).
