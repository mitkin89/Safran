[53] Syro-hex. gives: ܗ. ܕܪܘܓܐ܀ A word unknown to the lexicographers. “So indeed the codex too. The word occurs in Adler's _Vers. Syr._ p. 122, on Luc. xxii. 52, where ܡܬܗܐ[?] of the text is explained: ܡܬܗܐ[?]: ܦܠܛ̈ܐ[?] ܕܪܘܓܐ ܕܩ̈ܝܣܐ[?]. The word ܦܠܛܐ[?] occurs in Jesai. xxx. 17, ὡς ἱστὸς (ܦܠܛܐ[?]) ἐπ’ ὄρους, where there is a scholium in the margin: ܦܠܛܐ. ܗܘ ܕܝܢ ܕܘܠܐ[?]܀ Cf. also White in _Vers. Philox._ T. I, p. 620, where ܡܬܗܐ[?] is explained by ܦܠܛ̈ܐ[?] ܕܪܘܓܐ. I add that in the Rich codex of the British Museum, fol. 128 or 129, in an explanation of passages of the Peshitto, there is: ܩܪ̈ܝܬܐ[?] ܕܡܛܠ̈ܬܢ[?] (Cant. Cant. i. 17): ܕܪ̈ܘܓܐ ܕܡܛܠܠ̈ܐ[?].”—_Ceriani._ This more than suffices to defend the little word against Middeldorpf, who says: “It seems to be the Hebr. בְּרוֹתִים, with בּ omitted, which the translator thought had been wrongly prefixed.” As for the Greek word, Parsons copied from Cod. 252: Σ. αἱ φατνώσεις. οἱ στρωτερες (sic). βόρατα; the last of which is manifestly to be referred to the Hebr. בְּרוֹתִים. There remains the reading οἱ στρωτῆρες, which, according to Suidas, are τὰ μικρὰ δοκίδια τὰ ἐπάνω τῶν δοκῶν τιθέμενα. Cf. Vales. on Harpocrat. p. 176. Why then should we not believe that this reading, lying as it were in the middle, should be claimed for the Fifth edition, and that it lies hidden under the Syriac word of related meaning ܕܪܘܓܐ?
