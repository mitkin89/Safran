[41] “Sym. renders thus, without regard to the division which is in the Hebrew: “_And in the excellence of his beauty_ [perhaps ܘܒܡܝܬܪܘܬܐ[?] ܫܘܦܪܗ]. _In the clouds is the dwelling of God from the beginning, and from beneath the arms of the world._” —_Masius._ On ἀρχὴ Cod. 85 in the margin: ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς. Next, the words “_and from beneath the arms of the world_” have been wrongly translated, unless I am mistaken, from the Syriac ܡܢ ܠܬܚܬ ܕܪ̈ܥܐ ܕܥܠܡܐ, i.e. καὶ ὑποκάτωθεν βραχίονες αἰώνιοι, a version which Procop. in Cat. Niceph. p. 1669 quotes and explains without naming its author. Lastly, to the remains of Symmachus appear to belong the words found in the same Procopius a little earlier: οὗ καὶ οἴκησιν τὸν αἰθέρα [so everywhere for שְׁחָקִים Sym. in the Hexapla] λέγει διὰ τὴν καθαρότητα, ὑπεράνω δυνάμεων ἁπασῶν, ἃς λέγει βραχίονας αἰωνίους ὑποκειμένας θεῷ. Moreover, for ܒܐܐܪ Masius translated too generally “_in the clouds._”
[42] Anon. in Cat. Niceph. p. 1669. It is without doubt by Symmachus, with whom לָבֶטַח is rendered ἄφοβος, Psal. lxxvii. 53. Ezech. xxxiv. 27.
