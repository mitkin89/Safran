=== Blatt 1023 · S. 1007 · –Cap. III. 3.] HABACUC.

CAP. III.
[1] Hieron.: “Aq. and Sym. and the Fifth ed., like us, rendered _for ignorances;_ Theod. alone ὑπὲρ τῶν ἑκουσιασμῶν, that is, _for voluntary sins,_ and _for those who transgress of their own accord._ This [I say] in order that we may understand that, apart from the LXX, no one rendered _a prayer with a song._ For in the Hebrew too it has AL SEGIONOTH, which is said ἐπὶ ἀγνοημάτων, and we rendered, _for ignorances._”
[2] Thus in the text Codd. 62, 86, 147, which throughout this whole song agree amicably with one another. “In the Barberini Cod. another version of the Song of Habakkuk is adduced, with this note at the end: Τὴν ᾠδὴν τοῦ Ἀμβακοὺμ οὐχ εὗρον συμφωνοῦσαν οὔτε τοῖς Ο΄, οὔτε Ἀκύλᾳ, οὔτε Συμμάχῳ, οὔτε Θεοδοτίωνι. Ζητήσεις οὖν εἰ τῆς Ε΄ ἢ τῆς S΄ ἐκδόσεώς ἐστι. Nay, it agrees neither with the Fifth nor with the Sixth, as is clear from their passages adduced above: wherefore, as that of an uncertain translator, it is always placed last, with the word Ἄλλος prefixed, throughout the whole song. That it is the Seventh edition there is scarcely reason for us to doubt.”—_Montef._
[3] Thus Comp., Ald., Codd. 22, 36, 42, and others, Hieron., Syro-hex.
[4] Hieron.: “But what the LXX said, _I considered and was amazed,_ is found neither in the Hebrew nor in any of the other translators, so that, when what is not found in the Hebrew has been removed, it can be read according to the LXX: _O Lord, thy works; in the midst of two living creatures thou shalt be known:_ and because this seemed ἀδιανόητον, the preceding words were joined to it.”
[5] Euseb. in Dem. Evang. p. 279 (who directs ζωῶν to be written in the text of the LXX, against Hieron. and Syro-hex.). Syro-hex. cites: Ἀ. Θ. ζώωσον αὐτόν (ܐܚܝܝܗܝ). Σ. ἀναζώωσον αὐτόν (ܐܚܝܝܗܝ ܡܢ ܕܪܝܫ[?]). Hieron.: “For what we and Aq. and Theod. rendered, _quicken it,_ Sym. rendered, _quicken it again._”
[6] The obelus is in Syro-hex. Hieron.: “There follows: _When the time shall come, thou shalt be shown; when my soul shall be troubled;_ which, apart from the LXX, is found neither in the Hebrew nor in any of the other translators . . . But what is added, _when my soul shall be troubled,_ is joined in the edition of the LXX translators with what follows, so that it can be read: _When my soul shall be troubled in wrath,_ and the division [falls] here: then let there follow, _thou wilt remember mercy._”
[7] Thus Codd. 62, 86 (with λοιβὸς), 147.
[8] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܣ. ܬ. ܕܦܐܪܢ ܀ In the text Φαρὰν is rejected by Ald., Codd. 22, 23, 26, and others, Hieron., Syro-hex. Codd. 62, 86, 147 have Φαρὰν, not κατασκίου δασέος.
[9] Hieron.: “Aq. and Sym. and the Fifth ed. put the Hebrew itself, THEMAN; Theod. alone interpreted what THEMAN signifies, saying: _Eloim shall come from the south, and the Holy One from mount Pharan, to the end._ From which we understand that it was put by the LXX alone, _from the shady and thick mountain._” On Ἐλωεὶμ cf. Hex. on Mich. iii. 7. vi. 8. Somewhat differently Euseb. ibid. p. 280: Μεταλαμβάνεται γὰρ Θαιμὰν εἰς τὸν νότον· διόπερ ὁ Θεοδοτίων τοῦτον ἑρμηνεύει τὸν τρόπον, ὁ θεὸς ἀπὸ νοτίου ἥξει. Theodoret.: τὸ Θαιμὰν οἱ μὲν λίβα, οἱ δὲ νότον ἡρμηνεύκασιν. Cf. Hex. on Amos i. 12.
