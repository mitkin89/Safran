Now we must consider our Syrian, whose note, ܣ. ܘܬܫܬܪܐ[?] ܝܠܝܕܬܐ܀, Middeld. renders: Σ. καὶ διαλυθῇ ἡ ἐπιγονὴ (_offspring_), appealing to Amos vii. 1, where the Syrian renders ἐπιγονὴ ἀκρίδων by ܝܠܕܐ ܕܩܡܨܐ. But I would rather render the form ܝܠܝܕܬܐ as ἡ ἐπίγονος, as if the Syrian had found in his copy the corrupt ΕΠΙΓΟΝΟΣ for ΕΠΙΠΟΝΟΣ. [In fact, in the codex the Greek ΚΑΙ ΔΙΑΛΥЄΤΑΙ Ἡ ЄΠΙΓΟΝΟϹ is written beside the Syriac of Symmachus.] The word ἐπίπονος in this place has greatly exercised the critics, whose conjectures, not to say hallucinations, I forbear to copy out. For my part I do not see why ἡ ἐπίπονος should give such offence, if it is assumed that Symmachus here read not τὸ ἅπαξ λεγόμενον, הָאֲבִיּוֹנָה, whether _desire_ or _caper-berry_, but הָאֶבְיוֹנָה, _the wretched one._ Cf. Gesen. in _Thes. Ling. Hebr._ p. 13. I will add one thing: this word is found only once in the Greek Bible, namely in a fragment of the same translator on the famous passage Jesai. liii. 3, ἀνὴρ ἐπίπονος καὶ γνωστὸς νόσῳ. (Others perhaps will be better pleased with the opinion of Dr. Abr. Geiger, who says in the journal _Jüdische Zeitschrift_ etc. T. I, p. 57: “In Eccles. xii. 5 Symmachus renders וְתָפֵר הָאֲבִיּוֹנָה by καὶ διαλυθῇ ἡ ἐπιγονή, as Syro-hex. rightly interpreted, ܘܬܫܬܪܐ[?] ܝܠܝܕܬܐ... This meaning of the Hebrew word, _sexual desire_, or _generative power_, is also offered by the Midrash on the passage: זו התאוה המטלת שלום בין איש לאשתו, i.e. _the desire which brings peace between a man and his wife._” The same learned man, in a letter addressed to me, today recommends ἡ ἐπίγονος, in the same sense, which, however, I fear can in no way be reconciled with the usage of the language.
