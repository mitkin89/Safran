We would have contended that it is a scholium on this or the preceding verse, had not Jerome expressly asserted: "Symmachus, for what we have put, _For as the sound of thorns under a pot, so is the laughter of a fool,_ ... says: _For through the voice of the unskilled one is bound with chains._" Even so it is scarcely credible that Symmachus interpreted Hebrew so clear in so perverse a way, especially since, according to our Syriac, the same translator rendered the latter clause thus: οὕτως καὶ γέλως τῶν ἀπαιδεύτων.
