CAP. IV.
[1] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܣ. ܕܝܘ̈ܢܐ܀
[2] The same: ܗ. ܐܝܟ ܝܘ̈ܢܐ ܡܢ ܣܘܓܐܐ ܕܫܘܦܪܐ ܕܝܠܟܝ܀ ܬ. ܐܟܘܬ[?] ܫܒܥܝܢ܀ Nobil., Mat.: Ε΄. ἀπὸ πλ. τοῦ κ. σου.
[3] Syro-hex. gives the following on the words τρίχωμά σου κ.τ.ἑ.: ܐ. ܓܕܘܠܬܐ ܕܣܝܡܬܐ ܕܝܠܟܝ܀ ܣ. ܣܝܡ ܪܫܐ ܡܟܣܐ ܠܟܝ܀ Middeld. in his Commentary: “Ἀ. πλόκαμον _placed upon_ σου. Σ. _Put the head guiltless_ [ܡܚܣܝܐ, as wrongly copied in Norberg's transcript] _to thee._ But all this offers very great matter for doubt, since the Syriac is very obscure, and it is not sufficiently clear to which words of the Hebrew text it belongs.” The Syriac, as edited by us more correctly from the codex, can be rendered by divining rather than by conjecturing: Ἀ. πλόκαμος (cf. on v. 9. Other Greek words are βόστρυχος, πλέγμα, πλόκιον, ἐμπλόκιον, ἐμπλοκὴ) θέματός (cf. on v. 9) σου. Σ. θέμα (ἐπίθεμα, ἐπίθεσις) κεφαλῆς σκέπει (σκεπάζει) σε. But since צַמָּה is for Jarchi _a band that holds the hair_, and for Kimchi _a curl of the hair;_ and since Sym. twice (Cap. iv. 4. vi. 6) rendered the same words מִבַּעַד לְצַמָּתֵךְ by σκεπόμεναι καλύμματι, there can hardly be doubt that the Syriac readings are somehow to be reconciled with these Hebrew words, not with those that follow.
