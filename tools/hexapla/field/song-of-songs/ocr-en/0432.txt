[47] (Forts.) ܘ. Where the letter ܘ, as usual, indicates the Sixth translator, whose reading is not given in Syriac. Now we must consider the Greek. There is indeed the word μάραθρον, _fennel_, which one sometimes finds written also μάλαθρον, but which nevertheless seems foreign to this passage. But from μάλαθρον, enlarged by one syllable with the same vowel, comes μαλάΒΑθρον, _malobathrum_, whether a _tree_ (Plin. Nat. Hist. XII, 1: “_Syria also produces malobathron, a tree with a rolled-up leaf, of a dry colour, from which an oil for unguents is pressed_”), or a _reed_ (Arrian. Peripl. Maris Erythr. 16: ἐξινιάσαντες καλάμους τοὺς λεγομένους ΠΕΤΡΟΥΣ), or only a _leaf_ (Dioscor. I, 11: Μαλάβαθρον ἔνιοι ὑπολαμβάνουσιν εἶναι τῆς Ἰνδικῆς νάρδου φύλλον, πλανώμενοι ὑπὸ τῆς κατὰ τὴν ὀσμὴν ἐμφερείας … ἴδιον γάρ ἐστι γένος φυόμενον ἐν τοῖς Ἰνδικοῖς τέλμασι, φύλλον ὂν ἐπινηχόμενον ὕδατι). The origin of the name is disputed; but whatever is to be decided about the first part of the word, it is all but certain that βάθρον is an Indian plant name, called πέτρος by Arrian, and by modern describers of oriental matters _betel, betle, betre_, variously written. That these names are almost identical in sound with the Hebrew בֶּתֶר, we need not point out. Let the learned therefore consider whether our anonymous translator, provided his reading has been correctly set down by us, alone of all perceived the true meaning of the Hebrew word. Certainly the common interpretation, which is Bochart's, _mountains of division_, so called on account of the ῥωχμοὺς and χάσματα, i.e. _the clefts of the earth_, which are produced by the violence of rainstorms, is neither very probable in itself, nor has the proper affinity with the parallel passage Cap. viii. 14, where הָרֵי בְשָׂמִים, _mountains bearing spices_, are expressly mentioned. As for the writing of the codex, once the false reading ἐπὶ τὰ ὄρη μαλάθρου was admitted, it could easily happen that the Syrian translator, in setting down the nominative case of a word entirely unknown to him, chose μαλαθρος rather than μαλαθρον, for which μαλαθρως is the best known of all slips of the pen.
