[7] Σ. ἀνυπόστατος λ. ἀπόλλυται (ἀπώλετο Colb.). Thus Colb., one Reg. A dispute has arisen about the word ἀνυπόστατος, quite familiar to Symmachus, of one _whose onset cannot be withstood._ Hence some render _invincible, most mighty_, and fit ἀνυπ. λέων to the single word לַיִשׁ, just as the same Sym. put λέων ὀλοθρεύων for the Hebr. כְּפִיר Psal. xc. 13. ciii. 21. But since ἀνυπόστατος can mean _having no substance_, others think that the words ἀνυπ. λέων ἀπόλλυται belong to the whole sentence, so that the lion is called ἀνυπόστατος _who lacks substance_, i. e. _sustenance._ Thus Montef.: “_The lion, unable to subsist, perished._” But it is one thing _to have no substance_, ἀνυπόστατος εἶναι, the opposite of which is ἐνυπόστατος; another _to lack sustenance_, which is the notion required here. Moreover it should be noted that Syro-hex. on βορὰν gives this: Σ. ἐθηρεύθη (ܐܬܨܝܕ). Θ. θήραν (ܨܝܕܐ); from which one may infer that the complete version of this interpreter was as follows: ἀνυπ. λέων ἀπόλλυται, _because nothing_ ἐθηρεύθη.
