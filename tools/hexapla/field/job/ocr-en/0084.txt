For ܡܬܢܩ̈ܦܝܢ it seems we should read ܡܬܢܩ̈ܦܢ. Then the Syriac means word for word: καὶ θυγατέρες χειρὸς κολληθήσονται; how this is to be reconciled with the Hebrew I do not see.—I had written this before the letter of A. Ceriani came into my hands, which I shall now share with the reader. “The MS.: ܘܒܢ̈ܬ ܐܝܕܐ ܡܬܢܩ̈ܦܢ ܀, with a scholium written above in a script intermediate between Estrangela and Maronite: ܒܢ̈ܬ ܐܝܕܐ ܐܝܠܝܢ[?]. ܡܐܩܠܐ ܕܡܢ ܩܒܠ[?] ܐܝܕܐ ܀. The phrase ܒܪ ܐܝܕܐ is in our translator ἐγχειρίδιον Jerem. l. 42. Epist. Jerem. 15; but you yourself know how wide the meaning of this Syriac idiom is.” With the help of this scholium I hit upon a word well known to Aquila, an imitator of Homeric forms, χερμὰς or χερμάδιον, so called according to the Grammarians _because it fills the hand of the one holding it._ Thus Zonaras: Χερμάδιον, ὁ χειροπλήθης λίθος, παρὰ τὸ τὴν χεῖρα πληροῦν. My conjecture is made more than certain by Aquila's own usage, who at Deut. xxi. 21 for the Hebrew רְגָמֻהוּ[?], Ο΄. λιθοβολήσουσιν, rendered χερμαδιοῦσιν, on the testimony of Holmes' Cod. 108.
