[17] Nobil., and St. Cyril in Cat. Niceph. p. 818. D. Abr. Geiger in _Symmachus_ etc. p. 51 supposes that Symmachus put the more general word σκευάζειν in order to support the opinion of the Jewish teachers, who laid down that this precept is to be understood in a wider sense of _any mixture of meat and milk._ To us, indeed, Symmachus's version seems to differ from that of the LXX only in this, that he paid more attention to the idiom of the Greek language, in which σκευάζειν and σκευασία are elegantly used of the _preparation_ of food. (In the text after μητρὸς αὐτοῦ Cod. 58 inserts: ὅτι ὁ ποιῶν τοιαύτην θυσίαν, μῖσος καὶ παράβασίς ἐστιν τῷ Ἰακώβ. Cf. Hex. on Deut. xiv. 20.)
