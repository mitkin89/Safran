[24] Cod. 64, with the margin cut off: Σ. ὥρκισε δὲ μ . . . . ὥστε οἰκ . . . . Θ. καὶ ἤρξατο μ . . . . . τοικει . . Jerome, as usual, imitated Symmachus, rendering the Hebrew: “_So Moses swore that he would dwell with him._” But the transitive verb ὥρκισε absolutely requires that we read Μωυσῆν, not Μωυσῆς, in agreement with the Jewish teachers, who relate the fable that Jethro bound his son-in-law by an oath to devote his firstborn son to the worship of idols. Cf. D. Abr. Geiger in _Symmachus_ etc. p. 50.
