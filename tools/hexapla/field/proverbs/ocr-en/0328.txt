[22] Nobil. gives as scholia: κλῆρός σου πεσέτω, and μέσῳ ἡμῶν, the latter of which he wrongly refers to the words γενηθήτω ἡμῖν. It is no doubt from some translator, perhaps Aquila. Parsons. gives from Cod. 248: Schol. κλῆρός σου πεσέτω. For ἐν ἡμῖν in the text Codd. 23, 252 have ἐν μέσῳ ἡμῶν; and so perhaps our Syrian, who renders ܒܝܢܬܢ. See Job. viii. 17 in Syro-hex.
[23] A double rendering, the latter part of which Schleusner, in _Opusc. Crit._ p. 262, thinks originated with the Alexandrians themselves, because they almost always render כִּיס by μαρσίππιον; while the former belongs to another translator, perhaps Symmachus. As regards the usage of the Alexandrians, the translator of this book used neither μαρσίππιον nor βαλάντιον, the word solely approved by the Atticists, except in this single passage; and Jaeger rightly perceived that the former rendering savours more of his style. Add that the latter rendering is wanting in Cod. 23; and that our Syrian has marked: ※ καὶ μαρσίππιον ↙ ἓν γενηθήτω ἡμῖν; where the metobelus apparently should be moved to after ἡμῖν.
