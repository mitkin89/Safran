[21] Schol. in Nobil. The word πίνωσις is of uncertain explanation. Bochart, in _Hieroz._ II, 45, p. 489, understands it of the _wool of the pinna_, as if the translator had found חלך written for חלי and had confused it with the similar-sounding Talmudic word כָּלָךְ; by this name, he asserts, the Hebrews designate τὸ πίννινον ἔριον, i.e. the _wool_ of the _pinna_, a kind of pearl-bearing mussel, which S. Basil, in Hexaëm. VII (Opp. T. I, p. 68 E), attests to be of a golden colour: Πόθεν τὸ χρυσοῦν ἔριον αἱ πίνναι τρέφουσιν, ὅπερ οὐδεὶς τῶν ἀνθοβάφων μέχρι νῦν ἐμιμήσατο; Letronne follows in Bochart's footsteps to some extent in the journal _Journal des Savans_, August 1833, p. 512; in a papyrus of the time of Euergetes II, among the valuables carried off by a runaway slave, he believed he had read the letters ΠΙΝΑϹ Ι, i.e. _ten pinnae_, which he explains not of the mussels themselves but of _pinna pearls_, calling to his aid our anonymous translator, in whom, he contends, πίνωσιν χρυσῆν designates nothing but _a gold ring in which a pearl is set_. But I fear that all this is far from the truth. For granting that πίνναν is sometimes used of the gem itself, and that the spelling πίναν has something to support it (e.g. Etym. M. p. 672, 43: Πινοτήρης· ὁ τὴν πίναν τηρῶν, ἤγουν ΤΟΝ ΛΙΘΟΝ), the very form of our word points as with a finger to its derivation, namely from πίνος, πινόω, πίνωσις. Now it is well established that Πίνος with its derivatives is not always used of _dirt_, but sometimes of the _bloom_ of freshly picked plums and grapes, sometimes of that _patina_ of antiquity which was thought not so much to disfigure as to commend the works of ancient artists. Then πίνωσις χρυσῆ would literally mean a _golden patination_ (pardon the word); and whoever rendered חֲלִי thus (and why should we not believe it to be Aquila, who after his manner attends not so much to the sense of the passage as to the proper meaning of the individual words), it is credible that he had in view the cognate word חֶלְאָה, _rust_ (Ezech. xxiv. 6).
