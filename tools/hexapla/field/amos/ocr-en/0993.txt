=== Blatt 0993 · S. 977 · –Cap. VII. 14.] AMOS.
--- Fußnoten
[2] (Forts.) AZA [עִזָּה], I do not sufficiently understand, unless perhaps he put the word GOZI itself, and it was gradually corrupted by error into _Gaza_." Our Syrian seems to have taken Aquila's Greek not of the _king of Gaza_ but of the _treasure_ (gaza) _of the king_, perhaps more correctly, rendering: ܐ. ܘܗܐ ܠܐܚܪܝܐ[?] ܒܬܪ ܓܙܐ ܕܡܠܟܐ ◆ (The codex writes ܓܙܐ, _treasure_ (1 Esdr. viii. 18. Acts viii. 27 in Philox.), without the diacritical point, not ܓܙܐ, κουρὰ, as in Symmachus and Theodotion. Nevertheless, in the three readings a cross placed above the word refers to the Greek ΚΟΥΡΑΝ added in the margin.)
[3] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܘܐܝܟ ܕܢܐܡܪ ܠܐܚܪܝܐ[?] ܒܬܪ ܓܙܐ ܕܡܠܟܐ ◆ ܬ. ܘܗܐ ܠܐܚܪܝܐ[?] ܒܬܪ ܓܙܬܗ ܕܡܠܟܐ ◆ (The expression ܐܝܟ ܕܢܐܡܪ, ὡς εἰπεῖν, is also used for ὅσον (in μικρὸν ὅσον) in Hex. on Ezech. xvi. 47.)
[4] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܐܟܠ ◆
[5] Montef., who compiled from Walton, printed: Σ. τίς ὢν (sic) ὑ. Ἰακώβ. But the true reading of the codex, on the testimony of Walton, Stephanopolus, and Cozza, is: Σ. τί ὢν ὑ. Ἰ.; less correctly, it seems, for τί ὢν, or, which I would prefer, τίς ὢν, not, as Montef. attempts, τίς οὖν. The Hebrew is a concise expression for: _Who is Jacob, that he should be able to stand?_ which sense Symmachus expressed excellently. (Next, at מִי יָקוּם (v. 5) the same codex cites: Σ. ὢν (sic) ὑποστήσεται.)
[6] Cod. 86, which however at v. 6 cites: Ἀ. παρακλήθητι.
[7] Cod. 42.
[8] Syro-hex. ܬ. ܘܩܪܐ ܠܕܝܢܐ ◆
[9] Thus Ald., Codd. III, XII (in the margin), 22, 23, 26, and others, Hieron., Syro-hex., which cites in the margin: Οἱ λοιποί· κύριος.
[10] Cod. 86. On Sym. cf. Hex. on Psal. xxxvi. 36.
[11] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܡܨܠܚܐ[?] ◆ ܬ. ܡܬܦܫܪܢܐ ◆ Since Hieron. expressly attributes γάνωσιν to Aquila for אֲנָךְ, it is uncertain in which place, one might conjecture that in this first place (for the Hebrew word occurs four times in vv. 7, 8) our φιλόμηρον translator put γανόωντα, or γανῶντα, _shining_, which our Syrian rendered by ܡܨܠܚܐ[?] (= λάμποντα Acts xii. 7. xxvi. 13 in both versions). But see the following note.
[12] Hieron.: "Before we discuss the _adamant_, which Sym. and the LXX translated for what is written in the Hebrew ENACH, it must be said briefly that Aq. rendered this word γάνωσιν, Theod. τηκόμενον; of which the one means _tinning_, the other _melting_. ... The Lord therefore is seen standing upon a _tinned_, or _plastered_ wall; and in his hand a _tinning_, or _mason's trowel_, with which walls are usually coated, and receive not only beauty but also strength against the damage of rains and very frequent storms." According to this explanation, one must consider whether in the first place Aq. rendered ἐπὶ τείχους γεγανωμένου, to which word the Syrian less happily applied the other notion of _shining_ (as in the well-known phrase φαιδρὸς καὶ γεγανωμένος). The same certainly cites in the present place: Ἀ. _splendour_ (ܡܨܠܚܘܬܐ[?] = στίλβωσις Ezech. xxi. 15). Θ. τηκόμενον (as before). (In v. 8, on ἀδάμαντα in one of the two places, Cod. 86 cites with little probability: Σ. γάνωσιν.)
[13] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܐܬܬܪܗ[?] ܡܫܒܩ[?] ܠܗ ◆ Cf. on Cap. viii. 2.
[14] Cod. 86.
[15] The same, on the testimony of Walton and Cozza. The corrupt reading of Symmachus can be tackled by various conjectures, of which the simplest of all is ἐποίησεν ἄνταρσιν, comparing Hex. on 4 Reg. xi. 14. Jesai. viii. 12. On συστροφὰς Syro-hex. cites in the margin without a name: ܡܪܘܕܘܬܐ ◆, which word alternates with ἄνταρσις in Hex. on 4 Reg. l. c.
[16] Hieron.: "For _herdsman_, which in Hebrew is called BOCER, Aq. and Sym. and Theod. and the Fifth ed. translated βουκόλον, one who pastures herds, not sheep." Also Syro-hex.: Ἀ. Σ. Θ. βουκόλος (ܒܩܪܐ).
