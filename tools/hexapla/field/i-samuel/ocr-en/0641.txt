[14] Cod. 243 (with ἐρεμέοντο). Cod. 243* in the margin: καὶ ἠρεμέοντο ὁπουδ. “The word is undoubtedly corrupt.”—_Montef._ “I conjecture that we should write ἐρεμβεύοντο, or ἐρέμβοντο.”—_Parsons's amanuensis._ The same remedy had long ago been applied by the most illustrious Valckenaer on Eurip. Phoen. p. 250: “In Diog. Laert. IX, 63, for συνεπορεύετο Menagius found in the MSS. the doubtless genuine reading συνέρεμβετο. In 1 Reg. xxiii. 23, where the Alexandrians twice put ἐπορεύοντο, Symmachus had given ἐρέμβοντο ὁπουδήποτε. I point this out so that the worthless word ἐρεμέοντο, which Montfaucon published from the venerable Bern. MS., may not be attributed to the most correct of the Greek translators of the Old Covenant.” Suidas: Ἐρέμβοντο· ἀπεπλανῶντο. Οἱ δὲ ἐρέμβοντο κατὰ τὴν χώραν ἕνεκα τῆς ἰδίας ὠφελείας. Cf. Hex. on Psal. lviii. 16. cxviii. 118. Jerem. iii. 14. Hieron. had Symmachus before his eyes, elegantly rendering: “_they wandered uncertainly hither and thither._”

The epithet αὐχμώδης is commonly referred to the Hebrew זִיף, both here and in Cap. xxvi. 2, with the support of Euseb. in the Onomasticon, p. 198: Ζείβ. Ὄρος αὐχμῶδες τῆς Ζείβ. It must not, however, be concealed that in the passage Jerem. xlviii. 31 the noun αὐχμὸς is interchanged with the Hebrew חֶרֶשׂ (in קִיר־חֶרֶשׂ), whence the suspicion may arise that the superfluous words ἐν τῷ ὄρει τῷ αὐχμώδει are nothing but a double rendering of the Hebrew בַּחֹרְשָׁה, which would agree not badly with the meaning of the unused root חָרַשׁ or חָרַם, “_was dry, arid._” Cf. on v. 18.
