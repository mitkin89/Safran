Σ. ὑακίνθου; as well as from Cod. 87*: Ἀ. χρυσόλιθος. Σ. ὑάκινθον κατασκοπὴ χώρας (sic); where the gloss κατασκοπὴ χαρᾶς (Hieron. “_exploration of joy_”) pertains to the etymology of the Hebrew תַּרְשִׁישׁ, from תּוּר, _to explore_, and שׂוּשׂ, _to rejoice_.) Jerome's memory failed him: “_Tharsis_, which we have translated _sea_: Aquila put _hyacinth_, a stone which has the likeness of the sky.” But in fact the same author reports that Symmachus, not Aquila, translated _hyacinth_, in the Comment. on Cap. x. 9, as well as in Epist. XXXVII to Marcella (Opp. T. I, p. 172).
