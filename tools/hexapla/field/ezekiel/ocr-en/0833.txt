=== Blatt 0833 · S. 817 · –Cap. XX. 15.] EZECHIEL.
--- Fußnoten
[11] (Forts.) [‘But why Hieron. rendered στάσιν by _region_, I do not see; perhaps he read something else.’—_Montef._ Without doubt Hieron. attributed θρησκείαν to Symmachus, rendering it _religion_. Cf. Hex. on Jerem. iii. 19]; Theodotion, _strength;_ namely, that this is the foundation of all lands, because in it is religion, and the temple, and the ceremonies, and the knowledge of God.” Codd. 86, 88 cite: Ἀ. στάσεις (sic). Σ. θρησκεία. Θ. δυνάμεις (sic). Cf. on v. 15.
[12] Scholion in Nobil.: Κηρίον ὁ Ἑβραῖος καλλίστην εἶπεν. Σύμμαχος, στάσις ἐστὶν, ἔφη· στάσιν καλέσας τὸ αὐτόθι τὴν ἱερατείαν ἀνακεῖσθαι θεῷ. It is by S. Severus, according to our Syrian, who notes in the margin: ܕܣܘܝܪܐ ܩܕܝܫܐ ◆ ܟܢܘܪܐ[?] ܡܢ ܥܒܪܝܐ ܫܦܝܪܬܐ ܐܡܪ ◆ ܣܘܡܟܘܣ ܕܝܢ ܩܝܡܬܐ ܐܝܬܝܗ̇ ܐܡܪ. ܟܕ ܩܝܡܬܐ ܩܪܐ ܠܗ̇ ܠܬܡܢ ܕܣܝܡܐ ܟܘܡܪܘܬܐ[?] ܗܘܬ ܠܐܠܗܐ ◆. [14] Cod. Jes. Thus Cod. 86 at v. 8, according to De Reg. Hieron.: “For _offences_, which in Hebrew are called SECUSE, Sym. translated _nauseas_, the second edition of Aquila _cuttings-off_, to signify the bleariness of the eyes that is to be cast away, and as it were nauseas, which not only hinder the sight, but also cause vomiting in those who see them.” On Symmachus cf. on Cap. vii. 19, 20. xi. 18. In vain therefore does Valckenaer, in _Opusc._ T. II, p. 247, suspect that in our passage not Symmachus but Aquila wrote τὰ σίκχη.
