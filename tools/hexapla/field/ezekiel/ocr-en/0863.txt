[23] Codd. 86, 88. Hieron.: “Further, the interpretation of Symmachus describes that prince of Tyre as adorned with stones, like a most precious necklace [whence someone made a hexaplaric note at ἐνδέδεσαι: Σ. διέστιξαι]. Finally he calls the _setting_ of gold that in which the stones are fixed.” That the latter interpretation belongs to Symmachus, not to Hieronymus (which is the error of Rosenmüller, Gesenius, and others), Drusius rightly suspected. Hieron. himself translated according to Aquila: _the work of thy beauty._
