[5] Codd. 86, 88 (both with the faulty spelling ἐπεποίθησεν). Hieron. perhaps had in view the version of Theodotion, rendering the Hebrew: _And she went mad_ (or _went_) _after her lovers._ (Noteworthy is the somewhat recherché use of the words ἐριθεύεσθαι and ἐριθεία, of those who _strive by any means to win favour for themselves_, especially of magistrates _courting popular favour with excessive zeal_, or even _by evil arts_, of which usage the best and almost unique example is given by Aristot. Polit. V. 3. 9: Μεταβάλλουσι δ᾽ αἱ πολιτεῖαι καὶ ἄνευ στάσεως, διά τε τὰς ἐριθείας, ὥσπερ ἐν Ἡραίᾳ· ἐξ αἱρετῶν γὰρ διὰ τοῦτο ἐποίησαν κληρωτὰς, ὅτι ᾑροῦντο τοὺς ἐριθευομένους· καὶ δι᾽ ὀλιγωρίαν κ. τ. ἑ. Here, however, and more often in this chapter, Symmachus, not unfittingly for the sense of the Hebrew word, in keeping with his fondness for recondite Greek, uses the word of a harlot soliciting her lovers by canvassing, by address, by any means whatever.)
