[47] Syro-hex. ܬ. ܠܡܩܪܩܫܘ ܒܓܐܪ̈ܐ ◆. That word is the very one which our translator set in the text for the Greek ἀναβράσαι, to which the grammarians properly attribute the force of _tossing_ and _shaking_. Hesych.: Ἀναβεβρασμένη· ἀνακεκινημένη. Timaeus, _Lex. Platon._ p. 64: Βράττειν· ἀνακινεῖν, ὥσπερ οἱ τὸν σῖτον καθαίροντες; where see Ruhnken. As to the less common Syriac word, the form ܩܪܩܫ can be compared with the Chaldee קַרְקֵשׁ, _to knock, to strike a blow, to make a sound by striking;_ and it seems to bear roughly this sense in Knoes, _Chrestom. Syr._ p. 79: ܩܪܩܫܘ ܣܘܣܘ̈ܬܐ ܒܙܓ̈ܐ ܘܙܝܢ̈ܐ, _the horses rattled with bells and arms;_ a passage pointed out to me by R. L. Bensly, who aptly compared the Homeric (Il. XIX, 13): τὰ δὲ (τεύχεα) ἈΝΕΒΡΑΧΕ δαίδαλα πάντα. Moreover Bickell, in the _Glossary to S. Ephraem's Carm. Nisib._ p. 64, cites for the form ܐܬܩܪܩܫ S. Ephraem. Opp. T. III, p. 633 F, where from the context it seems to mean _to be agitated, to be shaken with a noise_. Further, in the reading of Theodotion, for ܠܡܩܪܩܫܘ, according to the usage of our Syrian (on which cf. Ceriani on Exod. ix. 32. xxv. 17), we must look for a different Greek word, which nevertheless does not differ in sense from the other: which indeed, having regard to the Greek books, may be πατάξαι; but far more suitable would be παταγῆσαι, corrupted into πατάγσαι, πατάξαι.
