[28] Jerome: "Aq. translated: _Who has measured the waters with the smallest finger?_ For this is what λιχὰς means; namely that not with the whole hand, but with a small finger, which is commonly called the _taster_, the whole vastness of the waters is weighed." On which passage Vallarsi: "Montef. preferred, as the editions before Martianay also had, λιχανός; for so the _taster_, or index finger, is called. But all the MSS., which both we and the praised Benedictines consulted, have with equal consent λιχὰς, which word denotes the smallest measure of the hand, with the thumb and index finger spread out."
[29] Jerome: "For the _handful_, which the LXX translated in the comprehending of the earth, in the Hebrew is written SALIS, which Sym. interpreted τρίτον, Aquila τρίσωμον; and we, to make it plainer, have rendered _three fingers_." Vallars. on the passage: "In the Vatic. ΤΡΙΣΩΜΑ; but in the Sangerm. MS. [and Bodl. Laud. 455] ΤΡΙΣΣΩΜ, on the testimony of Montef., who rightly suspects that the word τρίσωμον lies in error." From ΤΡΙΣΣΩΜ there easily emerges ΤΡΙΣΣΟΝ, which Aq. put for שָׁלִישׁ Psal. lxxix. 6.
