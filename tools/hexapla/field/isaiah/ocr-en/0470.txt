[7] Euseb., Procop., Theodoret.
[8] Curter. and Parsons. from Cod. Jes. For ἐγὼ ἐνετειλάμην, which we have adopted from Montfaucon's conjecture, the codex has, according to both, (ἑκουσιαζομένων) σιων ἐνετειλάμην, and at the end under another index ὑπερφανείας (sic) μου; but καὶ ἐκάλεσα is an error of Curterius.
[9] Curterius cites two readings. The latter Montef. thinks should be assigned to Theodotion. Parsons. cites from Cod. XII: Σ. ἐν τῷ δοξασμῷ μου; but from Cod. 88: Σ. χαίρονται (sic) τῇ ὕβρει μου. Ἄλλος· τῆς ὑπερηφανείας μου.
[10] Euseb., Nobil. Jerome p. 236: “But the name _giants_, for which in the Hebrew GEBORIM, that is, _mighty ones_, stands, the LXX and Theod. rendered after the likeness of the fables of the Gentiles, just as they also name Sirens, and Titans, and Arcturus, the Hyades, and Orion, which among the Hebrews are called by other names.”
[11] Curter.
[12] Curter., and Parsons. from Codd. XII, 88, all of which erroneously have: κύριος κύριος σκεύη ὀργῆς αὐτοῦ. But ὁρμῆς for ὀργῆς is to be imputed to Montfaucon's carelessness.
[13] Theodoret.
[14] Curter. cites: Οἱ Γ΄. ὀδικήσουσιν (sic). Cod. 88: Οἱ Γ΄. ἀδικήσουσιν. Montef. corrected it.
[15] Curter. Syro-hex. in the text: (Codd. 87, 91, 104 prefix καὶ) μήνιδος (ܕܐܘܠܬܐ[?]) θυμοῦ καὶ ὀργῆς.
[16] Jerome p. 239: “For what we have rendered _their splendour_, doubtless of the stars, Aq. and Theod. put the Hebrew word itself CHISILEEM; for which the LXX translated _Orion_, adding of their own, _and all the adornment of heaven_, which is to be marked with an obelus [thus in Syro-hex.].” The same p. 172: “The Hebrew word CHISILE the LXX rendered Ὠρίωνα; the Hebrew whom I used as teacher rendered it _Arcturus_. We, following Symmachus in general terms, said _star_ (al. _stars_).” Cf. Hex. on Amos v. 8.
[17] Curter., Cod. 88, and without asterisk Comp., Codd. 22, 36, and others, and Syro-hex.
[18] Curter. Syro-hex. ܠ.[?] ܫܡܫܐ ܒܡܦܩܢܐ ܕܝܠܗ. Chrysost. Opp. T. I, p. 17 E cites: καὶ σκοτισθήσεται ὁ ἥλιος ἐν τῇ ἐξόδῳ αὐτοῦ.
[19] Jerome p. 241: “The Hebrew word GAON, which plainly means _arrogance_ and _pride_, Theod. and the LXX always rendered _injury_, expressing the sense rather than the word.”
[20] The same ibid.: “Then a man will be more precious than gold, which in Hebrew is called PHAZ, and Aq. rendered it κίρρον [i.e. _of a reddish colour inclining to pale_], which is of the best and blood-red colour.” Cf. Hex. on Prov. viii. 19.
[21] Jerome ibid., where σπήλωμα is printed. (For σπίλωμα, _stain_, the Syriac ܟܬܡ, _he stained_, is to be compared, which both translators interchanged with the Greek ἐσπίλωσε, Jac. iii. 6. Jud. 23. On Ὠφεὶρ cf. Hex. on Psal. xliv. 10, and our _Prolegom. ad V. T. juxta LXX Interpp._ p. xvii.)
