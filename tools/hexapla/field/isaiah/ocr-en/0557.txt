[39] Jerome: "And what follows [in the version of the Hebrew text]: _Every one to his own avarice, from the highest to the last. Come, let us take wine_ etc., is not found in the LXX translators, but has been added from the Hebrew, and is marked beforehand with shining stars." Where the former part of the version is a manifest imitation of those words, ἕκαστος εἰς τὴν πλεονεξίαν αὐτοῦ ἀπ᾽ ἄκρου ἕως ἐσχάτου, which we have claimed for Symmachus as author; whence it becomes probable that Jerome had the same guide in the latter part also.
[40] Curter., in whom αὔριον is absent, against Parsons' MSS., and for περισσῶς περὶ σὲ ὡς is wrongly written. Cod. 88 also cites: Σ. Θ. δεῦτε λάβωμεν — αὔριον μεγάλη περὶ σὲ ὡς σφόδρα. Nearly the same words are read in the context of the LXX, as it is more fully exhibited by Codd. 22, 23, 36, and others; of which one or two exhibit μέθην and περὶ σὲ ὡς, but the greater part μέθῃ and περισσῶς. To these is added our Syriac, which continues for Theodotion: ܬܘ ܢܣܒ ܚܡܪܐ ܘܢܬܚܡܡ[?] ܒܚܡܪܐ[?] (οἰνοφλογίσωμεν) ܒܪܘܝܘܬܐ (μέθῃ) ܘܢܗܘܐ ܐܝܟ ܗܢܐ ܝܘܡܐ ܕܡܚܪ ܪܒܐ ܝܬܝܪܐܝܬ ܛܒ ↙ (περισσῶς). (The faulty spelling οἰνοφλογίσωμεν is exhibited by Cod. 147.) Finally Jerome, who in rendering the Hebrew imitated Symmachus, translated the Greek of Theodotion according to rule thus: _Every one together from its summit. Come, and let us take wine, and be drunken; and let tomorrow be such a day, great, exceedingly more._
