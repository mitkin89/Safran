[9] Idem. In the codex there is ἐκπρηστὴς, not badly; but ἐμπρηστὴς for שָׂרָף is assigned to Aquila at Deut. viii. 15. Jerome: “For _the flying basilisk_, which the LXX translated _offspring of flying asps_, in the Hebrew it is called ἐμπρηστὴς _and flying_ (perhaps _flying out_), which we can call _the burning one_, and in Hebrew it is called SARAPH.”
[10] Idem. If the reading is true, the translator seems to have referred מְעוֹפֵף not to עוּף, _to fly_, but to עָיֵף, _to be faint_.
[11] Idem. For εκ κρυφων, which is plainly corrupt, I would almost affirm that the translator gave ἐκροφῶν. For, first, at Jesai. xiv. 29, for the Hebrew וּפִרְיוֹ שָׂרָף מְעוֹפֵף, Jerome has in his version, _and its seed swallowing the bird_; but in the commentary: “From the asps shall come forth _flying serpents_ [which is the version τῶν Ο΄, ὄφεις πετάμεναι], or, _such as swallow birds._” Then, whatever be decided about biblical usage, it is certain that both the Talmudic שָׂרַף (see Buxtorf. in _Lex. Chald. et Talmud._ p. 2531) and the Syriac ܣܪܦ (see Hex. on Job. v. 5) have the meaning _to swallow_. For the rest, in view of the passage of Jerome, there may perhaps be someone who prefers καὶ ἐκροφῶν πετόμενον.
[12] Jerome: “Where we have rendered _carrying upon the shoulders of beasts of burden_, for _beasts of burden_ all alike translated πώλους, that is, _foals of asses_.”
[13] Curter., who directs it to be inserted into the text after ἔφερον. Codd. 22, 23 (under ※), 36, and others: οἳ ἔφερον ἐπὶ ὄνων θησαυροὺς αὐτῶν. But Syro-hex. refers the reading to the Hebr. אוֹצְרוֹתָם, noting in the margin after καμήλων: ※ ܣܝ̈ܡܬܐ ܕܝܠܗܘܢ ܀
[14] Curter. inserts in the margin between ἐπὶ and ὄνων: ※ Θ. ὤμων; on whose authority Grabe printed ἐπ’ ※ ὤμων ↙ ὄνων, for the Hebrew עַל־כֶּתֶף עֲיָרִים. But our Syriac, at καὶ ἐπὶ καμήλων, cites in the margin: Θ. καὶ ἐπ’ ὤμων (ܘܥܠ ܟܬܦ̈ܬܐ), with which agree Codd. 23, 109, 302, 305, which expressly read καὶ ἐπ’ ὤμων καμήλων.
