[27] Procop.: διά τε τῶν θηρίων τὸ ἄγνωστον, τὴν Ἑβραϊκὴν θέντες λέξιν, σιεὶν, οἱ λοιποὶ τῶν ἑρμηνευτῶν ἐκδεδώκασιν. Euseb. has σειεὶν. Jerome p. 174: “SIIM, which the LXX alone translated _beasts_.” The same p. 245: “For which Aq. and Sym. and Theod. put the Hebrew word itself, SIIM.” Cf. on Cap. xxxiv. 14.
[28] Jerome p. 174: “_And the houses shall be filled_, he says, _with dragons_, as we have said; _with typhoons_, as Aq. translated; OHIM, as Sym., expressing the Hebrew word itself; but the LXX and Theod. rendered _cries_, or _sounds_.” The same p. 245 has similar words, relying on which Montef. claims τυφώνων for Aquila, noting: “Curterius φώνων, wrongly.” But τύφωνες are improperly counted among the beasts dwelling in the desert; and besides Curterius, confirmed by Parsons, Cod. 88 cites: Ἀ. φωνῶν; and Syro-hex.: Ἀ. _cries_, or _noises_ (ܩܠܐ). The error, therefore, is to be imputed, as it seems, to Jerome, not to the Greek books.
[29] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܣ. ܢܥܡܝܬܐ. ܬ. ܒܢܬܐ ܕܢܥܡܝܬܐ. Curter. cites: Ἀ. Σ. στρουθοκάμηλοι. Procop., Euseb., Theodoret.: Οἱ λοιποί· στρουθοκάμηλοι. Cf. on Cap. xliii. 20.
[30] Montef., following Curterius, printed: Ἀ. τριχιῶνται. Θ. ὀρθοτριχοῦνται. But Greek usage knows neither word. Without doubt we should read: Ἀ. τριχιῶντας. Θ. ὀρθοτριχοῦντας; or rather τριχιῶντες, ὀρθοτριχοῦντες. Procop.: τούτους δὲ, φασὶ, τριχιῶντας Ἀκύλας ὠνόμασε. There is one who prefers τριχιῶνες, from a noun τριχιών; but this too seems to be rejected. The nominative is τριχιῶν from the verb τριχιάω, which is read in the Hex. on Deut. xxxii. 17. Thus at Levit. xvii. 7, for the Hebr. לַשְּׂעִירִם, the Hexapla cite: Ἀ. Σ. τοῖς τριχιῶσι. Θ. τοῖς σεειρείμ. Below, Cap. xxxiv. 14, the Hexapla give: Ἀ. Σ. τριχιῶν. Θ. ὀρθοτριχῶν. In the present passage the Syriac has: Ἀ. τριχιῶντες (ܣܥܪܢܐ[?]). Θ. ὀρθοτριχοῦντες (ܙܩܝܦܝ ܣܥܪܬܐ[?], _erect of hair._ Cf. Hex. on Ezech. xxxii. 10). Finally Jerome p. 245: “But the _hairy ones_, who are called in Hebrew SIRIM, Theod. rendered _with hair erect and standing_; Sym. and the LXX translated _demons_.”
[31] Jerome p. 175: “For _owls_ all put the Hebrew word itself, IIM; the LXX alone translated _onocentaurs_.” Euseb. and Procop. erroneously write σιεὶν. Cf. on Cap. xxxiv. 14.
