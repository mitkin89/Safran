[2] Curter. cites: ※ κάθισον εἰς τὴν γῆν, κ.τ.ἑ. Syro-hex. ※ ܐ. ܬ. ܬܒܝ. ܥܘܠܝ. ܠܐܪܥܐ. ܠܝܬ ܟܘܪܣܝܐ܀[?]; that is, ※ Ἀ. Θ. κάθισον, εἴσελθε εἰς τὴν γῆν, κ.τ.ἑ. The reading of Codd. 22, 36, and others is as follows: κάθισον (Cod. 36 add. εἴσελθε) εἰς τ. γ., οὐκ ἔστι θρ., εἴσελθε εἰς τὸ σκ.
[3] Jerome: "In the place where we have interpreted, _uncover thy shame_, for which the LXX translated, _reveal thy covering_, Theod. put the Hebrew word itself, _samthech;_ Aq. _semmathech;_ Sym. τὸ σιωπηλόν σου, which we may express, _thy silence._ This indeed we read also in the Song of Songs, where the beauty of the bride is described; at the end it adds, _besides thy silence;_ those who interpreted being unwilling to translate a word which in holy Scripture would signify shame." It is self-evident that Symmachus derived this word from צָמַת, _he was silent_, which happened also to the Elders on Cant. Cant. iv. 1, 3. vi. 6.
