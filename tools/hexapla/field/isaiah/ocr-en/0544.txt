[11] Jerome: "And in the Hebrew it is said: _The heavens shall melt away like smoke_, or according to Aquila and Sym., _shall be broken in pieces_, shall be crushed to nothing and after the manner of salt, and shall vanish; of whom the former said ἡλίσθησαν [ΗΛΙϹΘΗϹΑΝ. So expressly the Cod. Ambros. called in by Vallarsi. The old reading, μειωθήσεται, is owed not to the books, but to the ingenuity of Erasmus. But Montef., from the Cod. Sangermanensis, which has ΝΝϹϹΝϹΑΝ[?], and two Regii, which have ΝΗϹΗϹΑΝ[?] and ΝΕϹΗϹΑΝ, claimed for Aquila ἠλοήθησαν, _they were threshed_, which has nothing in common with _salt_. Indeed it is manifest that the Sangermanensis writing differs hardly a whit from ἡλίσθησαν], the latter ἁλίσουσιν [Erasmus by guesswork printed ἁλιώσονται. The same Ambrosianus has ΑΛΛΛΗϹΟΥϹΙΝ[?], the Sangerm. and the Regii ΑΝϹΟΥϹΙΝ; whence Montef. and Vallarsi with one accord assigned ἁλίσουσιν to Symmachus: which form, however, I should not readily believe could mean _to imitate the manner of salt_, as Montef. supposes, or _to vanish after the manner of salt_, as Vallarsi]; which manifestly draws its ἐτυμολογίαν from the crumbling and melting away of salt. Instead of which I wonder what the LXX meant to say, _the heaven was made firm like smoke:_ for if firmness is taken for strength, how is that which is firm compared to smoke?"
