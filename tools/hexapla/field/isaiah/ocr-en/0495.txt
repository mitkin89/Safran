[25] Jerome: “But I wonder what Theodotion meant, that for the Hebrew word which is called CISOA, for which Aq. rendered _vomit of filth_ [cf. on Cap. xxx. 22], and Sym. only _vomit_, he should say ἐμετὸν δυσαλίας, that is, _vomit of dysalia_, a word which I have not been able to find read anywhere among the Greeks, unless perhaps he coined a new name for a new thing. For among the Hebrews too it is a compound, since _vomit_ is called CI, and SOA _filth_. Whatever therefore causes nausea and vomiting can be called δυσαλία.” We have written δεισαλίας, following Bos, on whom see v. 13.

[31] “The same Barberini codex has the readings of Symmachus and Theodotion, where [for δυσαλία] δεισαλία is read, erroneously.”—_Montef._ But let us hear Bos in Proleg. Cap. III, whom Vallarsius plundered in his note on Jerome's Comment. on the passage: “Theodotion translates δεισαλία εἰς δεισαλίαν. For so it must be read, as we have also shown. In Hesychius there is: Δίσαλα, ἀκαθαρσία; but Martinius rightly pointed out that δισαλία must be read. It comes from δεῖσα, which in Suidas is ἡ κόπρος, for whom likewise δεισαλέα are κοπρώδη. In Theodotion's version δυσαλία was formerly read corruptly, which was a crux for Jerome . . . But the most learned Father was deceived by a bad reading, reading δυσαλία for δισαλία.” The inferior spelling δυσαλία will recur at Cap. xxx. 22.
