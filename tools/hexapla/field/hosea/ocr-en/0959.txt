[21] (Forts.) καὶ τὸν αὐτὸν ἀποπεραίνεται νοῦν. Ἐκδεδώκασι γὰρ, οἱ μὲν, ὅτι ἰδοὺ ἐγὼ ἀποφέρω αὐτήν· οἱ δὲ, ὅτι ἀπατῶ αὐτὴν, ἑτεροτρόπως, ὡς ἔφην, αὐτὸ δὴ τοῦτο σημαίνοντες, τὸ πλανῶ φημὶ χρησίμως νοούμενον. Syro-hex. cites: ܐ. ܡܦܝܣ ܐܢܐ ܠܗ̇ ◆, which Syriac word is interchanged with ἀπατῶ at Jud. xvi. 5; but with θέλγω (which savours more of Aquila) in the Hex. on Jerem. xx. 7. Cf. Hex. on Exod. xxii. 16. Job. xxxi. 27. Hos. vii. 11.

Hieron.: "For what we have said, _to open hope_, and the LXX rendered, _to open her understanding_, Sym. rendered εἰς θύραν ἐλπίδος, that is, _for a door of hope;_ Theod. διανοῖξαι [ungrammatically ἀνεῴξασθαι used to be printed, against Vallarsi's MSS., which offer ΔΙΑΝΟΙΞΑΙ (sic)] τὴν ὑπομονὴν αὐτῆς, that is, _to open her patience_, or _her expectation._"
[27] Hieron.: "In the place where we have put _she shall sing_, and the LXX translated _she shall be humbled_, in the Hebrew is written ANATHA, which Sym. rendered _she shall be afflicted_ [Cod. 86: Σ. κακωθήσεται], Theod. _she shall answer_, Aq. _she shall obey_, that is, ὑπακούσει; we take it rather from the Hebrew κατὰ λέξιν [perhaps καταλέξει, comparing Hex. on Exod. xv. 21. Psal. cxlvi. 7. Jerem. li. 14], that is, _she shall lead the song._" The same below: "The interpretation of Aquila and Theodotion, of whom the one put ὑπακούσει, that is, _she shall hear_, the other ἀποκριθήσεται, that is, _she shall answer_, is on our side, in that, when some lead the song, others answer singing together." (Dathe in _Opusc._ p. 37 is mistaken in asserting that the LXX and Symmachus put other points under the Hebrew, namely וְעֻנְּתָה.)

Middeld. attempts: Ἀ. σὺν τὸν _fluxum_, καὶ σὺν τὴν οἰνίαν, καὶ σὺν τὴν στιλπνότητα, noting: "To restore the Syr. ܪܕܝܐ in Greek is truly a most difficult business. Perhaps Aq. had written ῥεῖθρον (Sym. on Psal. lxviii. 3. Job. xxviii. 10), or ῥύσιν (LXX on Job. xxxviii. 25), having in mind the proper meaning of the verb דגה. I have put οἰνίαν in the Greek, since Aq. used this word at Zach. ix. 17 in place of the Hebr. תִּירוֹשׁ, and the Syr. ܚܡܪܐ is used at Prov. xii. 11 for οἶνος.
