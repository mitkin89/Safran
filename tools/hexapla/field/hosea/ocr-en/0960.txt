"In disentangling the words of Aquila I have toiled in vain. I have rendered the syllables [much as we have rendered them], the sense I do not know."—_Middeld._ Since by ἀκούων one can easily understand _one who hears the answers of God_, the difficulty lies not so much in the words themselves as in the style of the translator to whom they are attributed. But if we suppose that Aquila translated literally, καὶ οὐκ ὄντος ἐνδύματος καὶ μορφωμάτων, and that our Syriac had in his copy the faulty spelling καὶ ἀκούοντος for καὶ οὐκ ὄντος, it is very credible that he, inserting prepositions contrary to his custom, wished to provide for the sense of the words.
