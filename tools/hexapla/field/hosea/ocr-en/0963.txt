[32] Hieron.: "What the LXX translated, _he provoked the Canaanites_, is not found in the Hebrew." The reading ἠρέθισε is not extant today in the books, which all support ηρετισε (ᾑρέτισε Ed. Rom., left by Parsons, tacitly corrected by Tischendorf after Van Ess). Syro-hex. ܪܚܡ, _he loved._
[33] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܐܢܝܚ ܗܘ ܠܗ ܪܝܫܐ ܕܒܝܬ ܡܫܬܝܐ ܕܝܠܗܘܢ ◆. Bugatus on Daniel p. 134 renders: _He made to cease_, or _dismissed, for himself the chief of their banquet._
[34] Cod. 86 in continuation. After εἰδώλοις Walton, the words being, as he says, half eaten away, thought he read: Ἐφραῒμ ἐπίκλινον συμπόσιον . . . But the anonymous collator in Parsons' papers copied: ἐκεον ἀ ἐτεκλινητ . . συμποσιον αὐτοῦ, noting: "The erased reading, read somehow thus: ἔασον αὐτόν· ἐπέκλινε τὸ σ. αὐτῶν." (Today, in the badly damaged place, one can read: Σ. Ε΄. ηνωθη εἰδω|λοις ἐασον ἀ . . . | ἐ.εκλινε τὸ | συμπόσιον . . .) This reading, however, our Syriac excellently confirms: ܣ. ܡܫܘܘܬܦ[?] ܐܨܠܝ ܒܝܬ[?] ܡܫܬܝܐ ܕܝܠܗܘܢ ◆.
[35] Syro-hex. ܬ. ܐܢܝܚ ܗܘ ܠܗ. ܐܨܠܝ ܚܡܪܐ ܕܝܠܗܘܢ ◆.
[36] The same: ܬ. ܒܝܬ ܕܘܩܐ ◆. Immediately after, for αὐτῆς, αὐτῶν is read in Comp., Codd. III, XII (by the first hand), 22, 26, 36, and others, and Syro-hex. in the margin.
[37] The same: ܣ. ܐܚܒܘ ܚܘܒܐ ܕܗܘ ܕܥܘܕܪܢܐ ܕܝܠܗ ܨܥܪܐ ܗܘ. (Whether for οὗ Sym. wrote τοῦ οὗ, examples of which construction we have adduced on S. Chrys. in Epist. Paul. T. VII, p. 398, we do not know.) Strangely Rosenmueller on the place: "De Rossi infers that Symmachus read חבו (with Cheth) from the fact that in the Ambrosian Syro-hex. Cod. this interpretation of his is adduced: ܐܚܒܘ ܚܘܒܐ ܕܗܘ, _they loved his guilt._ But there is hardly any doubt that ܨܥܪܐ corresponds to the Hebrew קָלוֹן, and that הֵבוּ was entirely omitted by Symmachus, as by the LXX."
[38] Thus Codd. 22 (prefixing αὐτοῦ), 23, 36 (prefixing αὐτοὺς), 42 (as Cod. 22), and others, and Theodoret. Hieron.: "_A whirlwind of the spirit shall whistle in his wings._"
[39] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܕܐܣܪ[?] ܪܘܚܐ ܕܝܠܗ̇ ܒܟܢܦ̈ܐ ܕܝܠܗ̇ ◆.
[40] The same continues Symmachus' words: ܐܝܟ ܕܐܢ ܐܢܫ ܢܐܣܘܪ ܠܗ ܠܪܘܚܐ ܒܟܢܦ̈ܐ ܕܝܠܗ̇ ܕܪܘܚܐ ◆. Hieron.: "Symmachus, for what we have rendered, _the wind hath bound him in its wings_, translated into Greek in this way: _as if one should bind the wind in the wings of the wind._"
