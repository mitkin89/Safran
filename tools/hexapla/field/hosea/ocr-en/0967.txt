[18] The same: ܐ. ܨܦܪܐ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܡܚܝܢܐ܀ ܣ. ܨܦܪܐ ܕܝܢ ܐܘܩܕ܀ ܬ. ܒܕܡܘܬܐ ܕܗܢܘܢ ܫܒܥܝܢ܀ "The Syr. ܡܚܝܢܐ would have to be restored in the fragment of Aquila in Greek as ἀναπαυόμενος [rather ἀναπεπαυμένος]. For thus the Syr. seems to have read instead of ἀνακαιόμενος [ἀνακεκαυμένος], which I believe stood in Aq. for בֹּעֵר."—_Middeld._ We had taken another route, namely that ܡܚܝܢܐ is an active participle of the form ܚܝܢ[?], _he made alive_, which is used for the common ܐܚܝܢ[?] in Deut. xxx. 6 in the Pesh. And this alternates with ἐζωπύρησε 4 Reg. viii. 1, 5 in Syro-hex. The lexicographers give: Ζωπυρεῖ· ἀναζῆν ποιεῖ, ἢ ἐκκαίει; and it is credible that the latter sense was in Aquila's mind. But since the form ܚܝܢ[?] depends entirely on a single passage (where for ܕܢܚܝܢ[?] the Ambrosian Codex of the 6th century (B. 21 Inf.) reads ܕܢܚܝܢ[?] with one _Nun_), until it is confirmed from elsewhere we gladly acquiesce in the opinion of our sparing conjecturer, Ceriani, who surmises that the reading ܡܚܝܢܐ is a scribal error for ܡܚܝܢܐ[?], so that it is a masculine in the emphatic state; which state frequently occurs for the _predicate_, especially in the hexaplar version, even for verbal adjectives, above all when ܐܝܬܘܗܝ and the like intervene.

[25] Hieron.: "Ephraim therefore became like a _seduced_ dove, which in Hebrew is PHOTHA; by Aq. and Sym. it is called θελγομένη, or ἀπατωμένη, that is, _enticed_, or _deceived_; and by the LXX _foolish_, or _senseless:_ for ἄνους can express both." Cod. 86 cites: Ἀ. ἀπατωμένη ἀνοήτως, wrongly. With Hieron. agrees Syro-hex. ܐ. ܕܡܬܦܟܗܐ[?]܀ ܣ. ܕܡܛܥܝܢܬܐ[?]܀ Middeld. notes: "Ἀ. _disturbed._ Σ. ἀπατωμένη. Aquila's ܕܡܬܦܟܗܐ[?] I render in Greek τεταραγμένη [ταραττομένη]; but Montef. cited θελγομένη, which is quite foreign to the Syr." He is mistaken. The Syr. verb ܦܟܗ[?], on the authority of Castell, in the Pael means _he flattered;_ e.g. for κολακεύων Job. xix. 17 in Syro-hex. there is ܡܦܟܗ[?]. In the Ethpaal, therefore, it would regularly mean _he was coaxed_, or _soothed_, even if examples were lacking. But they are not lacking. For in Psal. xviii. 8, for Hebr. פֶּתִי, _simple_, which Aquila constantly renders θελγόμενον, our Syrian has: ܐ. ܕܡܬܦܟܗ[?]܀, at which passage Bugati appeals to the present example from Hosea.
