[20] “Thus MSS. [and a Schol. in Nobil.] But Ed. Rom. χιμάρους.”—_Montef._ Parsons does not cite the reading τράγους except from Chrysost. Opp. T. VI, p. 9 B; but our Syrian seems to have read so, rendering ܬ̈ܝܫܐ.

[22] Thus Comp., Ald., Cod. Alex., and others, against Syro-hex. Cod. 264 cites: Οἱ Γ΄. ἀγροῦ. More correctly Vat.: Οἱ Γ΄, καὶ οἱ Ο΄. δρυμοῦ.
[23] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܡܛܠ ܕܠܝ ܟܠܗ ܚܝܘܬܐ ܕܥܒܐ. ܒ̈ܥܝܪܐ ܒܛܘܪ̈ܐ ܕܐܠܦܐ ◊. Bugatus renders _on a thousand mountains_; but Jerome agrees with us, rendering the Hebrew _on the mountains of thousands._
[24] Agellius on the passage: “Not far different from this rendering (ὡραιότης ἀγροῦ) is Aquila, when he said παντοδαπὰ χώρας, _the varied produce of the field_, which St. Jerome imitated, giving _and the whole of the field is with me;_ nor likewise Sym., who renders πλῆθος, _the abundance of the field;_ and so Apolinarius elegantly: ἀνθοφόρου ὄλβον ἀρούρης.” For παντοδαπὰ it seems that παντοδαπία should be put, which Aq. interchanged with the same Hebrew in Jesai. lxvi. 11; or παντοδαπὸν, which is assigned to the same in the Hex. on Psal. lxxix. 14. Our conjecture is confirmed by the Syrian, whose note is: ܕܫܘܚܠܦܐ[?] (_variety_) ܐ. ܘܫܘܚܠܦܘܬܐ[?] ܕܚܩܠܐ[?] ◊ ܗ. ܘܥܪܕܐ ܕܒܪܐ ܥܡܝ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ◊. For the Vth ed. Bar Hebr. has ܥܪܕܐ ܕܒܪܐ, which comes to the same thing.
