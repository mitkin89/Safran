[4] Nobil.: Ἀ. Θ. τοῦ κακῶσαι. "The Vaticanus Ἀ. Θ. τοῦ κακῶσαι, and next, Ἀ. Σ. οὐ σφάλῃ. That οὐ σφάλῃ, however, without doubt refers to these Hebrew words, וְלֹא יָמִר, which above are rendered thus by Sym., καὶ μὴ ἀλλαγείς; so that two versions of Symmachus are cited at the same place; unless perhaps Ἀ. Σ. has been put for Ἀ. Θ."—_Montef._ Accordingly he printed: Ἀ. Θ. ὀμόσας τοῦ κακῶσαι, καὶ οὐ σφάλῃ, rendering: "_who swears to afflict himself, and does not deceive._" But such a sense absolutely requires καὶ οὐ σφάλλων (or σφήλας). Moreover, the form σφάλῃ does not exist, and σφαλῇ, which is the true reading, has a passive sense. It is truly strange that neither Montefalconius nor his successors saw the truth, namely that this note, Ἀ. Σ. οὐ σφαλῇ, belongs to v. 5.
[5] Nobil., Vat. Agellius: ὁ ὀμ. τοῦ ἑτ. εἶναι. Syro-hex. cites: ܣ. ܐܝܢܐ ܕܝܡܐ ܠܚܒܪܐ (τῷ ἑταίρῳ) ܘܠܐ ܡܫܚܠܦ ܀
[6] Vat., as above. It seems we should read οὐ μὴ σφαλῇ. Cf. the Hex. on Prov. x. 30.
