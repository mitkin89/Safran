[46] Montef. gives from Drusius: Ἀ. πάμμυιαν. The same printed on Exod. viii. 21: Ἀ. παμμυῖαν (sic). The source of this reading is Jerome's statement in the Epist. to Sun. and Fret. 86: “Κυνόμυια is not, as the Latins have rendered it, called ‘_dog-fly_’ with the Greek letter ῡ; but according to the Hebrew sense it ought to be written with the δίφθογγον οῑ, so as to be κοινόμυιαν, that is, ‘_every kind of fly._’ [Rather, κοινόμυια, wherever it occurs, is a common scribal slip for κύνομυια. See Scharfenberg. in _Animadv._ on Exod. viii. 21.] Aquila rendered it παμμυῖαν [so it was printed from Erasmus down to Martianay], that is, ‘_fly of every kind_’.” For παμμυῖαν, or rather πάμμυιαν (which, it is credible, arose not from a reading of the codices but from the ingenuity of Erasmus, who, as was his habit, rashly fitted Greek words to Jerome's Latin), Martianay had πάμμικτον, _a mixture of every kind_, printed, which Hieron. understood of _flies_. (In the Bodl. MS., Canon. Lat. Pat. lxxxviii, we have found πάνμικτον.) On our passage the Syrian conveniently gives: ܐ. ܟܠ ܚܠܘܛܐ܀ ܗ. ܚܠܘܛܐ܀. Bugatus renders: A. _fly of every kind._ Fifth, _fly;_ but this meaning of the Syriac word seems to rest only on uncertain conjectures; and the same learned man, on Psal. civ. 31, where ܡܚܠܛܘܬ ܚܠܘܛܐ is attributed to Aquila, more correctly rendered _mixture of every kind_, i.e. πάμμικτον. There remains the original passage Exod. viii. 21, where for κυνόμυια (ܕܒܒ ܟܠܒܐ) our Syrian gives in the margin: ܐ. ܠܚܠܘܛܐ܀ ܣ. ܐܝܟ ܡܚܠܛܝܢ܀ ܬ. ܣܓܝܐܐ܀; where Ceriani has probably conjectured that ܟܠ ܚܠܘܛܐ once stood for Aquila.
