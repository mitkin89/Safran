Syro-hex. has in the text διάψαλμα (ܕܡܫܚܠܦܐ[?]); but in the margin: ܥܒ. ܣܠܐܝܣܐ ܕܨܗܝܘܢ. “Hebr. _Salaisa Sion._ The Hebrew here, unless I am mistaken, is some petty Hellenist Rabbi or other, who wished the Hebrew word _Selah_ to be understood of the _wailing_ (Greek σαλαῒς) _of Mount Sion_, from which the Lord heard the cry of the prophet David . . . The word ܣܠܐܝܣܐ, entirely unknown to the Lexicographers, we judge to be of Greek origin, namely derived from the verb σαλαΐζειν, _to lament, to bewail_, and σαλαῒς [rather, σαλαϊσμὸς], _wailing, weeping_: on which see Hesych., Suid. and Etym. M., and likewise Salmasius in the notes on Lampridius, _Historiae August. Scriptores_, p. 181.”—_Bugat._
