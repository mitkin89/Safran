[37] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܗܘ ܢܕܒܪ ܠܢ ܘܥܠ ܡܘܬܐ ܢܚܕܬ ◊. A strange rendering, or rather paraphrase, and one quite foreign to the style of this translator. A matter obscure enough in itself is made more perplexing by another rendering, which, as it seems, Buxtorf first brought forward in _Lex. Chald. et Talmud._ p. 250, and after him Lightfoot in _Hor. Hebr. et Talmud. ad Act. Apost._ etc. p. 280. It occurs in the Jerusalem Talmud, Moed katon fol. 83, 2: תרגם עקילס אתנסיאה עולם שאין בו מות, i.e. “Aquila rendered (namely the Hebrew word עַלְמוּת, Psal. xlviii. 15) ἀθανασία, that is, _a world in which there is no death_.” If we assume that Aquila gave αὐτὸς ὁδηγήσει ἡμᾶς εἰς ἀθανασίαν (or ἐπιφωνηματικῶς, ἀθανασία!), then we must hold that our Syrian either rendered by a periphrasis, and a rather awkward one, contrary to his custom, or had before his eyes a different rendering of Aquila, καὶ ἐπὶ θάνατον ἀνανεώσει (or ἀνακαινίσει). I will add one thing: in two other passages (Psal. ix. 1. xlv. 1) where the Hebrew has עַלְמוּת, Aquila rendered νεανιότης.
