The Greek is his own, as it seems, for the Latin of Jerome, who l. c. says: “Where we have, _as the sons of the shaken out_, there one reads CHEN BNE ANNAURIM, which Aquila rendered, _as the sons of puberty_, Sym. and Theod., _as the sons of youth_, the Sixth, ἠκόνεος νοῦ, which we can express as, _of sharpened sense._” Where it is manifest that the particle _sicut_ represents not ὥσπερ, but οὕτως. [14] The version of the Sixth has depended entirely up to this day on an uncertain reading of Jerome. Montef. cites: Ϛ΄. ἀκόνεως νοῦ (al. ἠκονηθέντος νοῦ); both barbarous. The latter reading he borrowed from Drusius, who copied the old editions of Jerome. Finally Martianay, from a Regius MS., as he says, printed ἀκόνεος νοῦ. The manuscripts, as it seems, fluctuate between ακονεος, εκονεως, εκονεος, εκονηος, from which the Verona editors, by a _new attempt_, to use their own words, _fished out_ ἠκόνεος νοῦ, from an adjective ἠκονὴς (!) of the same form as νεηκονὴς (=νεωστὶ ἠκονημένος). But no one is unaware that νεηκονὴς is formed not from νέος and ἠκονὴς, but from νέος and ἀκόνη.
