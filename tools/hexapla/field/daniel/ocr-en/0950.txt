[CAP. XIII.]
[1] Syro-hex., which marks: Σ. Θ. ÷ καὶ ἦν ἀνὴρ κ. τ. ἑ. These words are marked in Cod. 87 with lemnisci (÷), not obeli, with the sigla Â. Ĉ. Θ̂. added in the margin of the codex, written thus _distinctly_, on the testimony of Bugatus, who inspected the codex itself, not rolled together by strokes into the form of an obscure monogram, such as De Magistris attests are to be seen in the apograph he used. But what the names of either two or three translators placed here with the obelus signify, no one has proposed an opinion probable in every respect. To us, at least, the solution of Bugatus seems preferable, who, having regard to v. 7 of the LXX version, where the names of Susanna herself, and of her father and husband, are introduced as of persons then mentioned for the first time, judges that Origen's intention was to indicate that the first four verses and a half of the fifth were wanting in the LXX, and were supplied from the other translators, especially Symmachus and Theodotion. But that with this hypothesis neither obeli nor lemnisci, but only _asterisks_, can be consistent, although Bugatus gave no warning on this matter, is more than certain. As regards the agreement of two or three translators, the siglum Σ. Θ., or Ἀ. Σ. Θ., as we have observed throughout the Hexapla, only intimates that the version is of one of the three translators, who here is Theodotion; but that the others agree with him whose words are given only as to the _sense_. And if anyone should object that the LXX version of this narrative could in no way have begun with the words περὶ ὧν ἐλάλησαν (v. 5), we answer that it may be that a shorter preface once existed in this version, in which, the woman and her family being passed over in silence, only the judges were mentioned; and that this preface, when another was substituted in its place, entirely perished is credible.—This is what we had to say on a most difficult question, by which we may defend to the best of our ability an opinion not so much our own as that of our Milanese predecessor.
