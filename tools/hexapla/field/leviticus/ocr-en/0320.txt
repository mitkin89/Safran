Chald. et Talmud._ p. 600: "הִידוֹר, ὕδωρ, _water._ In Succa fol. 35, 1, on the passage Lev. xxiii. 40, they write: אל תקרי הדר אלא הידור, _do not read_ HADAR (_of beauty_), _but_ HYDOR (_of water_); namely the tree which grows beside all waters, that is, _the citron._ In the Jerusalem Talmud, Succa Cap. 3, it is written that Aquila here translated the word הדר as הידור, ὕδωρ." Lightfoot has something similar in the Append. to _Hor. Hebr. in Epist. I ad Corinth._ p. 280, who, however, seems to have some doubt about the interpretation of the word הידור by ὕδωρ. Indeed, who in his right mind would believe that for the very common word הָדָר Aquila here put, not, as a hundred times elsewhere, διαπρέπεια (see Hex. ad Psal. xxviii. 4. xliv. 4. Jesai. xxxv. 2. liii. 2) or something similar, but the foreign word ὕδωρ? Cf. Anger in his little book _De Onkelo_ etc. Partic. I, p. 15.
