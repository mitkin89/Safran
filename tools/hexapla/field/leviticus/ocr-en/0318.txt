[14] Cod. 130 (on πεφρυγμένα): Οἱ λοιποί· ἄναλα (sic). Cod. 85 in the margin (on χίδρα) without name: ἄναλα, for which Montef. conjectures the non-Greek αὔαλα, _dried_, so that it would refer to קָלִי. Without doubt ἁπαλὰ should be written, which Cod. 58 has in the margin (on χίδρα) without name. Cf. on Cap. ii. 14, where it clearly appears from the present passage that the reading of Aquila and Symmachus, ἁπαλὰ, is to be fitted to כַּרְמֶל, not to גֶּרֶשׂ. Further, Codd. X, Lips. on πεφρ. χίδρα cite: Οἱ λοιποί· ἁπαλὰ καὶ λεπιστὰ, which is, unless I am mistaken, a double version of the same word כַּרְמֶל. (In fact the Samaritan interpreter in both places put for כַּרְמֶל קְלִיף, _husked_, λεπιστόν.) Finally Hesychius is to be heard: "_And you shall not eat parched grain and pottage from the crop_ . . . or according to the LXX: _And you shall not eat roasted cedrinea_ . . . For the oblation of the present gifts, which we have shown to be the mystery of the Only-begotten, reconciled us to God, and provided us the food _of new parched grain and pottages_, or, as the LXX, _of cytra_ [MS. _citra_]. For the precepts of Christ are not only _new_, but also _cytra_: either as _soft_; for so other interpreters rendered _cytra_; . . . or as _rubbed by hand and cleaned; for this too is how they interpret cytra_." Where for the last interpretation, besides the Samaritan, the Syriac and the Chaldee can be appealed to, which render כַּרְמֶל by ܦܪܡܠܐ[?] and פֵּירוּכָן.
