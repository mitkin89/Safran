CAP. XIII.
[1] The former reading is in Comp., Ald., Codd. IV, X, Lips., 15, 18, 29, and others; the latter in Codd. 16, 30, 52 (without the first ἢ), and others, Cat. Niceph., Arm. 1, and in Hesychius, who cites from the text of the Seniores: _a scar of signification_ τηλαυγὴς _or_ αὔγασμα. The same, in his exposition, distinguishes between τηλαυγὴς and αὔγασμα as if two kinds of leprosy, in such a way that the latter is very obscure, while the former is seen from afar. In vain; for αὔγασμα belongs to another interpreter, as we shall see presently.
[2] So in the margin Codd. X, Lips., which fit the reading to οὐλὴ (שְׂאֵת, _a rising of the skin_). But that λεπὶς belongs to סַפַּחַת is attested by the common Syriac, in which the Hebrew word is rendered ܩܠܦܬܐ, _impetigo_, whose origin is ܩܠܦܐ, _scale_, λεπίς. Cf. on v. 7.
