"The word σπανίζουσα, which, so far as I know, is read only in these Capp. xiii and xiv, should be changed to σπαθίζουσα, from σπαθίζειν, _to scrape, to shave_ [rather, σπαθίζειν can mean nothing but _to stir with a spatula_, or _to smear on_]; then it agrees well enough with מַמְאֶרֶת and with פֹּחֶתֶת [Ο΄. ἐστήρικται (v. 55), at which place Nobil. cites: Schol. Ἄλλος· σπανίζουσαν, against our books], and almost betrays Aquila."—_Bahrdt._ σπανίζουσα is perfectly sound, and is without doubt to be attributed to Aquila, who for the Hebrew מְאֵרָה, _curse_, rendered σπάνιν at Deut. xxviii. 20. Mal. ii. 2.
[55] Nobil., and Anon. in Cat. Niceph. p. 1042: Τὸ Σαμαρειτικὸν, φιλόνεικος· ὀπίσω δὲ παλαιουμένην αὐτὴν εἶπεν, ἀντὶ τοῦ, συγγηρῶσαν τῷ σώματι, καὶ ἀνίατον. For מַמְאֶרֶת the Samaritan has ממראת in the text; but in the version, both here and at Cap. xiv. 44, ממריאה; which, having regard to the Chaldee אמרה, _to provoke, to exasperate_, we would rather render with our Greek φιλόνεικος than with Castell _permanent_. As for the other version, παλαιουμένη, we are still in the dark.
