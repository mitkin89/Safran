[8] Hieron.: “In most Latin codices, in place of what we have put here, there stands _toward evening, after midday_; because we cannot translate the Greek word τὸ δειλινὸν literally; for which in the Hebrew is written LARUE AIOM, which Aq. interpreted as ἐν [ἐν is wanting in three codices in Lagarde] τῷ ἀνέμῳ τῆς ἡμ., that is, _in the wind of the day;_ Sym., however, as διὰ πν. ἡμ., that is, _through the breeze of the day._ Theod., moreover, more plainly, ἐν τῷ πν. πρὸς κατάψυξιν τῆς ἡμ., to show that, the midday heat being past, [there came] the refreshment of a blowing breeze.”
