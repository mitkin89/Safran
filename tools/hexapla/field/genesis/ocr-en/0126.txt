[20] Hieron.: “For _pleasure_ the Hebrew has EDEN: so here the LXX themselves have interpreted EDEN as _pleasure_. Sym., however, who a little before had rendered _a flowering paradise_, here put ἐν τῷ π. τῆς ἄλσης, which likewise means _pleasantness_ and _delights_.” For ἄλσης, a word unknown to Greek writers, Vallarsius adopted from four MSS. (to which are added three of Lagarde's) ἀκτῆς, in the sense of _a pleasant retreat_, especially on the seashore or by rivers; for which, besides innumerable Greek examples (as he says), he appeals from Latin authors to Cic. in Verrem VII, 25: _He lay drunk on the beach with his women;_ and Virgil. Aen. V, 613: _But far off, apart on the lonely shore, the Trojan women wept for lost Anchises._ Drusius unhappily attempts ἀλοσύνης for ἄλσης, from a gloss of Hesychius: ’Αλοσύνα· ἡδονή; where ΑΛΟΣΥΝΑ is a false spelling for ΑΔΟΣΥΝΑ. I pass over the conjecture of Jo. Alberti on the passage of Hesychius, that ΑΛϹΗϹ is nothing but Α̅Δ̅Ϲ̅Η̅Ϲ̅ for ΑΔΟϹΥΝΗϹ, or, if you prefer, Η̅Δ̅Ϲ̅Η̅Ϲ̅ for ΗΔΟϹΥΝΗϹ; for abbreviations of this kind are not found in manuscript books. Finally Holmes: “In a doubtful matter, it should be considered whether τῆς ἀκμῆς has the appearance of truth. Origen in the Lexicon, in Hieron. Opp. T. III, p. 626: ’Εδέμ· τρυφὴ ἢ ἀκμή. Perhaps τρυφὴ is τῶν Ο΄, and ἀκμὴ that of Symmachus. Further, Gen. xviii. 12: ’Α. τρυφερία. Σ. ἀκμή.” For my part, having weighed the matter, I side with the opinion of Vallarsius, without however conceding with Scharfenberg in his _Animadv._ p. 2 that the word ἀκτὴ is used by good writers of _a pleasant retreat_. This use of the word belongs, of course, to popular speech, in which _places suited for parties and feasting_ were called ἀκταὶ. This we have learned from the Schol. Venet. on Il. B, 395: ῎Εστι δὲ ἀκτὴ ὁ παραθαλάσσιος καὶ πετρώδης τόπος . . . Οἶδε δὲ καὶ ἄλλο τι ἡ συνήθεια (_familiar usage_) καλούμενον ἀκτὴν, ἀπὸ τοῦ συμβαίνοντος, οἶμαι, τοῖς πλοϊζομένοις λαβοῦσα. ’Εκεῖνοί τε γὰρ εἰς τοὺς τοιούτους τῶν τόπων ἀποβάντες ἑστιῶνται, αὐτή τε τοὺς ἐπ’ εὐωχίαις ἀφωρισμένους τόπους ἀκτὰς καλεῖ, κἂν τύχωσι μὴ παραθαλάσσιοι ὄντες. To the same point belong the Glosses: Amoenia (sic), αἱ ἀκταί; as well as the expressions ἀκτάζειν, _to indulge oneself_ (Plutarchi Opp. T. II, p. 668: τί δ’ οἱ πολλοὶ βούλονται πρὸς θεῶν, ὅταν ἡδέως γενέσθαι παρακαλοῦντες ἀλλήλοις, λέγωσι· σήμερον ἀκτάσωμεν), and ἀκταίνειν (=ἀκολασταίνειν, on the authority of Suidas), on account of the opportunities for gluttony and debauchery which such retreats afforded. With this agrees the use of the Latin word _acta_ in a bad sense, not indeed Virgil's, but Cicero's, of which the best example is Orat. pro Cael. 15: _The accusers indeed keep flinging about lusts, amours, adulteries, Baiae, beach-parties, banquets, revels . . ._ Cf. on Cap. xiv. 3.
