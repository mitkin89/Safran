[9] So the Roman Ed., from which the manuscript books do not depart one whit. The Complutensian reading plainly savours of a corrector: ἀντὶ τοῦ αἵματος ἀνθρώπου τὸ αὐτοῦ ἐκχυθήσεται; and no less S. Chrysost. Opp. T. IV, p. 262 D: ἀντὶ τοῦ αἵματος αὐτοῦ τὸ αὐτοῦ ἐκχυθήσεται. Other readings, or rather recensions, of the Holy Fathers and the versions are reported by Ceriani on the passage. Keeping close to the Hebrew, as is its wont, the Graeco-Ven.: ἐν τῷ ἀνθρώπῳ τὸ αἷμα αὐτοῦ χεθήσεται.
[10] Syro-hex. ※ ܒܒܪܢܫܐ ↙ ܚܠܦ ܕܡܐ ܕܝܠܗ ܢܫܬܕ. From the punctuation at ܕܡܐ, αἵματος, comparing also the Arabic version, which was made from the Syro-hexaplar, Ceriani probably infers that after ܕܡܐ (so, with the smallest point) a second ܕܡܐ has dropped out, which was to be joined with the pronoun ܕܝܠܗ, so that the Greek would run: ἀντὶ τοῦ αἵματος ΑἿΜΑ αὐτοῦ ἐκχ. As regards the version of Hareth, Holmes. from Arab. 1, and without the asterisk Arab. 2, renders in Latin: ※ _for man his blood in place of blood_ (_shall be shed_); for which Pat. Junius in Walton's Polyglot Bible T. VI, from both Arabic copies, with an asterisk in one of them, gave in Greek: (ὁ ἐκχέων αἷμα ἀνθρώπου) καὶ ἐξ ἀνθρώπου αἷμα αὐτοῦ ἀντὶ τοῦ αἵματος ἐκχυθήσεται, which differs from Holmes's version only in the rendering of the Origenian supplement ※ ܒܒܪܢܫܐ ↙. For this, however, we have found in the Arabic text ※ فبالانسان, i.e. ※ ἐν ἀνθρώπῳ, the copula in the apodosis being regularly redundant.
