[7] Hieron.: “For _a coat of many colours_ Aq. rendered a coat ἀστραγάλειον, that is, _a coat reaching to the ankles_, Sym. _a coat with sleeves;_ either because it came down to the ankles and had been adorned by the artisan's hands with wonderful variety, or because it had sleeves: for the ancients mostly wore colobia.” For ἀστραγάλειον (which I doubt is Greek, for ἀστραγαλωτὸν, _reaching to the ankles_) Lagarde found _astragalon_ in three MSS., and from this restored to Jerome _tunicam_ ἀστράγαλον, which is intolerable. Without doubt _astragalon_ represents the Greek ἀστραγάλων (פַּסִּים. See Dan. v. 5, 24 in the Chaldee and Theod. Graeco-Ven. in our passage has: περιβόλαιον παλαμῶν); and anyone can see that this very word lies hidden under the corrupt reading of Cod. 127, Ἀ. στραγγάλων. The same codex gives Symmachus's Greek, but interpolated. Cf. Hex. ad 2 Reg. xiii. 18, 19.
