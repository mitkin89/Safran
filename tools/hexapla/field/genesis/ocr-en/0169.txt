[12] So Syro-hex., Arm. ed., and without asterisk Ald., Codd. 14, 15, 16, and others, Arab. 1, 2.
[13] Nobil. Syro-hex. ܐ. ܬܠܬܐ ܩܘܦܝ̈ܢܐ ܕܓܘܪܐ[?] (ΓΥΡΕѠC) ܀ (Montef. edited κοφίνους, noting: "So all the MSS. [Cat. Niceph. p. 254, and Cod. 25, according to Lagarde]. Drusius [from Nobil.] had read κόφινοι, but the construction requires the accusative;" i.e. the _Greek_ construction, not the Hebrew, to which the nominative alone is suited.) Moreover, γῦρις does not mean _loaves of fine flour_ (ἄρτοι γυρῖται), but the _flour_ itself, or _the finest meal_, as Hieron. in Quaest. in Gen. p. 366: "For _three baskets of chondrites_ the Hebrew has _three baskets of flour_;" and also in the Book De Nom. Hebr. (Opp. T. III, p. 8): "Chorri, or Chorraeus, _flour_, or _floured_."
[14] Nobil. Κανᾶ βαϊνὰ are _baskets made of palm branches_ (βάϊα; see the commentators on Joan. xii. 13). Sym., however, seems to have interpreted the obscure word חֹרִי from Aramaic usage, in which language _palm branches_ are called by a special word ܚܪ̈ܘܬܐ[?], as Ferrarius attests from the Syro-Arabic Lexicons, which give: ܚܪܘܬܐ[?]. السعفة من النخل.
CAP. XLI.
