Aq.: _And the Lord said to him: Therefore_ [cf. on Cap. xxx. 15] _every one who shall slay Cain, shall be avenged sevenfold._ Sym.: _And the Lord said to him: Not so, but every one who shall slay Cain, in weeks_ [so in the MSS., not _hebdomas_, as in the printed editions] or _as the seventh, shall be punished._ LXX and Theod.: _And the Lord said to him: Not so, but every one who shall slay Cain shall pay seven penalties._”
[23] Procop. in Gen. p. 229: Τινὲς δὲ ἰδικώτερον, ὅτι δεῖ τὸν Κάϊν ἑπταπλασίονα δοῦναι τὴν τιμωρίαν· καὶ Ἀκύλας γὰρ οὕτως ἐξέδωκεν· ἑπταπλασίως ἐκδικηθήσεται. [24] Procop. in the continuation: Σύμμαχος δέ· ἑβδόμως ἐκδίκησιν δώσει· ὅπερ τινὲς ἐξέλαβον, ἐν ἑπτὰ γενεαῖς δίκην δώσει. The Syro-hex. cites: ܣ. ܫܒܝܥܝܐܝܬ ܬܒܥܬܐ ܢܬܠ܀[?]; where it can hardly be doubted that the Syriac translator had ἑβδόμως before his eyes; although, from the version of Jerome (who, however, reported Symmachus's reading ambiguously) and from the explanation mentioned by Procopius, I would almost believe that Symmachus wrote not the unusual ἑβδόμως, but ἕβδομος or ἑβδομαῖος. Cf. on v. 24.
[25] Syro-hex. ܬ. ܒܝܕ ܫܒܘܥܐ ܢܬܒܥ.[?], with a scholium closely attached: “He whom the seventh generation begot (ܗܘ ܕܐܘܠܕܗ ܕܪܐ ܫܒܝܥܝܐ) ἐκδίκησιν δώσει;” which seems rather to belong to Symmachus's reading. Hieron.: “Theod. _through a week._” Cf. on v. 24.
[26] Codd. 127, 130, 135.
[27] Syro-hex. ܣ. ܡܪܘܕܐ܀ ܬ. ܕܡܬܢܝܕ[?]܀. At στένων καὶ τρέμων (v. 13) Cod. 130 writes in the margin: ἀνάστατον σάλον. σαλευόμενος. Cod. 127 at ᾤκησεν (v. 16): Σ. ἀνάστατος. σάλου ἢ σαλευόμενος. Finally, at Ναΐδ Cod. 135 in the margin: σάλος. “The Greek readings of the translators [Σ. ἀνάστατος. Θ. σαλευόμενος] I have taken from Holmes's Appendix, in which, however, they are presented rather confusedly as regards the text and the names of the translators; I have separated them on the authority of the Syriac. The Syriac version of Symmachus would indeed literally be _rebellious_, but I think it renders ἀνάστατος slavishly, taking its meaning from the force of the root, though not correctly.”—_Ceriani._ At נֹדְדִים Hos. ix. 17 the Syro-hex. cites: ܐ. ܬ. ܡܪܘܕܐ܀, which we have without hesitation put into Greek as ἀνάστατοι. The form ܐܡܪܕ, moreover, is regularly used for ἀνεστάτωσε; e. g. Dan. vii. 23 in the Syro-hex., and Act. Apost. xvii. 6. xxi. 38 in the Philoxenian. Cf. Hex. on Thren. i. 8. Hieron.: “What the LXX translated _Naid_ is called NOD in Hebrew, and is interpreted σαλευόμενος, that is, _unstable_ and _wavering_, and _of uncertain abode._ It is therefore not a _land Naid_, as the generality of our people think; but the sentence of God is fulfilled, that he wandered hither and thither, a vagabond and a fugitive.”
