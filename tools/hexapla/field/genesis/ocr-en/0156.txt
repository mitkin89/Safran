CAP. XXX.
[1] Hieron.: “Aq. says: συνανέστρεψέν με ὁ θεὸς συναναστροφὴν, for which in the Hebrew is written NEPHTHULE ELOIM NEPHTHALETHI. Hence from _turning_, or from _comparison_, since it means both … she gave her son the name Nephthalim.” Where for συναναστροφὴν, before Lagarde, καὶ συνανεστράφην used to be printed, against the manuscripts, including, as regards the copula, those of Vallarsi. The Hebrew, as it is read today, Graeco-Ven. rendered: ἀντιστροφαῖς θεοῦ ἀντέστραμμαι.
[2] So Comp., Codd. 14, 15, 16, and others. Theodoret.: Τὸ τοίνυν, εὐτύχηκα, οὐ τοῦ Ἰακὼβ ἐστι ῥῆμα, ἀλλὰ τῆς Λείας, γυναικὸς, ὡς ἔφην, ἐν δυσσεβείᾳ τραφείσης, καὶ κατὰ βραχὺ τὰ θεῖα παιδευομένης. Οὕτω καὶ ὁ ταύτης πατὴρ ἔφη τὸ, οἰωνισάμην (v. 27).
[3] Cod. X cites: Ἀ. ἦλθεν εὐζωΐα. Σ. ἦλθεν Γὰδ πειρατήριον. For εὐζωΐα we have corrected εὐζωνία from Cod. 130, which has in the margin without the names of the translators: ἦλθεν εὐζωνίᾳ. ἦλθεν γάρ (sic). On Aquila cf. Hex. ad Gen. xlix. 19. Hieron.: “Where we have put _in fortune_, and in Greek it is ἐν τύχῃ, which may be called _outcome_, the Hebrew has BAGAD, which Aq. interprets _girding has come;_ we, however, can say _in readiness for battle._ For BA can signify the preposition _in_, and also _has come._” Montef. edited, apparently of his own invention: Ἀ. ἦλθεν ἡ ζῶσις.
[4] So Codd. 56, 58, 106 (without ὅ ἐστι), 107 (likewise), also the Bodl. edited by Tischendorf (likewise). Hieron.: “_Aser, riches._ _Riches_, that is, πλοῦτος, has been wrongly added, since the etymology of the name Aser is disclosed by the authority of Scripture, which says: _Happy am I, and the women call me happy._”
