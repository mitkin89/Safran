CAP. XIII.
[1] Cod. X. Less probably Cod. 127: Ἀκ. εἰς τὸν νότον. Cf. on Cap. xii. 9.
[2] Hieron.: “How could he, coming out of Egypt, have been _very rich?_ This is solved by that Hebrew verity, in which it is written: _Abram was exceedingly heavy_, that is, βαρὺς σφόδρα; for he was weighed down by the burden of Egypt. And although they seem to be riches of cattle, gold, and silver, yet, if they are Egyptian, they are heavy for a holy man.” More correctly the translator of the Graeco-Ven. made the Hebrew expression clear by a Greek idiom, rendering: ὁ δ’ Ἀβράμης ἔβρισεν ἄγαν ἐν κτήσει κ.τ.ἑ.
[3] Cod. X. Hieron.: “Finally, not as we read in the LXX: _He went whence he had come into the desert as far as Bethel;_ but as it is written in the Hebrew: _He went on his journey through the south as far as Bethel._”
[4] So Comp., Ald., Codd. III, X (in the margin), 20, 31, others, and S. Chrysost. Opp. T. IV, p. 333 D. “It is doubtful whether this last reading belongs to some ancient translator. In both renderings, however, צֹאן, _sheep_ or _flock_, is omitted.”—_Montef._ On the contrary, צֹאן is rendered πρόβατα, as will be evident to anyone who examines the passage; and κτήνη is not from an ancient translator, but a faulty reading of the LXX version. Cf. Scharfenb. in _Animadv._ p. 11.
[5] So Comp., Codd. III, X, 14, 16, others, and S. Chrysost. l. c.
[6] So Codd. X, 37, 74, others.
[7] Cod. X.
[8] “This reading, of some ancient translator as it seems, Combefis's papers have in the margin.”—_Montef._ It is a mere variant, which Codd. 14, 16, 18, others exhibit in the text. Moreover Montef., at παρὰ τὴν δρῦν τὴν Μαμβρῆ, cites: Ἄλλ. ἀπὸ ὁράσεως, which is nothing but some sort of interpretation of the Hebrew word. So Anon. in Cat. Niceph. p. 194: Σόδομα· τύφλωσις ἢ στέρησις [στείρωσις Cod. 135]. Μαμβρῆ ἑρμηνεύεται, ἀπὸ ὁράσεως· κ. τ. ἑ.
[9] Arm. 1: _the breadth_ ※ _of it._ So without the asterisk Codd. I, 14, 15, 16, others.
[10] “These words are marked with an obelus, inasmuch as they do not occur in the Hebrew, as a certain MS. Catena notes.”—_Montef._ They are wanting in Codd. I, X, 14, 15, 16, others, Arab. 1, 2.
CAP. XIV.
[1] Codd. X, 127, 130. Procop. in Gen. pp. 321, 322: Σύμμαχος δὲ ἐξέδωκε τὸ μὲν Ἐλλασὰδ (sic, against the Greek books), Πόντου· τὸ δὲ Ἐλὰμ, Σκυθῶν· τὸ δὲ τῶν ἐθνῶν, Παμφυλίας. Less well Nobil. notes at Ἐλλασὰρ: Ἀ. Πόντου. Σ. Σκυθῶν. Cf. on v. 9.
[2] Codd. X, 127, 130, Procop.
[3] Procop. Cf. on v. 9. Not unreasonably Drusius conjectures that Symmachus had in mind the noun φυλή, _tribe, nation;_ so that _Pamphylia_ would be a region inhabited by various nations, according to the opinion of the Jews mentioned by Jarchi: “גּוֹיִם, a place so called because very many nations had gathered there, who set up a king over themselves, by name Tidal.”
[4] Nobil., Cod. 127 (with συνέβαλλον). Cod. X, on the testimony of Griesb.: Σ. συνέβαλον. Σ. συνῆλθον, of which the former is noted at the word συνεφώνησαν, the latter at the word ἁλυκήν. Cod. 130 in the margin: συνέβαλον συνῆλθον. The translator of the Graeco-Ven. coined the verb ξυνεμορίασαν, from συμμορία, _a decury of those discharging public duties_.
[5] Hieron. on v. 8: “It must be known, moreover, that for what follows shortly after: _And they set the battle in array against them in the valley of salt pits_, the Hebrew has _in the valley_ SEDDIM; which Aq. renders τῶν περιπεδίνων, Theod. τῶν ἀλσῶν [as if he had read השרים[?] for הָאֲשֵׁרִים (Gesen.) or חֲרָשִׁים (Scharfenb.)], meaning _pleasant groves_.” The barbarous word περιπεδίνων, _of the surrounding plains_, was suspect to Vallarsi, to whom, from the reading of two books, πρινηώνων,
