CAP. III.
[1] Cod. 86 cites: Σ. σχολάζει. Θ. ἑτοιμάζει. Somewhat differently the Syriac: ܐ. ܢܬܒܛܠ[?]. ܣ. ܢܦܢܐ[?]. ܬ. ܢܛܝܒ. Middeld. renders: Ἀ. σχολάσει (cf. the LXX on Psal. xlv. 11; Theod. on Job. vi. 28). Σ. κατασκευάσει. Θ. ἑτοιμάσει. But the Syr. ܢܦܢܐ[?] of the Aphel form is always interchanged with verbs which contain the notion of _removing_, as περιαιρεῖν, ἀφαιρεῖσθαι, παραφέρειν, ἀπαλλάττειν etc. Therefore in this place we think that not κατασκευάσει but ἀποσκευάσει should be claimed for Symmachus, which is sometimes used in the Hexapla for the Hebr. פִּנָּה, followed by דֶּרֶךְ (e. g. Ἀ. Θ. on Jesai. xl. 3; Σ. Θ. on Jesai. lxii. 14); and which accurately expresses the notion of that word (_to clear_ a house, a road etc. _by removing what is carelessly scattered about and in the way_).
[2] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܒܘܪܝܬ ܀ Hieron. on Jerem. ii. 22: “_If thou wash thee with nitre, and multiply to thyself the herb Borith._ For _the herb Borith_, which we rendered as it is set down in the Hebrew, the LXX rendered πόαν, to signify the fullers' herb, which, according to the usage of the province of Palestine, grows in green and moist places.” In our Syriac ποιὰ is in this place ܥܩܪܐ ܡܫܝܓܢܐ, _washing-root._
