Syro-hex. cites: ܐ. ܕܡܬܦܫܛ[?] ܒܩܪܒܐ ܀, i.e. _poured out_ (ἐκκεχυμένος) _in battle._ The reading of the Greek copies is very remarkable, chiefly on account of the example of the verb καλπάζειν, which I do not know to have been found anywhere except in the Lexicographers, although the compound παρακαλπάζειν still exists in a classical passage of Plutarch, Vit. Alexandri 6: Εὐθὺς προσδραμὼν τῷ ἵππῳ, καὶ παραλαβὼν τὴν ἡνίαν, ἐπέστρεψε πρὸς τὸν ἥλιον, μικρὰ δὲ οὕτω παρακαλπάσας καὶ καταψήσας, ὡς ἑώρα πληρούμενον θυμοῦ καὶ πνεύματος κ. τ. ἑ. From κάλπη, εἶδος δρόμου (in which, as Pausanias El. I relates, the rider used to be carried on a mare, but at the end of the course leapt down from the mare and, holding her bridle, as it were raced against her), καλπάζειν is derived, to which Budaeus indeed assigns a transitive sense, _to urge on a horse prancing at its pace,_ in French _galoper un cheval,_ a word taken over from the Greeks; others, more truly as it seems, give it the same meaning, but intransitive. Thus Suidas: καλπάζειν, ἁβρῶς βαδίζειν. Cyril. Lex. MS.: καλπάζει, ὀξυποδεῖ. Now ὀξυποδεῖν, on the authority of Salmasius, is _to go with short quick steps, and as it were to be nimble with the feet;_ which notion agrees well enough with Aquila's usage in this fragment. About the Syriac translator I determine nothing: yet the suspicion arises that, in rendering a word unknown to him, he had regard to the better-known word κάλπη, _urn, water-pot._
