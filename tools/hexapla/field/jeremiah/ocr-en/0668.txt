For προλέγοντι Cod. 88, and, “as it seems,” Cod. 86 claim for Symmachus the compound παραπρολέγοντι, not known from elsewhere, which I formerly suspected to be corrupt; now, however, I consider it entirely sound and genuine, so that παρὰ exerts the same force as in the well-known word παραπρεσβεύειν, namely of one who predicts the future _falsely and fraudulently_, or even _to the harm_ of the people. But besides this fragment of Symmachus our Syrian notes on the word מְשֻׁגָּע: Ἀ. _insane_ (ܫܢܝܐ). Σ. _ruined_ (ܡܚܒܠܢܐ). The Latin renderings are Norberg’s. For ܫܢܝܐ Spohn tries παραπληκτευόμενος, on account of the Hex. on 1 Reg. xxi. 14; but the Syr. ܫܢܐ[?] is for our translator ἐπίληπτος in the fragment of Aquila on Hos. ix. 7, where likewise the Hebrew has מְשֻׁגָּע. Concerning the word which bears the title of Symmachus, the question is more obscure, especially since the Syrian had assigned to this translator another rendering, προλέγοντι, in his longer fragment. One thing comes to my mind: that the Syrian translator, after completing the version of the whole clause, perceived that the force of the Greek word παραπρολέγοντι had not been explained clearly enough by him, and therefore wished to add ܡܚܒܠܢܐ (not _ruined_, but _harmful;_ cf. Hex. on Psal. lxx. 4) to the former rendering, so as in some way to do justice to the preposition παρὰ.
