[1] Codd. XII, 86, 88, of which the former has λεβκαβὴ, but the latter λευκαβὴ. Anon. in Cat. Ghisler. T. II, p. 891 writes λεκκαβὴ. "Symmachus retained the Hebrew words as a proper name; for he put Λεβκαμή. The Hebrews say that by the secret method of writing called אַתְבַּשׁ, by which ת is interchanged with א, and ש with ב, and vice versa, כַּשְׂדִּים is indicated by לֵב קָמַי. Hence the Chaldee translator put: וְעַל יָתְבֵי אַרְעָא כַּסְדָּאֵי; the Alexandrian Greek: καὶ ἐπὶ τοὺς κατοικοῦντας Χαλδαίους."—_Rosenmueller._ Syro-hex. in the text καρδίαν ἐπεγειρόντων, in the margin in the middle character cites Χαλδαίους: Finally Codd. 22, 36, and others: Χαλδαίαν καρδίαν ἐπεγειρόντων.
