[4] Syro-hex., as edited by Norberg: ܐ. ܠܡܢܘܦܠܐ[?] ◆ ܣ. ܠܚܕܐ ◆. From ܡܢܦܠܐ[?], "_sieve_" (κόσκινον, Hex. on Amos ix. 9), is derived a word omitted by the lexicographers, ܡܢܘܦܠܐ[?], "_sifting_", κοσκινισμὸς (from κοσκινίζω, Hex. ibid.). Now Symmachus must be considered. "Syro-hex. ܠܚܕܐ, _into one_. _I suspect an error here_, says the learned Norberg. The writing is altogether faulty. Read ܠܙܘܥܐ, i.e. εἰς σεισμόν. For Symmachus at Jesai. xxviii. 19 renders זְוָעָה σεισμός."—_Spohn._ Codd. 86, 88 give: Ἀ. Σ. εἰς σάλον, a reading which in Syriac is ܠܙܘܥܐ just as εἰς σεισμὸν is. Cf. Hex. on Psal. lix. 5. Jerem. xxiv. 9. But instead of the faulty ܠܚܕܐ, both in this place and at Thren. i. 8, where the Greek text has εἰς σάλον, the codex has ܠܚܡܐ[?], which in the passage of Lamentations Middeldorpf had reached by conjecture. Jerome renders the Hebrew: "_And I will give them up to a seething_" (or "_commotion_", and "_straits_"); whence Spohn rashly invented a hexaplar note: Ο΄. εἰς ἀνάγκας (_into straits_). Ἀ. εἰς βρασμόν (_into a seething_; cf. Hex. on Jesai. xxviii. 19). Σ. εἰς σεισμόν (_into commotion_).

In Symmachus, after ܕܡܫܢܐ ܐܢܐ, ܐܢܘܢ seems to need to be repeated from the text; on which supposition the translator seems to have derived הִנָּחֵם from הִנְחָה, "_he led down_".
