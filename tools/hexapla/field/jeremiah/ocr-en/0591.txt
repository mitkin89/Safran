[2] (Forts.) in the text under the asterisk Syro-hex. For ἐν τοῖς ὦσι Ἱερ., λέγων, Codd. 22, 36, and others, and Syro-hex. have ἐν τοῖς ὦσιν υἱῶν Ἱερ., καὶ εἰπέ. Hieron.: “_And the word of the Lord came to me, saying: Go and cry in the ears of Jerusalem, saying._ This is not found in the LXX; but it has been added under asterisks from the edition of Theodotion, who rendered the Hebrew word CARATH, for which we have said _cry_, or _proclaim_, as _read_.” The Complutensian editor (it will suffice to have pointed this out once here), lacking Greek copies, endowed Jerome's Latin with Greek as best he could, thus: καὶ ἐγένετο ῥῆμα κυρίου πρὸς μὲ, λέγον· πορεύου καὶ βόα εἰς τὰ ὦτα Ἱερ., λέγων.
