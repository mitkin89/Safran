[30] In this much-vexed passage the Greek Hexapla afford not even a spark of light; but the Syriac has handed down to us remarkable readings of Aquila and Symmachus: ܐ. ܕܠܡܐ ܡܫܬܒܩ ܡܢ ܟܐܦܐ ܚܣܝܢܐ ܕܣܦܩ[?] ܬܠܓܐ ܕܠܒܢܢ. ܐܘ ܡܬܦܣܩܝܢ ܡܝ̈ܐ ܢܘܟܪ̈ܝܐ ܩܪ̈ܝܪܐ ܕܪܕܝܢ ܠܬܚܬ ܀ ܣ. ܕܠܡܐ ܡܫܬܒܩ ܡܢ ܟܐܦܐ ܕܬܕ̈ܝܐ ܬܠܓܐ ܕܠܒܢܢ. ܐܘ ܡܬܬܪܝܡܝܢ ܐܩܢ̈ܐ ܕܡܝ̈ܐ ܢܘܟܪ̈ܝܐ ܩܪ̈ܝܪܐ ܀. On ܐܩܢ̈ܐ in the reading of Symmachus cf. Hex. on Psal. lxiv. 10. cxxv. 4. The Greek of Aquila I had formerly constituted thus in the _Otium Norvic._ p. 43: μὴ ἐγκ. ἀπὸ Λιβάνου ἰσχυρὸς ἱκανὸς χιών; where I fear ἰσχυρὸς, whether for מִצּוּר, "_from the rock_", or for מָצוֹר, "_fortress_", can hardly be defended. Certainly far easier is the ingenious conjecture of my most learned friend R. L. Bensly, namely that the word ܟܐܦܐ has been accidentally moved from its place, so that Aquila's first clause should be read in accordance with the Hebrew truth: ܕܠܡܐ ܡܫܬܒܩ ܡܢ ܚܣܝܢܐ ܕܣܦܩ[?] ܬܠܓܐ ܕܠܒܢܢ. Then ܚܣܝܢܐ will represent στερεὸς, which the same translator put for צוּר of God in the famous passages Deut. xxxii. 31. Psal. xvii. 3. xxvii. 1.
