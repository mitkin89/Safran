[37] Syro-hex. ܐ. ܣ. ܕܢܦܩ[?]. ܘܢܡܬܚܘܢ[?] ܠܣܩܠܝܣܛܪܘܢ ܀, with the Greek written beside it: ΤΟ ϹΚΑΛΙϹΤΡΟΝ. Norberg notes: “The Greek word σκάλιστρον placed here is of uncertain meaning to me, unless it be the same as σκαλιστήριον, _a hoe_.” (Rightly. So κοίμητρον for κοιμητήριον in Hex. on Jud. iv. 18; ῥάντιστρον for ῥαντιστήριον in the Graeco-Venetian version on Cant. Cant. vii. 2.) Spohn compares the Chaldee סַקְלֵיטִין, _palace_, coming over to the opinion of those who explain the Hebrew word by _royal tent_. Schleusner in the _Nov. Thes._ s. v. judges that ܠܣܩܦܣܛܪܘܢ, σκέπαστρον, should be restored, which is found in a fragment of Sym. on Job. xxiv. 15. In vain. For the reading σκάλιστρον with the meaning _hoe_ is made more than certain by a scholium passed over by Norberg: ܣܩܠܝܣܛܪܘܢ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ. ܡܐܢܐ ܕܐܟܪܘܬܐ ܕܐܝܬ ܠܗ ܬܪܬܝܢ ܫܢܝܢ: ܘܦܠܚܝܢ ܒܗ ܐܝܟ ܕܒܡܪܙܦܬܐ ܀; that is, “Σκάλιστρον is an instrument of agriculture, furnished with two teeth, with which they work as with a hammer.” According to this interpretation, which, however, we cannot support by examples drawn from the cognate languages, the prophet, in describing the subjugation and devastation of Egypt by the Babylonian king, passes over to a new image, _and he shall stretch out his hoe over them_ (the stones), after the manner of a husbandman, who breaks up the hardened clod with the hoe. Cf. on v. 12.

[45] Here also our Syrian goes off into something altogether different: ܐ. ܣ. ܘܢܥܠܠ ܡܩܒܠܐ[?] ܕܡܨܪܝܢ. ܐܝܟܢܐ ܕܡܬܗܦܟܝܢ ܡܩܒܠܐ[?] ܀; that is, as Norberg translates, A. S. _and he shall shake out the clods of Egypt, as clods are turned over_ . . . “But these words are hardly sound: for the word ܡܩܒܠܐ[?] troubles me, and I suspect a fault in it.”—_Spohn._ Syr. ܡܩܒܠܐ[?] stands for Gr. βῶλος Ezech. xvii. 7, 10; for βῶλαξ Job. vii. 5; and is not so far removed from the Hebrew אֶרֶץ. Hesych. βῶλαξ, βῶλος, γῆ. The verb ܥܠܠ means _to glean grapes, to glean ears_, Gr. καλαμᾶσθαι, Jud. xx. 45. Jesai. iii. 2. xxiv. 13; hence _to search out accurately_, ἐρευνᾶν or ἐξερευνᾶν, as Aq. on Amos vii. 14, where see. But in the sense of _investigating_ the verb σκαλεύειν is more familiar to Aquila, e. g. Psal. lxiii. 7: Ἀ. σκαλεύσει σκαλευόμενος. Σ. ἐξερευνῶν ἐξερευνήσει. Psal. lxxvi. 7: Ἀ. καὶ ἐσκάλευσε τὸ πνεῦμά μου. Σ. καὶ ἀνηρεύνων τὸ πν. μου. Θ. καὶ ἐξηρεύνησε τὸ πν. μου. I conjecture therefore that in the present passage Aquila wrote, καὶ ΣΚΑΛΕΥΣΕΙ τοὺς βώλους Αἰγύπτου; but Symmachus, καὶ ἐξερευνήσει τοὺς β. Αἰγύπτου; whose versions, not dissimilar in sense, the Syrian, as is his custom, has made to coalesce into one. If any weight at all is to be given to my conjecture, we have the reason clearly in view why in v. 10 it is said of the same Nebuchadnezzar, καὶ ἐκτενεῖ τὸ ΣΚΑΛΙΣΤΡΟΝ ἐπ᾽ αὐτούς. The other clause, _as clods are turned over_, savours of a scholiast rather than of a translator, especially Aquila.
