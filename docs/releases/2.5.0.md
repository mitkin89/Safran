# Safran 2.5.0

The big one: the **whole Tanakh word by word in German**, a new **word card** that shows how a Hebrew word is translated, and many new texts and dictionaries.

## Interlinear – Hebrew and German word by word
- **Interlinear button:** a new *Interlinear* button above the text puts the German word by word under the Hebrew words of the Westminster text. A small panel with checkboxes lets you choose the works for the chapter: several translations one below the other, with a legend. Clicking a word highlights the matching words in all versions.
- **Zunz (Berlin 1838), complete Tanakh:** all 39 books, from Genesis to II Chronicles, are aligned word by word, including Ketiv/Qere and the Aramaic parts.
- **Goldschmidt (Berlin 1921), complete Torah:** all five books of Moses (5,846 verses) word by word, with Ketiv/Qere grouped.
- **Further translations:** S. R. Hirsch (Psalms 1–150), Julius Hirsch (Isaiah 1–66); Breuer, Philippson and Herxheimer for Ruth.
- **Septuagint and Targumim in the interlinear:** the Greek of the Septuagint (CATSS) and the Aramaic of Onkelos, Pseudo-Jonathan and Neofiti 1 under the Hebrew words, linked word by word.
- **Biblical Qumran scrolls in the interlinear:** one line per scroll under the Hebrew words, with differences from the Masoretic text marked – spelling, different reading, editors' reconstruction and plus (e.g. „אור“ in Isa 53:11).
- **About 250 corrections in Zunz's text:** found during the alignment and checked against the Hebrew, for example misread letters (Rachzug → Nachzug, Terasim → Terafim, Bölkerreiche → Völkerreiche), duplicated endings and missing or wrong words (Am 7:17, Ex 24:1, Mi 5:13, Lev 20:14, Neh 11:18, Est 9:25).

## Word card
- **One card per Hebrew word:** a new *Word card* button in the word popup shows the lemma with its distribution in the Tanakh, the dictionaries, the most frequent forms, the Septuagint and Targum renderings and its occurrences in the Qumran scrolls. You can pin the card next to the text, change its size, go back and forth (Alt+←/→) and search inside it (Cmd/Ctrl+F).
- **„How is the word translated?“:** a ring chart and list of the renderings in each aligned translation, Septuagint and Targum. Translations are grouped by German base form; each rendering opens its verses with the Hebrew form and the German wording. You can filter by binyan, form, person, gender and number.
- **All forms (table):** the paradigm of each word, with rows ordered as in German grammar books (*1. c. Sg. – ich* …) and a short meaning per stem. Under each form stands its rendering in the chosen translation, counted only from the verses of that form. Waw-perfects get their own line; ties, weak majorities and fixed phrases such as כֹּה אָמַר are shown openly. A click opens all renderings with bars and verses.
- **Dictionary → word card:** Hebrew dictionary entries with a matching Tanakh word get a *Word card* button.

## Reading and navigation
- **Everything on this passage (Cmd+I):** a new panel collects what Safran knows about a verse, folio or whole chapter: commentaries, parallels, legends (Ginzberg), Talmud & Midrash, halakhah, words, places, topics, dictionaries citing the passage, notes.
- **Commentaries on the whole chapter** (Cmd+K), per commentator, verse by verse.
- **Verse previews name their source:** every preview shows above each text which version it comes from; in the word card it follows the chosen translation.
- **New tabs open next to the active tab** instead of at the end; background tabs (Cmd/Ctrl-click) line up in order.
- **Version menu per column:** clicking a version replaces the version in that column; Cmd/Ctrl-click or „+“ adds a column.
- **Verse numbering traditions:** references in other numberings (KJV, Vulgate, Septuagint) are converted automatically (table from TVTMS, STEPBible.org).
- **Simpler search form,** lemma search in Greek, and recognition of many more German citation forms (*5 Mos.*, *Jirm.*, Talmud folios like *bḲidd 13ᵃ*, Mekhilta, Graetz, Josephus).

## New texts
- **Graetz, *Geschichte der Juden*:** all eleven volumes with 11,400 footnotes, page marks and an outline.
- **Mechiltha (Winter/Wünsche 1909):** German translation of the *Mekhilta de-Rabbi Yishmael*, aligned to Sefaria's segments.
- **Josephus in German (Clementz):** *Antiquities*, *Jewish War* and *Against Apion*, parallel to Sefaria's English, plus about 1,700 links to the Bible and other texts.
- **Pinner, Berakhot (1842),** with his introduction to the Talmud and Maimonides' introduction to the Mishnah; **Mishnah from the Talmud** for all 37 tractates (Goldschmidt, Pinner, Sammter, Rawicz, Straschun).
- **Qumran:** all 735 non-biblical scrolls – the 40 largest as separate books, the rest per cave – with lemma and morphology for every analysable word (including words with pronominal suffixes), about 6,000 parallels and the Pesharim linked to the verses they comment on. The biblical scrolls in the Tanakh books are clickable too.
- **Septuagint:** clickable morphology for every Greek word, a Greek word card, and Rahlfs for the books without a CATSS text (Hosea, Esther, apocrypha).
- **Targum Onkelos (Sperber)** with morphology; Pseudo-Jonathan and Neofiti 1 updated.
- **Tosefta Kifshutah** now appears as a commentary on the Tosefta.

## Dictionaries and reference works
- **Krauss, *Talmudische Archäologie* (vols. I–III)** with all endnotes, figures and outline; the **register** as a separate dictionary; citations in Krauss's notation are links everywhere.
- **Hamburger, *Real-Encyclopädie*, supplement volumes I–VI** as a dictionary.
- **Portraits** of translators and authors next to versions, dictionaries and commentators.
